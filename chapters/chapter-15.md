Noah catches up quickly, after staring at the open door for a bit, wondering just who it was that set him free from the confinement imposed by the god of Death himself. 

In a way, he had an idea. He looked at the broad back of the woman who easily set him free and nudged him to join her on a casual walk in the underworld. Sensing his curiosity, the goddess turned her head and smiled, flashing sharp flesh tearing canines that could not be hidden by her “human” facade.

Noah tried to calm himself down, sensing that even if he resisted - it would have been futile. If she wanted to eat him, no one except Anubis would stop her, and even then he wondered if the god would. 

‘Maybe I should just ask her to put an end to this…’

He wondered if that would solve everything, if disappearing completely would at least make the god of death lose interest in those who did not deserve his gaze.

“What are you troubled by so much, my little love?”


Noah chuckled, gesturing around:

“By all of this.”

She led him to the yard he had spent days looking at from his room, sitting him at a small set of benches, picking up a chair bigger than that which fit Noah. 

“Your mind is not easy, and that makes your heart swirl against its natural flow.”

Her eyes looked straight through him, and suddenly Noah felt conscious of his breathing, of every single contraction of his muscles, of the heartbeat in his chest. 

“Madam,” he started but she cut him off with a gesture.

“Come now, you know who I am.”

Noah hesitated, but went for it: 

“Lady Ammit?”

She seemed pleased with the recognition.

“I heard that Anu- He does not like the name given to him by the mortals, so I was afraid to offend you.”

“He doesn’t like a lot of things, which only leaves more for me to enjoy,” she laughed, sincerely, but her eyes never once moved from Noah’s worried face. “Now, tell me, what is it that has been sparking so much commotion around?”

Under her gaze, he felt small, unsure of how much he could tell. The fact that she didn’t know about Leo meant Anubis had kept it a secret, so he wondered if he should pretend not to know. On the other hand - the god had claimed that his friend would be fed to the very deity in front of him, so what would telling her in advance mean anything?

Noah weighed his options and decided to go for the one that pleased him the most: 

“There is someone locked up here who does not deserve the fate that’s arranged for him.”

The one that would likely go against Anubis’ plans. 






“A homunculus?”

The scientist was almost jumping with excitement as they walked through an empty corridor: all guards dismissed for the sake of confidentiality. Even Nephele was nowhere to be seen, but Anubis had little time to wonder about her situation. 

“One had not been seen in centuries, Monsieur. To find one these days is truly a miracle,” the man stumbled over his words, excitement bubbling in his mouth. 

“Stitches.”

With apologies, the scientist fixed his face and caught up, holding onto his notebooks and bag with equipment tossed over his shoulder. 

“So, what would you like me to do? Document it? Prepare it for you? Dissection? Recreation? Mind era-”

“I need you to find out who and when made it.”

Sensing the god’s bad spirit, the scientist went deep into contemplation. There was no way to find out for sure what the task demanded of him, but that only meant one thing:

He would be the first to find a way. 

“Of course, Monsieur.”

It was the best day of his afterlife in centuries. 




“So, you believe his time to die has not come yet?”

Noah nodded, fingers holding onto the cup of coffee he had not dared drinking. Ammit saw his hesitation and dispersed his worries:

“Liquids don’t count for food, darling. My, have you not had a sip of water this whole time here? Someone will get an earful from me later…”

Noah took a sip and felt his stomach growl at the sensation of heat inside of him. He closed his eyes in satisfaction, feeling the placebo effect of pretending he just had a proper meal. 

“I am afraid Anubis does not make mistakes so easily,” she took a strawberry out of a bowl and bit into it: red juice on her lips making Noah’s mind run overtime. 

“Well, he did with me, didn’t he? So, who is he to be so confident to feed an innocent soul to you, madam?”

He figured bathing her in compliments would work, noticing how the corners of her mouth would lift slightly every time Noah would show even the slightest doubt in the abilities of the god of Death. 

“He will feed Leo to you and call it a day, a simple way to close the case and call himself omnipotent and all knowing!”

At that shameless insult, the woman laughed.

“You have some sense to you, love. Indeed, over the millennia I have got to eat plenty of unsavory souls, all by his command,” she tapped on the table with those long nails that could rip through a human’s flesh like butter. “He really is rather stubborn…”

“That’s what I’m saying!” 

The woman thought for a moment, her eyes narrowing into mischievous crescents:

“So, what would you like to propose?”

“Take a look at Leo, without… eating him.”

“I do not have the abilities you might think I do, I can not confirm or deny that he is fated to die now.”

Noah finished his coffee and stood up, walking up to the goddess who was taller than him even when sitting down. Dropping onto the ground, he knelt. Immediately, Ammit felt a bitter aftertaste of disappointment in her mouth - a familiar taste of pleading, begging and crying, a scene she had seen millions of times before. However, none of that came through.

“Madam, I can offer you something better than a soul of a human if you help me.”

Her eyes lit up, sensing he had a better idea than simple mortal possessions like money or land she had been offered many times before. 

“Alright, I am listening, little one.”





Anubis knew it would take a while, so he locked the cell room in a way no one would disturb the “human” and the undead, returning to his chambers, stopping to consider checking in on his “mistake”.

Thinking back to their argument, he felt himself grow angry again, shaking his head and only walking to his office faster. 

“There is someone there to see you, Sir,” one of the cats murmured with pleasure that was enough to make him aware that the day was only about to get worse. 

“And speaking of someone who might want to see you,” the other started but he only growled, dismissing the two. Their job was to know everything around the palace, so they were aware of the situation in detail. While Anubis trusted them to keep things like the information about their prisoner a secret, he was well aware that gossip about his argument with the mortal was undoubtedly already spreading around the hallways and dungeons. 

He opened the small door to his private office and took a deep breath seeing a face that shone bright with a smile that meant nothing good:

“How long were you going to keep my lovely dinner from me?”

Ammit rummaged through his papers, but Anubis ignored the act knowing nothing there was a secret to her. 

“May I see it?”

“You may not.”

He hesitated. 

“Not yet.”

Ammit laughed, content:

“Just like he said, you are in doubt over your own actions. How very unlike you.”

Her words mattered little to him, and still he was too late to stop the question that slipped:

“You saw him?”

“The little mortal? Why yes, he was quite accommodating, and quite appetising.”

Anubis sighed, getting a laugh out of her. 

“You are at a crossroad, and I can see how much this matters to you, sibling.”

She and Anubis were not related, but in the millennia of coexistence, the two revered each other as a shared blood. 

“May I offer some advice?”

The god did not answer, only sitting down at his table, feeling exhaustion washing over his divine form. He needed rest. Taking silence as agreement, she continued:

“If you force the truth to come out, it will distort itself out of spite.”

“Truth is not a living being to hold grudges.”

“But those who tell it are.”

She tapped at his desk, leaving a small dent in the wooden surface.

“Seek truth from where it lies naturally. Perhaps, it will open up willingly if you are only a little more considerate.”

He growled.

“I have been nothing but considerate with him! I have spent time and energy being nothing but considerate and how do I get paid for that?!”

She looked at his little tantrum like he was a child, waiting for him to calm down before adding:

“Considerate?”

“Very much so.”

“Is that why he had been starving for nearly a week of mortal time now, dear?”

Anubis opened his mouth and shut it immediately.

He had not realised that. It had been ages since he had to take care of a mortal, assuming it would be enough to leave that to his staff. The staff who had no memory of what being human was like. 

“There it is,” Ammit straightened up, fixing her braid. “Before you make decisions and feed me yet another uninspiring meal, try to at least feed him one.”



Noah paced around the room, noticing that during his absence the room was once again cleaned to perfection. Including the bowl of fruit that had a small note sticking from between the apples. 

He hesitated, feeling like his plan might be reckless if Ammit does come through with the request. A minute later he pulled the parchment out and looked:


[          :)     ♥️         ]


He struggled to decide if it was creepy or cute, so he just grabbed a pen and drew a couple symbols as well as he could before placing the note back. 




[          :)      ♥️    🚪❓🤝     ]



Despite the communication issues he hoped this would be enough. He arranged the fruit in a way that would catch attention and find himself too tired from earlier stress and exhaustion, relishing in the memory of drinking a hot coffee earlier. In a bit, he fell asleep against his will, realising his body was truly reaching its limit. 



Something shook him awake, and the first thing he saw was the one he wanted the least. 

“Get up.”

He ignored the god and pulled the duvet over himself before it was taken from him entirely. 

“This is an order.”

Noah looked at Anubis in disbelief, propping himself up and almost failing, his arms too weak to even hold his weight. 

Anubis watched the scene in silence before helping him get up, fully aware that his hands would have been pushed away had the human had more strength. 

“Why are you here? I never invited you.”

“This is my home.”

The man hissed at the audacity and finally rose from the bed, feeling a gaze on himself. Weirdly it was not judgemental but something else. 

“Get dressed, we are going out for dinner.”
