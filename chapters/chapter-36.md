The voice was rushed, less so over their casual walk speed and more over the growing enthusiasm. 

“I still can’t believe this,” Henry marvels, physically stopping himself from touching the face he had only seen in a couple paintings. The first time he tried, Leo squinted, clearly showing his distaste, but did not back away. 

Henry felt like the world was slowly morphing into a dream. 

They walked to the cemetery in a daze, with Noah following behind, feeling like his feet were carrying him against his will. He tried to stop, but the dream would not allow him to stay behind. 

“Here,” he could hear Henry’s voice. He could hear them entering. 

“Does this… spark any memories?”

Through the crack in the door Noah could see how Leo’s face eased into a smile that was believable but not how he would normally smile: a well crafted act. 

One prepared long in advance.

“Memories of my death? Not really?”

Noah tensed at the words of death. Was Leo trying to play a part of Henry’s research target? But why?

“Ah, fair. Nothing pleasant about that, really. A stab wound that bad was bound to be painful.”

Noah watched the way Henry’s hands wandered over the coffin as if their master was gathering strength to ask something.

“I was wondering, I mean… It really is you, isn’t it?” He stared at Leo in awe. “I knew it was not just the looks. You are Cecil, right?”

Leo patiently nodded, his eyes patiently following the way the researcher fidgeted with his sleeve. 

“I want to know how you did it.” 

“How did I do what?”

“Defy death.”

Noah felt himself stop breathing. As the two spoke of the forbidden in the silence of the cemetery. 

“Hard to say. The one who gave me this chance spoke not of Death but of another power.”

“Was it Life?”

Henry pressed himself closer to Leo, taking in his every word. Leo backed away but still kept his smile. 

“No, not Life. But if it’s life you are seeking, I could help you. There is nothing tricky about creating some.”

Noah watched the way the skin broke and the blood dripped onto the stone, unable to look away. 

“There are forces stronger than Death, forces older than Life.” 

Leo guided Henry’s bleeding wrist above the casket and squeezed the flesh until it burst with red. 

“Know where to look for guidance.”






A drop of something cold on his forehead finally pushed him to open his eyes, forcing his lungs to take in the cold humid air of the cell. Noah jolted upwards, sitting down on the stone floor, feeling his entire body ache.

‘How long was I out of it?’

He checks his head for injuries, assuming he had lost his consciousness - everything seemed to be in order. 

“Leo?”

Only when he looked up did he see the eyes that stared at him with open disgust. Just the side glance and the curve of his friend’s mouth was enough to snap Noah to his senses fully, forcing him to stand up. 

“Hey, is everything alri-”

“How dare you use this power on me…”

Noah shut his mouth. Never before had he heard Leo speak to him this way: poison dripped through every word he uttered with difficulty, as if they were pushed out with difficulty. 

“I am sorry, I didn’t mean to-”

“You spend too much time with them, now you think that I am below you so you can just force your way into my memories?” Leo punched the bars that separated them, forcing Noah to take a step back. “You use their tricks on me, the very tricks that force men to their knees in gratitude for nothing more than selfish indulgence of those beasts?!”

Noah gripped tight around the box in his hand, feeling like the insides of it were softly burning, reaching to his skin. It was a pleasant sensation, that still put the hair on the back of his neck standing up. 

“I didn’t know what would happen, I didn’t know…”

Leo only hissed, like the words themselves burnt him. 

“You are lying. I can tell. Ten years together and you think I can’t tell when you are lying?”

He really did lie at part, knowing just what kind of relic was in his arms. 

“Why do you say it like all I’ve been doing these years is lying to you?” Noah quietly replied. “In a way, out of the two of us, am I really the guilty one?” 

Leo’s eyes narrowed down. 

“There it is, the reason I could have never told you. The very way you are looking at me now.”

“What way?”

“Like I am a fake. A mistake.”

Noah felt like his stomach flipped upside down, like a whole different person was standing in front of him, bringing back memories of the time Leo tried to choke him. What side of him was real: the one that silently agreed to be saved, or the side that was so deeply scarred by the pain of his existence it did not believe in any remedy but vengeance. 

“I never thought of you this way. I always thought of you as a friend.” 

Leo walked towards the bars and outstretched his hand. 

“If so, give me your hand,” he said with a smile that did not reach his eyes. A moment later, he beaconed with his hand again. “Come now, what’s there to be scared of, Friend?”

There should be nothing to be afraid of, he knew, and still it took him a moment. Slowly, he placed the box on the ground, watching the way the man’s eyes followed it. Finally, he placed his hand in Leo’s and jolted when it was pulled, forcing him closer until his face hit the metal bars. 

Instinctively, Noah tried to pull away, already feeling the way his skin would bruise at both the hits on his face and the tight grip on his wrist. Before he could even say a word, he felt warmth under his fingertips, spreading into an odd sensation that was weirdly familiar. 

He pushed himself away, just enough to turn his head to see just what his hand was nested around: the silvery liquid that barely held its form slipped between his digits, melting and reshaping imitating… breathing. 

“Leo…”

He stared at it mesmerised, fighting the urge to squeeze the small piece in his hand, defenceless and at his mercy. 

“Since you wish to play with my life, here you go. Do with it as you wish.”

He didn’t want to, no, no no, that was not what he intended, but before he could open his mouth, Leo pushed him away hard, causing the smaller man to tumble on the floor, hitting his shoulder as he instinctively protected the small heart in his hands. 

“Leo, please, for God’s s-”

He shut his mouth before it was too late, sitting up, hoping he could still reason with Leo when he was faced with a stare that erased all those thoughts from his mind. A blank, deep stare right at him, looking at him and away at the same time. 

A chilling sensation of being perceived and not seen at once. 

“Leo-”

The door behind him was pushed open with a bang, forcing him to kick himself up onto his feet. 

“You!” Anubis growled before he grabbed onto Noah’s collar, shaking him with enough force to make his head spin. “Can you not sit calmly for just one day!” 

Before he could scold more, the hound’s eyes narrowed, nose twitching lightly as he took a sniff, staring at the side of Noah’s face where the man could only now feel that his skin was split open where he had hit the metal. Looking over him, Anubis bared his teeth at the man behind the bars. 

“Stop, he didn’t mean to!”

“Oh, like he didn’t mean to choke you back then?” There was rage that was seeping through every word, primordial power that came from the predatorial side of the deity. “You can’t be covering your eyes in blissful ignorance forever, Noah. Some wounds heal better when you no longer ignore them.”

The moment Noah tried to scratch at the hand that held him to be let go of, Anubis pushed him back and walked to the cell, only a low growl echoing in the room. 

“Ah. There it is. It finally happened.”

He had no idea what those words meant, rather he instinctively knew what they meant. Noah tried to push through to look, but the god held him in place. 

“Let me go!” 

“I will, in a moment,” gone was the anger, leaving behind only the passive impartial tone of someone familiar with how the scene was to play out. “You can see for yourself just what I mean.”

There was a sound of the lock clicking open and dropping on the floor. Noah froze on the spot, stopping his thrashing. He swallowed when he heard the squeak of the iron doors opening, feeling the grip on his body easing up, allowing him to move but not letting go completely: monitored. 

“Leo?” He had no idea why his voice came through so weakly, why he was so afraid of what he could not even see. There was a soft step, a movement behind Anubis who stepped away to show him what made Noah’s heart skip a beat. 

There was no face he could perceive, no name he could recall. There was a being he knew, and yet the knowledge itself felt so faint it threatened to slip away any moment. The eyes that stared blankly at him were no longer anything but a sensation, a gaze he could feel but not see. A gaze he could not return. Every part of his body screamed to turn away, move, run. 

Get away.

And still, he took a step forward, ignoring the hand that tried to keep him in place. He reached out, unable to call the name that was no longer a part of his memories, a name that no longer existed, a name that no longer had a face to put to. 

All he could say was: 

“Please…”

He could feel the hot tears on his cheeks reminding him that there was something he was desperate to reach out to, something that was so important he once wrote it down on the wrist that now bore nothing more than a smudge, a silhouette, a couple letters that would not quite align into a word. Was it a word? Was it a place? A name?

The stranger stared at him moving closer. It was no longer human, it was it, for it bore no humanity, no name and no memory. It was an empty phantom left behind of something that should have never lived as long as it did. A stain on the law of nature. A life that wasn’t meant to be.

“Please.”

It heard, it moved, it reached out with its hands and wrapped them lightly around Noah’s neck - one last memory that sparked in the man’s eyes before it was extinguished forever, leaving only a dreadful shock of having his windpipes being forcibly closed. 

The moment the survival instinct kicked in and he jolted, Anubis pulled him back, holding him as Noah took a couple deep breaths trying to calm himself down. 

“Do you see now?”

He didn’t see, and that was the problem.

“Do you remember?”

“I do.” He struggled, but he did. He remembered remembering. 

“So does he. He remembers the primal part of his existence,” Anubis pushed the shape back into the cell and watched the red marks on Noah’s throat darken just like the marks on his hands and cheek. “The last force that kept him moving.”

Noah wanted to argue, but using the moment while the god was busy examining his face, he slipped the heart that was still in his hand into his pocket, hoping it would be fine in there. 

It was the moment the heart left the body that Time had finally taken its claim over the artificial body, so he thought there was a chance that what lingered in the shadowy limbs was only an aftertaste, a phantom of the real deal. 

And the real deal, despite wanting to kill him, still had more to it. 

“I am fine, you can stop now,” he finally pushed the god’s hands away, feeling like a moment more and it would have got either suspicious or weird - neither was a good option. Anubis complied, watching the way his body shivered before turning to close the cell doors. 

“By my estimations we still had more time. So, what happened?”

He turned to look at Noah in a way that spoke “I know something did happen and it was fully on you”.

Noah nervously shifted on his feet, purposefully putting a facade of the good old naive mortal.

“I came to ask him about something, but when I touched him, something weird happened.”

“Elaborate.”

“I saw the past.”

The hound’s eyes threatened to burn through his skull.

“Elaborate.”

Noah mentioned the night at the mausoleum, the events he witnessed, finally picking up the box on the ground.

“I think it was this thing’s doing.” 

“Impossible…” 

Noah was about to argue when he realised the god spoke with less confidence and more… shock. 

“Well, that’s my guess so far, I mean-”

He jumped away on instinct when a hand reached out to the box, pulling it closer to his body. 

“Hey! That’s mine!”

“Noah, this is not a toy,” there was a sense of unease in the voice of Death as he tried to take the box from his hands again. 

“And I am not a child! I placed a bet and won it, I earned this thing myself, piss off!”

Anubis froze, eyes on the package. 

“You have no idea what you are playing with. This thing is meant to be useless, if it has some power in it - it’s not safe for you to handle.”

“And how come?” Noah pouted. For once he held in his hand something powerful, something that could, if he only knew how to use it, put him a bit above a dead weight and a plaything in the eyes of gods. 

“Noah, your path so far has been fogged in mystery and coincidences, one more concerning than the other. This is not something that should be working the way it did, neither should it be active at all. Please, hand it over.” 

Anubis asked patiently, but his voice shook like it was a more pressing matter than he would let on. 

“No. In fact,” Noah remembered: “You still have the other half, don’t you. You hand it over.”

He outstretched his hand, half afraid the jackal would consider biting it off. Instead, Anubis’ hand shook and he took a step back. 

“You should not be abusing this power, Noah.”

Just hearing Anubis speak in a way that was softly scared, almost… intimately terrified, made Noah pause.

“I won’t abuse it, I promise. I just want to… have something to protect myself with.”

Anubis’ ears twitched. He stared at the bleeding face of the mortal that had died too many times for one lifetime. 

“Please, Anubis… I know this is too much, but I can’t always rely on others.” 

He took a step forward, feeling like even if he gets hurt even more that day it would not even matter. 

Anubis pulled out an identical box from his pocket and before he would hand it over, he gently caressed the surface as if in contemplation: long nails lightly scratching at the ornaments. He took a long look at Noah, finally giving him the small package in defeat. 

“Thank you.”

Anubis shook his head. 

“Don’t thank me. Thank the owner of this scythe who permits you such frivolities.”

The moment Noah took the hold of the two, he smiled bright, pushing back the grievances of everything that had just happened. 

“Thank you Time for being here for me. And thank you Death for spoiling me rotten.”
