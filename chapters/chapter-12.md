The cold autumn air hit his lungs, making him shiver from the inside. Oddly enough, he felt refreshed, without a hint of tipsiness from before: as if born anew.

“They are not here,” Anubis caught up, leaving the bar and casting a side eye at the bowl or water left by the door. His slightly wrinkled forehead bent in a frown, an unnatural amount of emotion for the otherwise expressionless god. 

Noah could tell. He wasn’t sure why, but he knew that even if he run around he would not find his friend around. That same old feeling of unease he had been battling for days, for years, was back. 

“Where is Leo?”

He felt his fingers twitch, itching to scratch at something, anything, to ground him as an outlet for the anxiety swirling inside. Anubis looked at him with a raised brow. 

“Why would I tell you?”

Noah did not have time for this. He didn’t have time to bicker, didn’t have time to argue and spit poison back and forth: he could allow it any other time, when it was about himself, about his own unfortunate immortality. But now, this time, it was not his life at stake. 

“Please.”

Anubis, clearly taken aback by the half broken quiet plea, cleared his throat. 

“Nephele is still following them, as intended.”

Of course he didn’t have her phone number, neither did he even know if she had one to begin with, but as the god took a step forward, something in Noah’s mind eased a little and he followed stead, quietly, afraid to ask questions. 

They caught a cab and as Anubis paid, the two sat quietly in the car, for a minute, two, ten.

“They went far, didn’t they?”

Noah didn’t question if they were really going in the right direction, he didn’t even hear what destination the hound asked for. He simply knew that they were going to find them, it was a feeling of such certainly it was almost terrifying. 

Anubis said nothing, turning to look at Noah, as if evaluating something before finally letting out a sigh. 

“You will make this more complicated.”

It was a statement that would otherwise, in any other situation, get taken as offensive, but Noah, already realising how screwed things were if the very God of Death was seemingly uneasy with what was about to happen. 

“I can’t just sit this one out,” Noah tried to give a pathetic smile, his old habit to elevate troubles by joking around. A habit he had used so many times before with Leo in the many years of their friendship.

“That was never your choice, I am afraid.”

Anubis’ voice was low and quiet, one that would be used to deliver bad news, and Noah shut his mouth, feeling unexplainable sorrow in his heart, tears threatening to gather in the corners of his eyes despite him not even knowing why. 

He cut the conversation short, for once out of his own will. 





“So, it’s here?”

Leo looked at the small stone mausoleum: well kept, with fresh flowers and well trimmed grass and ornate bushes. With a modern lock, the structure had the only gates in the entire cemetery that were well oiled and opened with ease.

Leo chuckled, watching the way his companion shifted on his legs, unable to take his eyes off the man for even a moment, infatuated, completely at a loss for words. 

Like a worshiper met with their God. 

Leo grabbed Henry’s hand and pulled him into the yard, touching the key the researcher held in his sweaty hand, opening it finger by finger: 

“Can you show me what’s inside?”

Henry nodded, biting his lip as if he was not even sure if he could speak in the moment he had dreamt of for many years, ever since he smiled at a small insignificant remark in a letter addressed to someone long dead by someone he would grow to worship through the traces of their existence in all that times was not cruel enough to destroy. 

He put the key into the lock and with a practiced move pushed the doors open, looking to the side to see the awe on the face he had only seen on sculptures before. 

Leo walked in, hand brushing against the stone that in centuries had felt the warmth of Henry’s hands. 

Abandoned, but found and adored anew by someone now faced with the impossible. 

“Are you… Are you real?”


He knew it was a stupid question, as stupid as the situation itself, but watching Leo smile fondly as he looked down at the stone coffin, Henry did not care about the answer. 

This was surreal, and he wanted this moment to last forever, no matter if it was real or not.

“I have… I have read your letters, I have traced your clothes down to every museum I could,” Henry felt his already red face heat up even more. “I have held your favourite musket, thinking… what would it be like if one day you taught me how to shoot.”

He knew it was stupid, but the way Leo affectionately listened to him while tracing patterns on the stone coffin lid made him forget any and all common sense:

“I… I felt like if only we were not…”


“Born centuries apart?”

Finally, Leo looked at him, a fond smile not touching his eyes. 

“Say, have you ever opened this coffin?”





The idea of walking through a cemetery with the god of death was not pleasing, but Noah, who had always had a bit of an unreasonable fear of ghosts found it oddly comforting: what spirit would dare to attack him with such company. 

Instead of his phobias he focused on his worries. 

It was only a hundred times worse.

“Are you sure he is-”

Anubis paused, turning to look into the darkness as if hearing something Noah was not privy to. Alas, it was not a ghost. 

“You got here fast,” Nephele was sitting on a stone plate that once served as a tombstone, a small pack of crackers in her hands. 

“That’s a relief.”

Both the inhumans turned to look at Noah. 

“Will you take him with you?”

“Yes he will,” Noah chimed in. 

Anubis didn’t comment, only resuming his walk, leaving Nephele behind. She waved at Noah before going back to her snack as if nothing had happened. 

“I have a request that I know you will not honour,” Anubis started as they walked deeper into the unlit parts of the cemetery. 

“Then why take me along?”

“Like I said, this was never your choice, neither was it mine.” 

It was a cryptic way to answer, but somehow Noah felt like he understood: the situation was complicated for both of them, and if even Anubis had no say in the matter, who was Noah to do anything but follow the mysterious path laid out for him. 

Only a few meters later, Anubis’ arm stopped Noah, his eyes fixed on a small mausoleum where a dim light shone from the old metal doors. 

Noah felt like throwing up: this was not the scenario where he wanted to find his friend. He had no idea what was happening, but walking into a mausoleum with the god of death was not a good omen. 

“Is he inside?” Noah whispered and Anubis nodded. 

“My request for you, if you wish to make it as simple as possible for both yourself and your friend, considering you do not care about me - whatever I do, you do not interfere.”

It was a lot to ask for, considering he had literally killed Noah barely half an hour ago, leaving Emmy behind shaken. Still, Noah’s silence was taken for agreement and the god stepped forward: dress shoes ruffling autumn leaves in the dark. 

Noah followed, walking behind, watching the light creep onto them from inside the structure when Anubis paused, his shoulders shaking for a moment, the sight so unspeakable Noah immediately shook himself and bolted: he knew whatever was happening was too much, even for the god of death. He needed to know. He had to know. 

“Leo?”

He pushed Anubis to the side, trying to charge in, slipping through the arms that tried to stop him when Noah finally saw the scene indoors. 

A metal knife shone a little as Henry’s hand shook at Noah’s scream, blood spilling on the floor, over the edges of the stone coffin, as bright as the blush on Henry’s face. He seemed dazed, ecstatic, lost in the moment where the blood pouring out of his wrist into the coffin was only weakening his body to lean further back, resting against the stone walls. 

“Leo…”

The man that Noah had known for a decade, the man who once told him it’s alright to fail three exams in a row, the man who swore to be his friend until death does them part was now turning to look at him with a content smile: 

“Hi there, Noah.”



An arm grabbed around him from the back and as he was pulled away, the lights dimmed and Noah gasped for air, feeling the familiar sensation of his heart stopping. 

In the last moments of his yet another life, he scratched at the hands that held him, watching the way Leo’s smile dropped into a disappointed frown at the sight of him dying. 








He woke up in the ER, ignoring the coffee and formalities, storming immediately into the scales chamber, slamming the doors open. 

“Where is he?!”

He didn’t wait for a reply, running up the stairs further than he ever dared venturing, just to grab onto the hound’s long coat, pulling with force. 

“Why am I here?! What did you do to him?!”

Noah knew Anubis would not even grace him with an answer, so when the hound spoke, he shook: 

“He is detained. For your own safety, I had to evacuate you.” 

The words made no sense, so Noah paused, hand still gripping into the god’s clothes. 

“What do you mean detained? What do you mean for my safety?”

He had no answers, only more questions, and it annoyed him, so before Anubis could ignore him or give vague half-answers, he pushed him hard, as hard as a human could, emotions finally bubbling up: 

“Why are you even meddling with him! Is having me here for your amusement not enough that you have to get my friends involved?!”

Anubis’ dog head turned to face him, expressionless features calm:

“Amusement?”

The large hand pulled Noah’s away, with force. 

“Pick your words carefully, considering you are nothing but a nuisance.”

No, not anymore. He would not take this anymore. Slapping the inhuman hand off, he slammed his fist into the chest above his eye level, pathetic and weak, but he would no longer be quiet: 

“Nuisance?! Is that supposed to be an offence?” Noah laughed. “You make it sound like I am here on my own will. Admit it, you fucked up, you made me this way. You act all high and mighty, an ancient God that you are, but face the reality: you can’t even own up to your own mistakes once!”

Anubis remained quiet. 

“What? Am I wrong?!”

Noah felt his anger boiling, he knew this was wrong, but once he started, there was no stopping what was to follow:

“You say you don’t know what’s wrong with me, but this is your expertise. This is what you do! This is what you exist to do! How can a god of Death not know how to kill someone?!”

He let out a shaky laugh, watching the eyes of the hound staring at him, listening to every word he knew he would regret, but it no longer mattered: not when someone else was involved. 

“And now you go around pretending you can fix things, telling me I should follow, killing me if I am not deemed worthy to watch or something?! Who are you to decide that when it is my friend, when it is my life?!”

“Your friend?”

Just hearing the god’s voice made Noah want to punch him right there and then. 

“You call him your friend, but what if I can tell you you know nothing about him?”

“Shut the fuck up,” Noah cried out, feeling like tears were coming back up. Just in one line, gone was his bravado, gone was the anger: all that was left was fear. He knew there was so much beyond his understanding, but he just prayed he could keep others out of it. He hoped that if he screamed loud enough, cursed enough, he would make the god of death fix his eyes on him alone. 

He wanted to be broken enough for the rest not to matter. 

And yet, he failed even at that. 

“He is my friend. And he is alive. You can’t kill him.”

Anubis nodded, gently taking Noah’s arm off his coat. 

“I can’t. Because he isn’t human.”
