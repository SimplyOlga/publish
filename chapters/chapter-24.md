When they made it back Noah did not let Anubis get a hold of the creature. 

“You’ll kill it, won’t you?”

Anubis frowned. 

“It’s a reasonable accusation, I understand, but the situation is rather different.”

He spoke as he kept up with Noah who was rushing to the archives before the working hours would end. 

“How’s that different? So Leo has to die but Noah Jr. is ok because he is of your own blood?”

“That’s not the case, and please give it a proper name. The difference is in the sacrifice for its existence.”

Noah was trying to shake the massive number given to him before off, but it was pretty hard 

“When a being is created of godly blood it settles in the world as a responsibility of its creator. It will never leave my side and it will not come to the overworld.”

The last part made Noah a little sad. 

“Also, he will not be needing alternative sources of life force, considering his ancestry.”

“So are you saying that Leo was… was it all done at once?”

Anubis thought for a moment, probably wondering how to best say it. 

“I am not certain. Accumulating over 800 souls at once it hard, so it was likely done over the duration of his life.”

800 people had to die for his friend to live. Did Leo know, he wondered… 

“Even split by 10 years, that’s a lot of deaths.”

“Not deaths, disappearances. A lot more people vanish of the face of earth than you might think.”

“But you should be expecting them to die, no?”

Anubis opened a door for him, avoiding his eyes as if guilty. 

“I am not all knowing, I know what needs to be known when the time comes. But if they never come - the time doesn’t either.”

“So to you it’s like they never existed to begin with?”

The god nodded. 

“Family members leaving home and never coming back, not a sight of them found later. Abductions, trafficking, conspiracies. Bodies lost in natural and manmade disasters - you sneak a body here and a piece there and you have yourself enough.”

“Yeah, ok, that’s enough, I got it.”

It made him sick even thinking of how many different circumstances were weaved around Leo’s existence. 

“So, I won’t hurt this… thing.”

Anubis pointed at the creature in Noah’s hands and it jumped eagerly trying to grab onto the finger. 

“As long as it stays here - I can take care of it in a way that doesn’t disturb the natural balance.”

“And no one will need to die?”

“No one.”

Noah knew the god meant it, but just in case he offered his hand for a handshake.

“Promise?”

“Promise.”




The cats took a liking of the small thing immediately, probing for the story behind its creation, but only getting promises to share it later. For information. 

“I have a name, and you said I could check the entry.”

“Oh dear we have at least a couple thousand of them.”

Noah was about to groan in frustration when the cats set down and shifted their attention from the creature to their work. 

“Give us just about three hours and we will send you the info.”



As he walked around the palace aimlessly waiting for the time to pass, he went back to the ER and drafted a “thank you” note for the janitor and a grocery list for when he would inevitably go up the next day. 

Knocking on Nephele’s door he waited a bit before it opened. 

“Hi, would yo-“

“Noah, what the hell did you tell the Janitor about me?”

She stepped out of the room and squinted at him. Of course he knew he had done nothing wrong, but just the occupation itself made him feel guilty by default. 

“I didn’t say anything, what do you mean?!”

Nephele eyed him patiently. 

“He came to my room earlier asking me why I hurt you. Like I would waste my time doing that when you can’t even defend yourself.”

Noah ignored the last part, eyes shooting wide at the idea. 

What?!

“I swear I didn’t say any-“

That’s when he realised - the scratches. He touched the side of his face where the ointment was applied realising it was already healed while they were at the library, but maybe the Janitor saw traces of the injury earlier, making a wrong assumption. 

‘He probably assigned me those rooms as the safest bets and I still came back beaten up. Of course he had to pick through the suspects.’

Ammit would have not left a crumb of him, if she set her mind to it. 

Anubis was the Janitor’s direct employer, what could he think?

So that all left him with Nephele. 

“It was a misunderstanding… I will clear it up.”

“You better. He is the best guy around these parts, I don’t want to change the ceiling lamps by myself again, have you seen how high up they are?”

Noah nodded. 

“Why are you here though?”

“Came to visit.”

“You want something, spill it already.”

Of course she’d know, but before he could explain the creature in his pocket moved, slipping from the hiding spot and falling on the ground much to Noah’s shock. 

“What is that…”

The woman sat down to look at the thing with her cloudy eyes. 

“Oh my god Noah, what the hell am I looking at?”

“I made it.”

“Yes, and I can see you weren’t the only contributor.”

He was about to give an excuse when she shut him up with her hand.

“I don’t want to know, keep that to yourselves. You better have come here for something else.”

She listened patiently.

“Could you come to the overworld with me tomorrow? As a payback I will make you dinners for the entire duration of the trip.”

She hesitated but still asked:

“And where would we go?”

“No idea yet.”

“And what are we looking for?”

“No clue.”

Still, she agreed.




Back at his room, after a dinner consisting of yet again instant ramen Nephele had left for him, Noah put the small fluff on his bed, getting a good look at it. 

It really looked like a misshapen fluffy cub. 

‘Like if you describe a dog to someone who’s never seen one and then ask themto draw it.’

The creature rolled around happily. 

“You must be hungry. Leo said he needed food, so you must need it too, right?”

Noah decided he would bring various things from the overworld for it to try, but for now he settled on offering it a piece of dry jerky from the grocery bag. It seemed quite pleased, the food disappearing into the fluff at a speed unmatching the size of the creature. 

“No hurry, there is more.”

He’d always wanted a dog, finding them rather charming, but treating a creature made superficially with his own dna as a pet felt wrong. 

“Do you want to stay the night here?”

The fluff only run around the bed as Noah settled down. The moment he slipped under the blanket, however, the creature jumped down and rushed to the entrance door, settling down there. 

“You can sleep with me, there is plenty of space.”

It didn’t move, only turning to listen to sounds from the outside. 

“Oh. Are you trying to guard me?”

It was as adorable as it was charmingly pointless, so Noah picked one of his pillows and a sweater and put them down near the creature as a makeshift bed. 

“Let’s get you comfortable, my little knight.”

That night, he slept soundly, even with all the excitement for the day ahead.
