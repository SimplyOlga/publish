
For a solid moment no one spoke. 

Internally, Noah tried to stay calm. Sure, he spoke out of an intrusive thought, but he had his reasons. 

It seemed like the pantheon of gods knew that there was a homunculus residing in the halls of the palace. Whatever reason they could have for their interest, Noah would rather attract it onto himself rather than Leo. Since Anubis was greatly distressed with his friend’s origin, Noah assumed it would be better to keep the gods away from the man all together. Not to mention the fluff in his pocket - he wanted neither of them close to the child. 

“You ought to be kidding me,” Minerva did not stop a grin from crawling onto her face as she eyed the way Noah clung to Death's arm. “Looks like even a self absorbed hermit like you sometimes descends from the peaks of his moral grounds and seeks the company he proudly looks down on.”

Minerva eyed Noah with a newlyfound spark in her eyes. 

“Never thought you had a hand for such a craft.” She leaned in to look at Noah from more angles. “Fine work. I know someone who’d pay quite a lot for it.”

Being called ‘it’ didn’t feel right, but he stayed composed, not letting go of Anubis and feigning ignorance. He had to be convincing enough, and if he played the dumb role well, they would lose interest and he could tag along behind Anubis, making him do all the dirty work of asking the questions. 

“Not for sale.” 

Minerva ignored the hound, hands reaching to touch Noah’s hair. It took a decent effort not to pull away. 

“The market yearns for variety.” She nodded back towards her silent golden companion. “The same doesn’t do it anymore for the most. Quiet, cold, uninspiring. You on the other hand seem to have developed new, more warm blooded tastes…”

Sensing Noah’s growing discomfort, Anubis stepped forward, shielding him.

“The fee for touching is not the one you can afford, Minerva.”

“We can always trade.”

“You have nothing I could need.”


“I have information you might find useful.”

Noah did his best to pretend not to listen, but thinking about the goddess of Wisdom in front of him, he could not help but think she could be of use. Silently he prayed Anubis would play along. 

The God of Death eyed her, his cold voice unchanged:

“I don’t need your advice on anything. But since you are here unannounced, at least do me one favour of opening the doors.” 

The goddess paused, taken aback.

“First this thing,” she pointed at Noah. “Now - you are going to the parlour? What has become of you.”

There was unhidden mockery in her voice that made Noah’s blood boil. Gently he pulled at the sleeve of Anubis’ shirt, persistently until the God let out a sigh and acted his part well:

“What is it? You are distracting me, be quiet.”

A bit of back and forth and Anubis grabbed him by the collar and pulled him to the side, hissing an openly ingenuine apology towards his guests. 

The moment the doors behind them shut and they were alone, Noah finally relaxed and smiled:

“That was smooth, than-”

“What were you thinking?!”

Anubis had his head in his hands, utterly lost for words. 

“This isn’t the worst outcome, think about it - this way they will think I am nothing more than your science project.”

Anubis eyed him in defeat: behind his eyes Noah saw quiet acceptance. The god was seeing sense in his actions. Not only was it protecting the other two artificial creatures in the palace, but it would also conceal the real reason Noah was in the underworld. It was clearly a smart move and Noah basked in the silence of the god being unable to pick at him. 

Instead of picking at him, Anubis picked him by the collar again, shaking him lightly. 

“Do not speak with anyone, do not even look at anyone who tries to approach you, you hear me?”

“Yes! Yes, now put me down.” Noah fixed his shirt and mumbled: “I get the idea.”

“No you don’t.” At the tone of the god’s voice Noah paused. “To them humans are traitors who had left them to rot in the shadows - insolent and self centered, focused on their egos and selfish progress. They hate humanity, and treat humans accordingly.

“But don’t think a homunculus would get a better treatment. To the gods left alone to tend to themselves grow insane with boredom and lack of authority. If they can’t assert dominance over the humans, what do you think they do with dolls shaped to resemble defenceless traitors thrown in the den of starving angered lions?” 

Noah knew it was likely even more serious than the way Anubis described. 

“They will tear you apart for fun, in the best outcome.”

“And in the worst?”

Anubis took a deep breath, like the subject was something he never wanted to touch. 

“Eternity of slavery to those who see you as nothing but a tool for the release of their pent up desires.” 

Shutting his mouth, Noah looked away, swearing not to step away from Anubis even for a moment. 

“I will handle the talking. If you feel like you need to tell me something, do what you did just not, but only once. Seeing me tend to your whims will be suspicious to them.”




They returned to the hall and Anubis put on a frustrated expression as he ignored Noah clumsily running to keep up with his wide steps. 

“I’d apologise for the wait, but I find it a fair repayment for the earlier.”

Minerva scoffed.

“What happened to your own key?”

“I gave it to someone else.”

Uninterested, Minerva fetched something from the folds of her dress, and Noah bit his tongue before he could let out a sound. In her hand she held… a hand. A severed hand that held tightly onto a ring of metal keys that swayed and dangled as she rummaged through them.

“You taking it with you?” She pointed at Noah when she finally found the right one. 

“You have a problem with that?” 

Her eyes squinted with a grin. 

“Not at all.”

Pulling Noah a little closer to himself, Anubis walked towards the slowly forming door in the everlasting fog. Minerva inserted the key into the rusted lock and turned it once clockwise and once counterclockwise. One would assume the action to undo the previous, but the lock clicked open and she pushed the door open.

The smell of tobacco, alcohol and old fabric immediately filled the hall, heavy and intoxicating. 

“I will see you around, Death. Pass my regards to… Ah, nevermind, I forgot you don’t keep too many connections.”

She laughed and disappeared in the darkness beyond the door, her golden companion bowing and following behind silently. 

“Where does this lead?” Noah asked in a whisper, afraid they could still be overheard, but the god replied confidently: 

“The place of debauchery and delusion - the last resort for those who serve no purpose but refuse to disappear.”



As the door behind him shut closed, Noah squinted at the sudden noise that filled his ears: laughter and chatter, angry screams and ecstatic moans. Noah shut his ears to give himself a moment to adapt and Anubis let him take a breather. 

They could no longer talk freely, so as soon as he was good to go, he nudged the god just a little in a way no one would notice. Anubis resumed walking, ignoring the human trailing behind, like he was walking a dog. 

‘How the tables turned…’

They passed by tables occupied by people playing blackjack, their faces obscured by the shadows, features lost and unmemorable. 

Noah wondered if they were real people of a decoration, but yet again he could not ask. 

“I am looking for the owner,” Anubis tried to ask from a bartender who only silently watched him without a thought registered on his face. Frustrated, Anubis slammed on the counter, getting no reaction out of the employee. It was clearly inhuman. “I command you.”

His command was ignored completely. 

“Whatever.”

Clearly, Anubis disliked this place as much as it disliked him. 

“Keep up.” 

Sensing growing frustration in the god’s voice, Noah ran after him, not testing the waters. As they passed by tables of gamblers, Noah looked quickly into the game, feeling his legs swaying under a wave of nausea: the betting corner of the table was filled with gold, stained with blood. In the dim light he could see an outline of what he could assume was a head of a small creature chopped clean at the neck, bleeding down onto the coins. A sacrificial offering. 

Feeling like no one was watching them, he pulled at Anubis’ sleeve, but instead of leaning in to listen to him, the god lifted him up and sat him on his arm, allowing Noah to quietly whisper into his ear:

“Are they living people?”

“They are worshipers, scarce but existing, ready to sacrifice whatever they have for the attention of the starving deities.”

Noah saw a man jump from his seat, a silent scream unable to break from his mouth as if such emotions were not permitted at the parlour. As he reached for the gold taken from him, his face contoured as he dropped down, clenching onto his severed hand cut clean where it touched the coins. 

‘No cheating policies… But do they ever win?’

It felt like everywhere above them were eyes watching every game with satisfaction of the only benefitting side.

“Who owns this place?”


Anubis’ snout furrowed as he spat in disgust:

“It’s a shared ownership of the big three.”

Noah thought back to his school lessons, recalling the familiar title of the head echelon of the Greek and Roman faith.

“Jupiter, Neptune and Pluto?”

“Worse… Modern times do not require worship of the winds, thunder and the seas, so their gods are gone.” Noah noticed how Anubis ignored the mentioning of the Roman god of Death, but did not point it out. “What’s left is pleasure and ways to kill time that never ends.”

They walked into a corridor where lights were dimmed even more, causing Noah to shiver. It was as if the air around was tingling his senses, already deprived of enough stimulus when he noticed how muffled the noises of the casino had become. 

“There is the chase of reward, the gambling parlour run by Fortuna. The gods who have nothing left come to gamble their worshipers or offerings, ending up sinking into the pit of the false sense of achievement.” 

Noah hung to the side, holding himself with his hands around the god’s neck. A door next to them had a small line of light shining right through the small transparent section, and unable to look away, as if drawn to it by an odd natural temptation, Noah peeked at the figures barely covered by the silken sheets, skin glistening and hair dropping onto the pillows.

“You can guess the second owner.”

Anubis shook him a little, causing the man to snap out of his trans, feeling colour rising to his cheeks.

“The Goddess of Love?”

“The God of Blind Passion, Cupid.”

That one would surely never run out of worshipers, Noah thought, resisting the forced desire to look into the next door as Anubis hurried his step. 

“So, who do you think would be the best to ask? The third?”

Anubis shook his head, shushing him into silence before a figure emerged from one of the doors, completely ignoring the two. It was a goddess, judging by her height, but her eyes looked lifeless and her hair, tied into a braid, was framing her exhausted face that did not even turn towards them for a moment. She looked depleted of life, moving on instinct from a room of pleasure towards thick curtains at the end of the corridor. Silently, Anubis nodded towards her and Noah pulled at his sleeve to put him down before they went.



Her room was a small storage space mostly taken by shelves, leaving space only for a bed and a table surrounded by 9 massive jars of dark brown liquid of slightly different shades. Noah thought it was dark and uninviting, but the moment his eyes met that of the owner of the space he felt like it was oddly fitting: everything about the goddess spoke of desire to live secluded, her face betraying utter shock and fear at the sight of Death. 

“It isn’t my time! I never agreed to it!” She screamed, backing away with arms outstretched as if she could stop Death itself. “Did Cupid send you? I don’t want to go yet! I told him I don’t have to go yet!”

Noah turned to look at Anubis, sensing the tension in his eyes that never once betrayed his feelings. 

“I am not here for you.”

His voice, however, sounded hollow and tired. 

“Then why would you be here? Please, leave. Not today.”

Anubis ignored her, taking a chair and sitting down, pulling Noah to follow by his side. 

“I have a few questions that I want you to answer.” The woman clearly did not trust him, her figure shaking as she watched the god take his seat. Her teeth bit into her lips drawing blood as she tried to ground herself in the growing anxiety. “It is about neither you nor me. I seek medical advice on a matter from someone else.”

The goddess finally took a step closer, listening with unease. Noah saw how her body was fully wrapped in cloth, long gloves hiding every inch of her skin from the tips of her fingers to the neck. She was dressed like a white ghost, her silhouette slim and even somewhat sickly. He wanted to ask Anubis who she was, but his lips were sealed.

“Since when does the God of Death seek medical advice? Doesn’t that contradict your very work?”

“The reasoning and the rest concern you little. You have to remember how you are to keep this from the others, and what price you’ll pay if you disobey.”

Weirdly enough the direct threat had little effect on her. 

“I have little to gain from selling you out, and even less to lose if it comes to it. Out with it and leave me alone.”

Her fingers traced over one of the big jars, the liquid inside swaying lightly under the smallest disturbance. As Noah caught himself staring, Anubis pulled him closer:

“I wish to increase the lifespan of a homunculus.”

Noah finally perked up, listening to the conversation. However, his hopes were immediately shattered.

“Just make the necessary sacrifice, or is it forbidden to your kind? No one will bat an eye here.”

“Assuming such a way is impossible.”

She frowned.

“Then it dies. Get a new one.”

“An answer rather unfitting your status, Panacea.”

The name seemed familiar, but Noah pushed his wandering thoughts to the side, watching the way the goddess walked between the massive transparent amphoras in deep thought:

“I can’t perform a miracle. If it is set to die, you let it die.”

“Can I transfer it into a new body?”

She eyed him with suspicion but Anubis didn’t bat an eye, keeping his cold tone even when the gaze brushed over Noah. 

“Why such an elaborate waste of time?”

“I asked a question.”

She shrugged, curiosity leaving her eyes to be replaced with tired indifference.

“It is possible. But you will only face the same issue anew. That is considering it even sets - how would it be reborn if it has no soul?”

Noah felt like the question held a tint of mockery, so he gritted his teeth to suppress the violent urges to tell her a thing or two he knew better. 

“No one wastes time like that, you know it well.” She continued: “Once a homunculus is done serving its purpose, you dispose of it or reuse the materials. Essentially it will stay the same - an empty doll to follow its initial purpose.”


Noah knew what Anubis thought - Leo would stay loyal to his master no matter what, seeking to fulfil whatever condition was given to him as his original sin. Still, Noah held on to the small hope in those words. 

‘What if the reason the purpose remains is precisely because they do have a concept of a soul, just… different?’

“Do what you wish, but there is no walk around it. When an item breaks - there is a point it can no longer be mended.” She scuffed. “Even I can’t cure death, or you’d be out of business.”

The goddess of medicine looked over the massive jars behind her, from one to another, a soft loving gaze mixed with sorrow on her exhausted face.

“Neither of them could. And that’s how they got here.”

Anubis used the moment she was distracted to give a small pinch to Noah to get him out of his cycle of spiraling thoughts. 

“So there is no way?”

“You are a god, figure one out yourself.” She tapped on the glass. “If you can’t prolong the life, cancel the funeral.”

Noah liked the way she thought.

“Not an option.”

Noah also knew that answer was coming.

“Then if you can’t handle life, and you can’t avoid death, thread the line connecting them in hopes to bend it into a loop.”

Anubis growled, he did not like loops. 

“Work with the third option.”

She sat down at her desk and opened a drawer, pulling out a small hourglass and placing it in front of them. 

“Defy time.”
