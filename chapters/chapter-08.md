Three old letters, each dated a month after another, each in a neat handwriting, consistent, smooth, elegant. 

He picks them up one by one, eyes going over the lines he has long memorised, still marveling at every curve and every stroke of the pen.  

He compares the transcripts to the original, marking where he thinks the wording could be adjusted, before bringing the old manuscript a bit higher to his face. A glance around the room, a sneak over the corner to confirm he is alone in the room. Not a soul in the archive except for his. 

He brings the letters to his face and brushes his lips lightly over the dry old ink, taking in the smell of the old paper, imagining what it could have smelled back then. 

Perfume. Tea. Tobacco. Yes, it would smell of tobacco, especially after all the morning cigars he knew were smoked as the letters were written. 

If only he was a part of those mornings… 



Packed into his bag, the letters are set aside and Henry sorts his paperwork neatly before closing the room for the night. Tomorrow he would come back, for another transcription, another mindless work on the side, another needless bureaucratic procedure demanded by the university. For tonight, he was free. 

He patted the pocket where the papers were stored, humming a tune to himself, feeling like his heart was betraying his excitement. 

“Going home?”

He smiles politely to his colleague - a head professor of the archaeological department and passes by, rushing outdoors to cleanse his mind off work with some cold autumn air. 

Tonight he will pay another visit to his lover’s home. 

Tonight he will tell him about his day, slipping into a few details here and there, before opening a bottle of liquor. 

‘To your health!’

“Apologies, is this the history department?” 

Henry stares at a young woman lost on campus and nods, politely directing her where she is heading, slightly impatient to leave. A new hire, a fresh face at work. 

‘It’s alright. There is time.’

A small chatter, a little talk more so out of politeness than out of genuine interest. 

“Sorry, I am in a bit of a rush,” he puts on a smile and feels his face burn with heat. “It’s my partner’s birthday today, apologies.”

The woman laughs, pushing him to the exit, apologising as she tells him to ‘run, run, run’.



On his way, Henry buys a bottle of old whiskey, a favourite of his lover, and a small bouquet of his favourite flowers: two daisies, four lilies and two peonies. A drop of colour in his hands as he smiles at his own reflection in the bus window, the long drive allowing him to refresh all the topics he was going to bring up, thinking of how he would take this opportunity to say only the right words. 

He opens the gates and gently locks them behind himself, pulling a key out of his pocket, opening the door and slipping in. 

The privacy they were allowed to share could be seen as isolating, but to him it was intimate, it was comforting. 

He sets his work bag to the side and props the bouquet into a vase on a side table. 

“Work has been a lot lately, you know?”

He pulls the tie around his neck loose, feeling relieved to leave it all behind. 

“There is a new hire, she seems nice, I think I will try to have lunch with her tomorrow.”

“Don’t you think I forgot what day it is today,” he shuffles on his feet, awkward and uncertain how to get to it. “I was not sure what to get you, so I was hoping we could share this?”

A bottle of old nice whiskey that took a serious portion of his salary sits next to the flowers on the table. He pours a glass. 

“Thank you for being here for me, I wish…”

He chokes on his words, unable to voice the one thought that comes every time he visits.  

“Thank you. Thank you so much.”




“So, someone is going to die, or?”

The two, and a dog, sat in the campus yard, watching students pass by. The idea of any of them dropping dead in front of him was traumatising already, so he mentally prepared himself for what Nephele was about to say:

“That would have been too nice.”

Right. He forgot who he was dealing with. 

An older woman passed by them and raised her voice, telling Noah off for keeping ‘his dog’ off the leash. Reluctantly, looking at the doberman sitting solid five meters away from him, he connected the chain to the collar, showing the woman how securely the ‘beast’ was now held. 

He didn’t want to dwell too much on the situation. 

“So, what do you need me for?”

Nephele looked into the distance at the crowd before replying:

“If the theory holds, you might be able to bring me to the exact subject of interest.”

“Me? Why?”


“You seem to attract death. In the wrong way.”

Noah looked at the god sitting by their bench, grimacing. He wished he didn’t. 

“So is this what you do? You come to collect those who are…due?”

At the phrasing even the hound gave him a look.

“Only those who are difficult to handle. Those who mess with the natural order of life and death.”

“Like me?”

She eyed him through her pitch black shades. 

“No, you are a different case. I would never wish that kind of work to anyone.”

Rude. 

Noah stood up, tugging at the leash out of habit before releasing it and awkwardly patting on his pockets to check for his phone and keys. 

“Alright, so we just need to find that someone, right?”

“When I see them I will know.”

“Then, how about we just go on a walk?”


At the mention of a ‘walk’ the hound moved, pulling on the leash with more force than Noah expected, seemingly out of desire to get this over with. 

“Uh, Nephele…”

It was only when he said her name out loud did he realise it - not only did she have a face and a soul, she also had a name! Marvelous! 

“Nephele, why is he here?”

She looked at her employer rushing ahead of them.

“He needs to ensure you will behave under my watch.”

He could not see her eyes, but he felt like she was staring right into his, unwavering. 

“Yes, can you tell him I will. Also,” he lowered his voice and whispered. “Does he have a name? Like… a normal name? Like yours and mine?”

Her lips tightened into a straight line. 

“Like,” Noah tested the waters. “Maybe, Anubis?”

She hissed, slapping him on the shoulder. 

“Never call him that, he hates the name. Given to him by mortals, of mortal sound, without his approval. It is a claim, an attempt at ownership over his pow-”

“Ouch!”

Noah cried out feeling teeth on his leg, immediately letting go of the leash that Nephele caught. 

“I got it, I got it!”

He had never imagined ancient gods to be so fragile. Rubbing on his bitten ankle, he saw the same old woman from before look at him with a scolding expression before he leaned in and gave the dog a few pats on the head. 

“That didn’t even hurt! What a playful pup!”

He shouted loud enough for all to hear. 





“So, I was thinking, if you were here, would you… I mean, no, what am I asking…”

Henry takes the last sip, emptying the entire bottle before it drops to the floor with a loud clunk. He wants to cry, he feels heat on his face and a growing sense of despair in his chest. Still, today was not the day. 

“Happy birthday.”

He says it again, into the quiet of the space where he alone takes a deep breath before he shifts his wait to press his forehead into the cold stone. 

“I read your letters again today, those from September around your 23rd birthday. Lady Sharon would have loved to have that dance with you. She said so in her own diary.”

He always talked too much when he was drunk. 

“Did you dance with her? You never mention it in your letters.”

His fingers trail over the stone. 

“I would dance with you.”

They pause at the seams. 

“I would have danced with you every night. If only those letters were written to me, would you… would you have danced with me back then?”

The flowers in the vase, already lightly withered, were the only drop of colour in the crypt. 

In the ancient mausoleum, the stone of the coffin against his lips was cold and soothing.
