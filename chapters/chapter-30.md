“Defy time.”

Something said so lightly as if time was something that could be simply put on hold. If the unkempt appearance of the woman could mislead him to believe she was a mortal, it was that one simple line that reminded Noah he was in the presence of a goddess.

“You offer me a bargain with Saturn?”

Noah knew the name - the Roman god of time believed to rule over Latium during its prosperous time. Just the mention of someone of the sorts made him tremble with an uneasy feeling - that same anxiety that would spike driven by his inner survival instinct was crawling back out. 

But Noah was never good with listening to his survival instinct, so he lightly pulled at Anubis’ sleeve to encourage him to continue. However, Panacea shook her head, a hand on her temples soothing a growing headache. She opened a drawer of her desk and popped a couple colourless pills into her mouth without any water. Wincing at the bitter taste, she discouraged the two:

“Saturn has not been seen in centuries, a lot believe he had lost hope in modern history, others say he had not been since since around 1947 by the human calendar.”

Noah tried to recall what was important about the year, and noticing his struggle Anubis gave a hint:

“The doomsday clock?”

Panacea shrugged.

“I have not heard anything from him in about two centuries, who can say what tipped him over the edge. Some even dare whisper he could be… you know, dead?”

Anubis chuckled.

“I would have known.”

A soft smile creeped onto Panacea’s mouth. 

“That’s a relief. I miss the old man. Let me know if you meet him, he was the last one worth tolerating here, perhaps I will follow suit.”

“And leave them behind?”

The hound nodded towards the massive jars behind her, the glass of which the goddess gently caressed only a moment ago. At the mention of the amphoras, her face stiffened. 

“Don’t look at them, Death.”

Trying to avoid the growing mistrust in her voice, Noah stepped forward and reached to the edge of the desk too tall for him to look at the hourglass. Feigning ignorance, he poked at the hourglass to remind Anubis what they were here for. 

“Is this the one you are asking for?”

Panacea reached over the desk and caught Noah’s head in her big hand, fingers swiftly reaching to open his eyelids to examine the way his pupils constricted under the light of her lamp. Noah used all of his willpower to not pull away until she was satisfied. Surprisingly, Anubis did not interfere, so Noah relaxed a little, assuming she was not to be afraid of.

“A peculiar creation you have here.” 

Noah thought she had realised they lied, but she only looked at the God of Death with the same tired look:

“Saturn had been gone long enough that some daring ones took to looting his old estates. Some of his belongings are out for auction, today of all days.” She looked at Anubis with a grin. “Lucky you.” 

Noah could feel the way Anubis held back looking at him at those words. They both shared the same thought. 

The luck was starting to feel less like a coincidence and more like an interference, and it was hard to say if it was a blessing or a curse.

“What could there be of interest to us?”


At ‘us’ Panacea was taken aback, eyes moving between the two, with Noah trying desperately not to laugh. 

“I don’t know, you’ve known Time for longer than I have. Aren’t you two old friends going hand in hand?”

Anubis said nothing, and Noah sensed that the god had succumbed to old emotions he should not be prying into. 

“Noted. Let’s go.”

Without letting Noah answer or walk by himself, a hand grabbed onto his collar and dragged him out of the room, only allowing him to wave his hand at the goddess that took another pill from another jar and laid to rest her head on the desk. 

She really was tired. 

As the curtains to her pharmacy closed, she took the moment of silence as her only chance to rest. In a moment she’d be back to brewing concoctions that go against the principles of her and her family: liquids that intoxicate, powders that grant peace of mind at a price of addiction, oils and creams that stimulate the body and numb the mind. 

“Let’s get to work,” she whispers to the only family she has left, preserved, tucked away from the hands of Death that visits so casually. She doesn’t allow herself to miss them, for it was the only way. It is just that sometimes, some days that are harder than the others, she wishes they would respond to her. She wishes that under her touch was not cold glass but warm skin of her sisters, brothers and father, who once believed that offerings would not run dry and medicine would bring humanity to prosperity of body instead of collapse of mind. 



“Auction?”

In the corridor, Noah ignores the sounds from the private chambers where way too many people were being way too vocal.

“The gods have to entertain themselves to the degree where the disappearance of a friend is not a cause for grief, but a chance for a new acquisition.”

That made Noah grimace. The more time he spent at the parlour, the more words of Anubis about the character of the other gods made sense. He was simply hoping that the lucky coincidences would not run out by the time they made it to the auction.

“What would we be looking for? What are we buying?”


Anubis stopped in his tracks, causing Noah who was following behind to bump right into his lower back.

“What are y-”

He was picked by his collar and lifted up, shaken lightly and growled at. 

“We? What are we buying? Allow me to remind you, one last time -you are to speak to no one, not act on a single other whim of yours, to stay away from all and everything. We are not buying anything, I will do the bidding and I will make the decisions.”

“I get it, I get it.”

He really wanted to protest, to say at least something in his defence, feeling like a pathetic tag-along who only caused troubles, but thinking back to their situation and how much was at stake, he silently held back. They had to play this part inconspicuously enough so as to not get the others in trouble. He thought of his friend whose name was slowly fading from his memories, he thought of the fluffy creature that was in his pocket for moral support. He reached into the pocket to soothe it.

“As much as I’d pray to whatever god needed for it to be true, I fear that you don’t actually get it.”

Noah ignored the growling of the god when his hand touched the soft, fluffy… nothing.

He felt sweat on the back of his neck, eyes unfocused as he heard Anubis sigh, dropping him to the ground.

“Let’s go, and don’t cause any trouble.”

Noah felt his throat dry up before he could even take a step. One pocket, another. The creature was not there… 

‘Oh my… fucking god…’

He felt blood drain from his own face as he recalled the last time he touched or heard the creature, pretty sure it went through the doors to the parlour with them. 

Oh, he screwed up. Hard.

He struggled to keep up with the God of Death, looking around, pretending to take in the environment like the naive little homunculus he was. Internally he was praying, screaming and cursing all at once.

‘Anubis said homunculus are bound and tied to their creators, surely it will come back… surely it will find us.’

He tried to comfort himself, completely ignoring the shouts and cheers of the gambling den.

‘He has his father’s astute mind and his… other father’s nose. Surely he can sniff us out.’

He really wanted a smoke feeling like his heart was about to give up from stress. He had only had his creation for a bit over a week and had already lost it…

“No mortals.”

He snapped out of it when Anubis was spoken in a tone he’d never expect anyone to use at the hound.

“He is not mortal, he is my companion.”

Noah looked up at the bouncer standing between them and a golden arch leading into soft red lights.

“No mortals.”

Anubis sighed, hand reaching to Noah’s chest, giving him just a second to prepare for what was to follow. The sharp nails dug into his skin through the fabric of his jacket, hand sinking in to grab over his heart. Unable to spare him a moment to adjust, Anubis pulled the beating heart out, his other hand helping Noah keep his footing as the world around him blurred. No matter how many times it’d happened, it was still unpleasant and hard to get used to. Feeling cold air on his exposed heart, he focused on the lights around the bouncer who watched his organ beat slowly in the god’s hands.

“Is this proof enough that I know what can and can’t be done around these parts?”

The bouncer bowed.

“Apologies, you may proceed with your companion.”

Scoffing, Anubis pushed the heart back in and pulled Noah along with his feet barely coming to senses to get back to moving. If he could, he would have said a thing or two about the experience, but he stiffed the complaints for later. 

The grand auction hall was barely seated: rows upon rows of red velvet chairs left unoccupied as the gods sat far from each other, some smoking, others leaning into the touch of their companions. Noah really wanted to ask if they could have a secure booth as far away from them as possible, but Anubis found them two seats in the back rows, away from the rest where his figure could be less visible. Figuring his presence would not be noticed without a stir, Noah sat down quietly, watching the stage. He had never been to an auction, so he had no idea what to expect. 

“There can only be one bidding per person,” Anubis whispered. “That is to ensure the crowd stays entertained, and the same old items pass from hands to hands to prolong the joy of it.”

“I have no debit on me.”

“That’s why I will place the bet. You are not to say anything, not a word when the auction begins - the hosts will latch on any opportunity to sell, especially if they see you are with me.”

“Are you a regular?”


“The opposite.”

The lights dimmed and with the faint smell of extinguished candles came the smell of alcohol. Old wine, strong spirits, and something almost vulgar by itself mixed into the air making Noah feel dizzy. 

The auction began with the curtains drawn to the side, exposing a table with items, with no Host in sight. Only a slow voice that didn’t fit Noah’s expectation of a fast paced show:

“A fine evening, a fine display, and an even finer audience. Tonight we have a real spectacle ahead of us. Well then, let’s not waste any more time!”

Noah was about to sigh at the horribly delivered joke when a voice right next to him spoke out of nowhere:

“A spirit of booze speaks of something being wasted, how vulgar.”

Noah turns to look at the guest who sneaked to sit right by his side despite all the empty seats, swearing he had not heard them approach at all. The moment he was about to comment, a hand gripped tight at his left shoulder. Anubis towered over him, staring down at the man leisurely lying down taking two seats at the same time.

“Wouldn’t you know a thing or two about vulgarity. No talking during the show.”

Oddly enough, Noah felt like the hound held back on his wording, and that made him uneasy under what should have been a watchful gaze of the deity. Instead, the man’s eyes were hidden behind an ornate piece resembling a mask, fully the upper half of his face with no cuts for eyes. An intricate blindfold that shimmered in the dim light as the auction began. 

“Look who’s talking about rules with a human by his side.”

Anubis’ hand on Noah's shoulder shook, betraying a stir in his emotions. Whoever was sitting next to them saw right through their trick and chuckled. 

“What fine offers we have for you tonight dear guests!” The voice of the Host beamed with excitement that stirred the bored audience to attention. “First, we have Daphne’s lower roots, dried up and freshly rehydrated. Soft, ready for use.”

Noah stared at the piece of wood that stayed the bottom of the glass jar red with its blood like sap. He tried not to think about it, ignoring the murmurs in the audience.

“But that is only an appetiser, for we have something exquisite and as old as time itself for later!”

In the light of the oil lamps, a box opened to drop to the side and reveal its contents falling onto the desk and stage.

“The proud father time has left us his heritage, rusted and broken, but immortal nonetheless. The second item is the broken scythe of Saturn.”

“Hm, worth smelting it into a nice bracelet, I suppose.”

The man next to Noah did not seem impressed, but both the human and Death stared at the rusty metal dead set in their plan. They had to get it. They had to ensure Anubis’ bid would go through for the second item.

“And last, but not least, a truly unexpected addition, a breath of fresh air, a peculiar shape with a peculiar origin.”

The third item dropped the small drapery hiding it from the audience, and the gods gasped in unison, leaving only Noah and Anubis dead silent.

“Noah…”

Anubis’ voice held a murderous intent and the man swallowed, looking at their small artificial creature happily playing with a piece of fabric stuck to the side of a small locked cage.
