In the morning, when he woke up to the silver sun beaming into his face, he squinted, immediately reminded of the reality he was about to face yet again. 

This was not a dream. He was dead and likely in trouble with the very God of Death. 

Turning to his side, he allowed himself another five minutes. 



Knock Knock

There was certain politeness to the knock, and relieved under the daylight, he slipped out of the bed and peeked into the window, startled:

It was someone Noah had never seen before, but at least he could see them.

“I was informed you would not be opening the doors just to anyone, but I am here to pick you up upon the request of your caretakers.” 

Noah frowned at the term the woman behind the door used, but as he studied her face he finally noticed something: she had a face. A human face. 

She had a soul and a face, a beautiful combination in Noah’s opinion. 

Choosing to take the gamble, he clicked the lock open and pushed the massive door open, looking at the woman who stood just a meter away from him. 

She had a pair of shades obscuring her eyes and thick long reddish hair that felt almost too bright to his eyes but he thought it was due to the odd lighting in the room. 

“You are Noah, is that right?”

Her voice was stern, as if reporting for duty. 

“Yes, and who mi-”

“Come with me.” 

Unable to protest, he put his shoes on and left the room, closing the door in hopes no suitors would visit in the meantime. 

They walked back towards the scale room, so Noah felt somewhat relieved. 

“Are you here by an order of…his?”


She gave him a side eye he could not quite read through the glasses, but could guess the meaning of. 

“Yes.”

They did not exchange a single word until Noah walked through the now familiar doors, noticing immediately that the scales behind the throne were missing. 

“There you are, Noah!”

The cat, this time alone, waved at him before turning to giggle at the way the Hound refused to look at the cause of all of their problems. 

“Has something happened?” She inquired with the voice of someone who clearly knew everything already.

“Someone has authority issues,” the hound hissed. 

“And someone has poor manners” Noah whispered, breaking a laugh out of the cat. 

The woman that escorted him to the hall bowed to the god, almost making Noah concerned about his actual authority issues. Anyhow, it was late already. 

“Sir, I was informed you had a personal request for me. I am at your service.” 

The hound dismissed the bow and sat down, his eyes finally on the level with Noah’s.

“This one can not stay underground long, but he can not walk the realm of the living as a mortal,” the woman listened attentively, her frowned face betraying the discomfort at the idea of just what order she was about to receive. “I heard you recently lost your colleague, I assumed this could be a good opportunity to solve the issue.” 

Though her bow, she cast another nasty side eye at Noah who only waved back at her, knowing that no matter what personal opinion she held of him, as long as he was issuing the command, she would keep him safe and-

“I don’t particularly care what happens to him in the line of duty, he is fully at your disposal.” 

Much to his relief, the cat added:

“We will need him to come down every three days to confirm his situation, but other than that, Noah,” she turned and winked at him. “You can go back upstairs.” 

The woman straightened up, clearly deep in thought. 

“Does that mean I am not dead?” Noah held on to the straw of hope. 

“You are neither dead nor alive,” the god spoke. “You will join the company of those who suffer from the same predicament, it is your only way to go up, if you still want to.”

Curiosity took better of him: 

“And what other option do I have?”

He could have sworn the god smirked at him, or so was the feeling he got for the dog features remained motionless. 

The inhumanly long fingers waved, as if calling for someone from the shadows. On command, one of the creatures Noah had seen in the yard slipped out of the darkness, standing on its improportionately long legs, swaying lightly side to side, as if struggling to keep balance. 

“When a soul enters the underworld, it has two options,” the god of death raised two fingers. “If it is judged fair and light, it advances to the afterlife.”


“Your soul can not be judged,” the cat reminded. 

“So, as you can not enter, the second option opens up for you: that which presents itself to those judged unworthy.”

In the back of his head, Noah remembered the mythological stories he liked as a kid. 

“The soul is devoured, isn’t it?”

The god nodded. 

“So what is… this?”

Noah pointed at the creature that did not even seem to follow the sounds of voices around, guided purely by the command of its master. 

“That is what you will become if you linger unjudged for too long.”

So those must have been the creatures that wandered by his room at night. 

“They yearn to have a soul again, a new chance, a new possibility to be judged and leave the eternity of service,” the woman next to him watched the creature with a dent of remorse and disgust on her face, her lips thin as she bit hard. “To them you are what they lost long time ago - a chance to die.”

Looking at the shadow, he felt guilty knowing he should be thankful even for a chance to be consumed, for a chance to rest. And yet, he couldn’t.

“So, what do I do if I don’t want to become… this?”


“You follow what I say.”

There was clearly a hint of grudge from the last night in the way the god did not hesitate. 

“Fine, what do I do, Sir?”

“Nephele will instruct you, I am busy.”

What was the point of establishing the authority, then, he wondered. 

Nephele simply nodded, accepting the order. 

“You will serve as my assistant above the ground, helping me with my work.”

That did not narrow it down much, but Noah held on to the idea of going to the world of the living, immediately thinking of contacting Emmy about everything that happened. Or, some of it. 

“I am down,” he immediately outstretched his arm for a handshake Nephele hesitated to accept. With a question in her twitched lips, she turned to look at the god. 

He chuckled.

“Nothing quite like human blindness in the light of their ambitions mistaken for bravery.”

Nephele shook his hand and the deal was sealed: with little detail and even less room for flexibility. 





He woke up and poured a cup of coffee, admiring the scent of bitter roasted beans he freshly grounded himself. A spoonful of milk, no sugar, a drop of honey. That was his way, the same way he had his coffee every morning for years, and will do for years to come. 

Noah checked his notifications, answering a few messages from his friends asking about his trip, but he only said he will talk about it later. The morning was too good to spend it stuck in his phone, especially with the arrangement he made. 



“While it would require solid force, once a hit is secured, the shattering of the structure is highly impactful: both on the nerves and the lungs that could be damaged by the debris.”

He sat in the back of the auditorium and watched his friend take on a role she had dreamt about ever since they met at a party in high school. Emmy’s eyes lit up as she pulled out an old piece of bone that Noah thought he would never see again, relieved to see her make peace with an old loss that had left a scar on her heart. 

She was a in better place, living her best life, and Noah was honoured to be a part of it. 

As she placed her tray with a bowl of soup and slightly stale bread, he mixed the coffee in his cup and looked at her with a smile.

“I thought you’d be here later. It’s not like you to be punctual.”

He grimaced, unable to dispute the fact. He watched the woman soak the break in the broth before opening his mouth to ask something. 

Something important. 

Something he was struggling to remember. 

“All good?”

“Yeah, yeah. Just open-mouthed, staring at the bright new professor at the academy!”

She gave him a look of mischief, one they had shared many times before. 

“A reasonable reaction,” she turned to strike a pose before looking at him seriously. “What’s wrong, Noah?”

“You ever have this feeling you are forgetting something, but like…”

She looked at him expecting a continuation. 

“You ever feel like you forgot what you forgot?”

“Right…”

“No, Emmy, I did take my meds.” 

She thought for a moment. 

“Presque vu? A feeling of having something on the tip of your tongue?”

“More like down my throat… And I feel like it’s important.”

She looked at her watch, before turning to her phone. 

“This reminds me, I need to have a call real quick. Be nice, wait for me.”

“Good to know one of us remembers things.” 


He wanted to joke, wanted to keep the spirit lighthearted, but the more he thought about his feelings, the more the deeply nested anxiety in him grew. 

Like any young person in trouble and in need of professional advice, he picked up his phone and looked up ‘a sense of forgetting something’ on google. 

“Look around yourself, take a moment to take in your surroundings. Perhaps, there are objects and images that will inspire you to recall what it is you are missing.”

Noah took a deep breath, setting his phone aside before turning to look around the cafeteria.

A group of students, chatting and showing each other videos, reminding him of the old times in college when he was naive and stupid. That was not what he was looking for. A shelf filled with cutlery and glasses, bringing back parts of his long to-do list, featuring cleaning up the apartment after his long absence. Not that. Slowly, he took in the room: the wide pillars, the bright potted plants, the haste in the queue, the broad windows with a figure watching him, the tables pushed to th-

He turned back to the figure. 

The woman behind the glass stood among the bushes of the campus garden, staring right at him unmoving. He knew her. He recognised the bright red hair and round sunglasses. And more than that he recognised her companion. 

The doberman raised his head and growled.
