When there was nothing left in his stomach but acid, he spat on the floor and finally straightened up to look at his friend. 

Bound by leather belts, Leo was resting against the surface of the metal table, lifted a little at an angle to put the open body on perfect display. Staring at the gaping hole in the chest in front of him, Noah felt nauseous again, taking a step back to lean against a wall. The smell of blood, burnt flesh and his own vomit hit into his nose and made his eyes tear up. 

Anubis, who had been watching from aside, stayed silent, allowing for the moment to play out as it should by itself. In a way, there was nothing more he needed to say: the way the flesh parted into a mess of bones and meat that could not be called human was enough on its own.

And yet, the gore was not the most unsettling part of it all. 

“I am sorry, Noah, this isn’t the best I have ever looked for you.”

He talked. Leo kept on talking with a small smile on his face, disregarding the fact he was cut open with no organs inside. 

Anubis kept his distance, watching Noah’s reactions. The moment the man opened his mouth to speak, his body shook, weakened by malnutrition and the sudden shock. Still, he took a deep breath and managed to speak: 

“Leo,” his voice almost broke, but he went on. “How are you-”

The face of his friend dropped, eyes hiding away from Noah as if embarrassed by the situation. 

“I never wanted for you to see this.” 

“You… knew?”

Leo laughed, the motion of his non existent diaphragm making his muscles contract. 

“Eventually I had to come to terms with it, you know. I would never get sick, I never even as much as cough or sneeze, my body doesn’t even bruise.”

Noah listened to him talk about it as if it was natural as ever. Because to Leo it was.

“Remember the health checkups at uni? Yeah, they could not even take my blood because it would not flow the right way.”

Leo looked at Anubis, recalling his life:

“For years I wondered what was wrong with me, and yet could never find an answer better than… Better than just not being human.”

“Leo, you are-”

“No, it’s alright, really.” Once again that same comforting smile contoured his lips: the same smile with which Leo would spend hours comforting Noah after a bad test, a bad day, a bad breakup. To him now, this was nothing more than something they could overcome with just a bit of encouragement. 

He laid on the surgical table, opened up, pushing himself once again to be the pillar of strength Noah so needed. 

“I know what I am now, Noah. And that’s alright.”

Noah felt his fists clenching, his body shaken like in a fever. He remembered where all of this was leading.

“Yes. That is alright.”

He turned to stare at the god who oversaw everything with a detached demeanor that showed no hesitation or emotional involvement. 

Cold. Unaffected. 

“It is alright.”

Finally, Anubis allowed their eyes to meet, sensing the shift in Noah’s tone. 

“It is alright to be a little different, that means nothing.”

The god almost sighed, but held his breath. Listening. Allowing for him to speak his mind.

“Yes he isn’t human, but so has been his entire life,” Noah took a step closer to the deity, watching the dark eyes follow him. “I’ve known him for a decade, and how come it was never a problem, huh? And suddenly it is?”

The god looked between the two, weighting his words. 

“Must we discuss it here?”

“Oh, so you think it doesn’t concern him?”

“That’s not w-”

“Do you also talk about me behind my back?”

The hound paused.

“Well, yes?”

Noah groaned, punching a wall next to Anubis who didn’t even flinch. Turning around, he took a step towards his friend when a hand landed on his shoulder holding him back. 

“Let go.”

“Whatever you think of doing, I won’t allow it.”

Noah glared at him over the shoulder:

“I don’t remember asking for permission.”

With a startled yelp, Noah was picked from the ground and moved towards the door, despite the thrashing, kicking and scratching. 

“Get off me! Why must you ruin everything!” Noah struggled for air, spitting curses. “It all started when you showed up! Put me down!”

Anubis kicked him out of the room, and just before the door was about to close, he looked at the prisoner who watched them leave with a smile quite unbefitting a man on a death row. 



The moment he was put down, Noah tried to rush back into the room, determined to bite through the leather belts that held his friend if needed, but the hands of the god stopped him. 

“Let go!”

“We need to talk.”

“I am not listening until you take him out of that room, in one piece!”

The god paused, ignoring the nails digging into his short fur. 

“That might not be entirely possible. A few parts of him are already elsewhere.”

Noah felt his skin turn cold, sweat dripping into his collar. 

“You really are devoid of any compassion.”

The hound looked away, without a comment. 

“You do realise I will keep thrashing until you let him go, right?” Seeing the annoyed look on Anubis’ face, he added: “What are you thinking? Killing me again? Well, I will just come back here, no? I will keep crawling to do everything against your demands again and again, and you have no way to get rid of me, you hear?” 

He punched hard into the god’s chest.

“Never forget, I am the mistake you made. A mistake you will have to deal with fo-re-ver.”

Noah had no idea just what he was trying to imply, but watching the way the hound's ears twitched he knew he hit a nerve. 

The exhaustion of all the kicking finally settled in, and he allowed himself a deep breath before mastering the courage to ask: 

“Why must he die?”

Sensing the change in his tone, Anubis carefully thought of his answer, deciding against more petty quarrels.

“Artificial creation of life is not only forbidden due to moral complications, or the high costs of the process, but also due to underlying questions that rise up when one attempts to go through with it.”

“Like what?”

Anubis finally let go of the man, feeling like they could finally talk.

“The reasoning for it. Why go through one of the most elaborate procedures, forbidden, frowned upon, highly taxing, unless there is a goal.”

Noah frowned, hating to admit the concern made sense. Still, to him this was not about an abstract case, but about someone he knew:

“Why not? How many people are there who do something unreasonable just because it's hard or borderline impossible to?”

“And most of them hold personal motives,” Anubis explained patiently. “Fame, vengeance, religious reasons.”

“Why can’t it be the same in his case?”

Somehow Noah was starting to doubt his own defences, and the thought made him shiver in anger at himself. 


“You said it yourself: your friend had never shown any signs of abnormality, living a perfectly fine life.”

Noah thought back to their university days, the sleepless all-nighters, the parties, the exams and the graduation. He thought back to their work days, meetings once a week, quick brunches on weekends. It was all… so normal. 

He loved normal. 

He had always craved normal. 

He had finally found normal, and then-

“Would someone create life, paying a price for it, only to set it out into the world claiming nothing in return.”

“Some people… are just kind enou-”

“No, Noah. Not in such cases.” 

Noah bit at his tongue, swallowing the curses. What was he supposed to say? 

“Will you really judge him just for the reasons someone else might have had?”

Anubis nodded.

“Will you kill him just because of the unknown that bothers you?”

“I will. And you have to understand, that-”

But Noah did not listen. 

Anubis watched the figure walk away, realising that even millennia into his existence he was no closer to understanding the odd ways humans work.



Noah shut the door behind himself and kicked at his bed. And then a drawer, nightstand and a wall, feeling like pushing the last remaining energy out of himself just to deafen the shouting in his head. 

He was burning with anger, but mixed into it were rapidly settling fear and sorrow.

He could not let this end like this. Not because someone else decided this was proper. Not because someone unrelated to Leo and him made rules that had to be obeyed. Noah never agreed to play by the rules, neither did Leo. 

So, he had to act. 

A light tap on the door made him jump, hiding the object he had been staring at under his duvet. 

“Are you here, dear?”

He was relieved it was Ammit. If Anubis even dared coming to see him now, Noah would not hesitate to attempt the unspeakable: 

Killing Death with his own hands.

“Yes, madam.”

The woman walked in, closing the door behind herself, eyes on Noah’s disheveled state. Only then, uncomfortable under her inspecting gaze, did he realise he must have looked rather out of it after all the one sided fighting he put up a bit earlier. 

“You look quite determined.” 

Noah shifted on his feet before reaching under the duvet to pull something that made Ammit laugh. She walked up, slowly, as if afraid to scare the small human who had just gone through a lot. He fingers wrapped around his hand, eyes inspecting. 

“Are you sure tonight is the night?”

Noah nodded, feeling the anger rise in him again: the gods were all the same, mocking his decisions and resolve. 

“Have you ever done this before?”

“Of course not.”

“Oh.”

She laughed, immediately apologising once Noah glared at her. 

“Something this simple might hurt, especially if you don’t know just where and how to use it.”

Her hand slipped into the fabric of her lower dress, pulling out something from a pocket. 

“Here, a gift.”

Noah accepted the cold metal piece.

“Can you do it for me?”

He knew she would say no, but it was worth a check. 

“If I do it, things will get a lot more complicated, dear. And you know, he would not like me to lay my hands on you.”

She winked, waving and bidding him farewell. Right at the door, she turned to give her last advice:

“Aim for the artery. I will wait for the news later.”

As the door closed, Noah sat down on the bed, feeling like his already fragile life was drained out of him. The vision of Leo on that table was still vivid, so he tightened his grip and took a deep breath. 

The two knives in his hands were cold to the touch. One, simple and barely sharp, from the dinner at the restaurant just earlier, that he slipped into his pocket. The other - an ornate decorated blade from the demonness herself. 

He slowly brought them both up to his throat and closed his eyes. 

He could do it. 

A direct hit on the artery.
