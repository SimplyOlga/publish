This time Noah did not bother the god, picking his clothes himself. Surely as long as he kept it black the hound would not mind anything, so he pulled everything black from his closet and settled for a nice pair of trousers he kept for special events, a nice white shirt and a black sweater on top. It was casual enough for Emmy, and decent enough for the God of Death. They still had plenty of time until the evening, and watching the man on his couch deeply caught in his reading, Noah couldn’t help but feel awkward and defeated. 

Their relationship clearly was now more strained than ever before, with the god, for the first time in the time they’d known each other, actually choosing to do his work instead of micromanaging Noah’s every step.

‘I am supposed to feel better about this, but somehow this asshole managed to make even that feel depressing.’

He knew it was his fault, but sitting down on the armchair and picking up a book so as not to bother the hound with the sounds of TV, he struggled to focus on a single word. Confined to the small apartment, unable to leave alone, he was forced to bear the awkward silence and the weight of their situation. 

Still, he used it to his small advantage. For whatever reason, Anubis did not take down his disguise for later, keeping the human appearance that was a lot less familiar to Noah, and a lot more honest than the emotionless canine form. Noah saw the way the god’s eyes squinted at smaller print on his documents, the way his brows wound frown as he made corrections and wrote some small notes down. Sometimes, his mouth would even open to mumble a few words in a language Noah could not understand, and the idea of so much personality unwillingly opening on display for him made him hold back a small laugh. 

“Something amusing?”

Even the way his brow rose at the question was so human it made Noah chuckle. 

“Not at all. Just interesting to see you at work.”

The god’s lips pursed into a thin line. 


“If your curiosity is demanding, I could always have you watch the judgements.”


Noah put his hands up in a surrender, recalling the traces of his own blood on the scales.

“Hard pass, but thank you.”

They really were fundamentally different, but he knew the ancient  Death had his own small weaknesses that separated the god from the concept. The wobble of a pen when he was idle, the little hums and murmurs, the scribbles over his own redacted notes. How much, Noah wondered, had Anubis inherited from humans who had worshiped and feared him over the millennia?

So many questions rushed through his head.

Had Anubis ever got close to anyone before?

How was it to lose his comrades, the only ones who could match his age old wisdom?

Had he ever felt affection and desire to learn from a human?

“You are staring.”

“I am sorry.” Noah picked an excuse: “Just unused to this form of yours.”

The dark eyes squinted, small wrinkles in the corners.

“I thought it would be fine not to switch it again since it’s somewhat taxing. But if it bothers you…”

“Not at all!”

Instead, Noah put his book down after barely reading a page and leaned closer, shamelessly staring into the pitch black eyes that widened at the audacity. 

More questions.

Where did this specific form come from? Had it belonged to someone else before? Was it shaped in someone’s likeness? Was it shaped for someone’s likes?

Anubis held himself with a dignity unmatched by anyone Noah knew, but as soon as the man moved into his personal space the stoic face crumbled under the emotions the god was not used to having to hide. His eyes darted side to side, unsure what to do: for the first time ever in their proximity Noah was almost the same height, making the god feel… unusual.

“Have you made this form yourself?”

Noah’s fingers lightly ruffled through the dark hair, admiring the details of a few grey strands dropping on his fingers as he tried to fix it back. 

“It was inevitable to be forced to come to the overworld.”

More questions.

Why? For what? For whom? 

Had Death been wandering the streets of the mortal world for his job only?

It suddenly got Noah so intrigued, so interested and so… defeated.

He was jealous of the freedom, of the power, of the ability to come and go. Soon, Noah would just go, leaving behind so much he could only hope Anubis would enjoy in his stead.

“You should do it more often.”

How could the immortal not understand the joys of life, locking himself underground and distancing himself from the inspiration and whimsy humanity had to offer.

He was jealous of all Noah would never get to experience, all that Anubis chose never to experience out of his free will.

“Why?”

“There is a lot this world has to offer.”

Anubis scoffed, but weirdly didn’t pull away from the touch on his hair. 

“You talk like an old man.”

Noah chuckled.

“I am soon to die, let me pretend I lived a long enough life to sound a little sentimental.”

Anubis allowed the hands on his hair to drop down, poking at his skin, pulling at the perfectly ironed collar of his shirt.

“When I am gone, would you give it a shot?”

Anubis looked at the man with a hint of something Noah struggled to describe: a heavy, all encompassing emotion only a god could feel towards something as fleeting for him as a human life.

“I’ve given it plenty before.”

“Do it the way I would.”

Noah drops back on his seat and Anubis snaps out of it, suddenly aware of the way his face had softened with it his consent. 

“Elaborate.” 

Noah thinks back to his life and wonders how much of it he could recommend another. 

“Travel. I recently went on a trip - a life changing experience.”

“Noah, there isn’t a place in the world where Death hasn’t stepped.” 

Unfair. 

“Then do something daring. Just with a parachute.”

Anubis only raised a brow without comments. 

“Jump without a parachute?”

The deity sighed, putting his papers away. 

“It might be hard to understand, but humans are built to dream of goals befitting their short lifespan. In the grab scheme of things your existence matches only the short insignificant pleasures, and you should leave it this way.” He thought Noah would protest, but the man listened calmly. “Humans stay in the scope of their joys and their ambitions: you eat, seek comfort and socialisation, you reproduce. That’s the natural scope of things.”

“Then what’s the scope of gods?”

Anubis knew the question was coming, and still it caught him off guard. 

What was it that the gods dreamt of? Power? Control? Worship?

What ambitions would dictate the destination of their millennia long journey? With everyone else, at the end of their lives it would always be him. But who would be there for Death when its time comes? 

“We exist to exist. We live to serve the purpose.”

Noah lets out a groan. 

“Come on, be for real now!”

“I am, as you say, for real now.”

Anubis picked up his papers hoping he could pretend the conversation never happened, suddenly oddly ashamed of his uninspiring answer. 

“You work, you oversee death, but you’ll do that for eternity. Is there nothing else you’d like to do?” Noah leaned in again to pry into the gods' papers. “Wanna go bowling?”

They really were fundamentally different, but Anubis smiled. 

“Your attempts flatter me, and yet, leave them.”

“Not a bowling kind of guy?”

“Noah, imagine a man who’d never had any food but water and stale bread.”

“So me a month ago in your palace?”

He shut his mouth and nodded, keeping the jokes to himself. 

“If you were to give the man a drop of the richest spiced tea and a crumb of sweetest biscuit, do you think it would satiate his hunger for more?”

To the god, mortal ambitions and joys were nothing more than a second worth of pleasure in the lifespan of eternity. 

“We could still go bowling?”

“We could.”





Henry shuts the door to his apartment and presses his face against the cold wall of the entryway. The situation was becoming more and more of a mess as he recalled the messages he had received from his private investigation contact. 

They were all hiding something. A lot. 

“The name you gave has never been in any records, but I found his birth registration and residence. As for his family, that’s where it’s a little hazy.”

No registrations, no wedding papers, no death certificates. Only names that bore no records of existence, as if fabricated for convenience. 

A man with a perfect set up background to go around pretending he had nothing to do with the disappearance. 

Pretending he had no secrets. 

Henry slammed his fist into the brick wall and allowed the pain to numb his growing annoyance. He knew Emmy would not even listen, he knew she would dismiss it all out of sentiment towards a liar who knew about Leo, who was keeping him away. 

A liar who was standing between them. A liar who should have never walked into that mausoleum that nigh… 

‘You’ll pay for that…’

He knew he was being dramatic, but at the thought of Cecil’s fingers on his, or the intimacy they shared when his blood first spilled onto the stone coffin… 

He yearned for more, he knew he could have more. 

Pushing himself to the study, he rummaged through the papers in search of the old portrait he always kept around - the handsome face framed in soft curls, the ornate vest and silver rings. Cecil had been the definition of beauty to Henry ever since the moment he first saw the old painting for the first time. 

“You are there alone… I’ve kept you in that cold stone cage for too long, I’m sorry.” 

The universe was telling him the time was now, and no matter if the Cecil of today was a visage, a ghost or a fake, he knew that everything was telling him to cease the moment. 

“I’ll get you out, I promise.”

He traced his fingers over the old picture, moving to search for a letter he kept on his desk - a love declaration that was never addressed to him. 

He needed to hear him, even if the words would be in his own head. He earned for the sweet words and little sentiments, basking in the knowledge that whoever as meant to hear them was no longer alive. 

‘May death keep you away, but I will take back what’s mine.’

He finds a printed scan of the old parchment and pulls it from the pile of books only to pause. 

His fingers pick up something that was not there before. 

A long bright red, almost glowing hair. 





“Alright, behave.”

Anubis gives him a look, but says nothing, noticing how agitated and stressed the mortal was. 

“I’ll be on my best behavior, albeit I don’t understand why I am here.”

“Emmy said she wants to know who’s in charge of my health considering I passed out last time and keep disappearing under the excuse of hospitalisation. You should have picked a better lie last time…”

Anubis only grins, opening the door to the restaurant for him. 

“I’ll keep the chatter lively, don’t doubt my abilities.”

Noah was already full of doubt. 



They sit down and order: red wine, a fish filet for Noah and a raw steak for Anubis. Noah does not think much about the highly carnivorous choice of the jackal and only waits patiently. 

“She says she’s running late due to work.” He reads a message. “Says to start eating without waiting for her if we are hungry.”

The god immediately takes his cutlery and cuts into the bloody piece of meat. 

“Right, of course you are the hungry one of us…” 

“Variety in food is among humanity's best qualities.”

“So I gathered from Nephele.”

Anubis takes a sip of his wine and adds:

“Work at such a late hour?”

Noah’s fork freezes above the fish and he stares at the god with a furrow. 

“She started only recently, I would not be surprised if she’s often held back.”

“Right, by the history department?”

Noah feels his hands shake. 

“Nephele says she’s with the one and only target of yours as we speak.” 

Noah looks at his phone and feels the knot in his throat painfully growing. He was lied to, once again. 




“Who gave you the right?!”

Henry listens to the woman curse at him, the outcome of his confession exactly as he expected. Emmy tugs at her hair, unsure what to even say. 

“Take it as you wish, but your friend is a liar.”

“Shut up and leave him out of this.”

Henry was done with this stalling, taking a stance for himself. 

“Your friend goes missing and your other so called friend never bothered to tell you he might have his entire life fabricated? I don’t know about you, but I’d say it’s a little weird.”

“Weird?” She stared at him with open disgust. “What’s weird is chasing after a corpse. Get a grip on yourself and you better not step a foot near Noah, especially don’t you dare even think of asking him about his life. He has a lot on his plate without your insane gossip!”

She was about to storm out of the university break room before pausing to catch her breath. 

“We are done here. I thought asking you for help would be fine, but you only make me realise I want you nowhere near my friends. You lured Leo into god knows where, I should be reporting you to the police as we sp-“

She didn’t get to finish when a hand gripped into her collar, pushing her into a wall.

“You can say all you want, curse me as much as it takes to make you feel better,” he spat words like poison into her face, and Emmy shut up under the anger she had never seen the man show before. “But don’t you think you’ll stand between me and him.”

He saw the woman in front of him let out a small laugh before pushing him away with more strength than Henry expected.

“Stay away from all of us…”





Noah watched the doors of the restaurant swing open and saw Emmy look around the room in panic before spotting him waving and rushing to their side. 

“I’m so sorry I’m late.” 

Noah sees the distress in her eyes and swallows the bitter feeling of betrayal in his throat. 

“You didn’t have to run, it’s nothing.”

She looks at them with a complicated expression of concern and worry, a mix that someone could mistake for… guilt. 

She takes off her coat and jumps when her chair is pulled out slightly to help her. Noah watches Anubis be his most gentlemanly self and internally sighs. 

“I believe we didn’t get the chance to be introduced properly last time.”

He presents himself with at most kind smile and lures Emmy into a small chatter while they wait for food. Watching them talk, seeing a little smile on Emmy’s face as he tells her about Noah’s health improvements should have put him at ease, but every time he sees her look at him, Noah feels like crying. 

It really was a suffocating sensation. 

“Your pasta, ma’am.”

The moment her food is there, Noah excuses himself to the restroom and splashes water on his face. 

‘So what if she had a child she never told me about. Who cares?’

But one case is a president for a pattern. One lie is a taste of a fully fabricated tale. 

“You should be out there, you wanted this.”

He stares at the god through the reflection and sighs. Could he really not even go to a restroom alone?

“She’ll be fine…”

“Did what I say pressure you too much?”

Noah felt his fingers dig into the cold porcelain of the sink under them. 

“I don’t even know what I feel about it anymore. In a way, who cares if they tell me anything at all!”

He heard his voice break half way through but hiding the cry from the deity was impossible, so he buried his face in his cold hands and finally broke down. 

“I just want… to leave them with a good memory… of myself.”

The hand on his shoulder was heavy, but comforting. 

“They can hide as much as they want from me, I just… I wish they’d tell me a lie or two to at least make me feel like… like I’m there.”

The hollow feeling of leaving nothing behind crushed at him like a wave. 

“I just want to be here. I want to be a part of this.”

He took a moment to calm down, the god silently allowing him the moment they would not speak of while Noah washed his face and blew his nose. 

“Don’t tell her about this.”

“She doesn’t deserve to know.”

The god’s eyes were cold in his sympathy. 

“Noah, have you considered that they don’t deserve your honesty you pray they accept?”

He never intentionally tried to be honest, it just came as is. But thinking back to everything, he wondered if I he’d ever have the chance to demand something in return. 

That night could be the only chance for him to be selfish and demand answers.




The heavy lid of the coffin slid down onto the floor in the puff of dust. Untouched and undisturbed, the velvet that draped the bones lay sunken into the crevices of the withered body. 

Gently, Henry takes the bony fingers into his hand and feels just how frail his lover is. 

“These hands once touched someone who isn’t me.”

He knew he had no right to demand reciprocation. 

“These lips once kissed the skin of someone else.”

But what he could do, was to give them a chance.

To defy time.
