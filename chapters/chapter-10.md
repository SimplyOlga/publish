“Two dead, minor injuries on the passengers, yes, yes-”

He listened in on the report an officer was giving to someone on the phone. 

Two dead. 

How interesting, how coincidental. Noah thought back to his life, thinking that surely this was enough death around him by now. Still holding his grocery bag, his hand clenched from the stress of the events, he sat on the concrete road a bit away from the commotion and took a deep breath.

“Rough night?”

If he wasn’t so tired, the voice would have startled him, but instead he only moved to the side, watching Nephele sit by his side.

“I know.”

He didn’t need to think about it too much. 

“This isn’t your fault. That’s just how it works.”

“What, do you want some?”

Noah watched the crowd slowly disband, feeling like for everyone involved this would be a matter of two days of talk before the subject wears off. 

“So, will they now be judged and sent to the afterlife?”

The hound gave out a quiet bark in agreement. 

‘Well’, Noah thought, ‘as long as they find peace’.

His fingers brushed over the photograph. 

Soon, soon he would take his leave to fly out to London to see the portrait in person. To feel their eyes meet without the unnecessary medium, just the two of them, in one room. No photograph, no dry ink and no stone coffin between them.
