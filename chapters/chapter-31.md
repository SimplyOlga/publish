
The two stared at the small creature in the cell, with Noah biting at his lip. He sure hoped the creature would find them, but this was not the best way it could have happened. 

“Let us begin!”

The lights dimmed, leaving only the first item shown on stage. With only a couple hands rising up to add their bid, the entire audience grew captivated by the other two items. 

“Noah…”

Turning to look at Death, Noah had no excuses. 

“I don’t know how that happened.”

He said no lies, but they both exchanged anxious looks at the realisation: there was only one bidding they could do. Such were the rules.

“Whatever you are thinking about, abandon the idea.”

Sensing the intrusive thoughts to bid against the god’s will, putting himself on display, Noah only groaned. He had to make a choice. 

“Leo’s time is running out,” he whispered, “If we don’t get the scythe today, what are our chances?”

To begin with, neither had an idea if a random item belonging to the God of Time would even do them any good, so it was a gamble to begin with. But with how things have been aligning for Noah to be there that night, he had to try. 

“Can I do a bidding?”

“Absolutely not.”

“It says one bidding per person, I am a person.”


“Homunculus don’t count as individuals.”


The entire time the stranger by their side observed their argument with an amused smile, only speaking once the first part of the show was done:

“You two might have to make a decision quickly.”

Noah saw the way the god’s eyes glimmered in the light of the stage as the second item, the remnants of Saturn’s scythe, was brought back out. He had to make a choice, between the lives of his old friend and a being he brought into this world with responsibility to keep it safe.

“We will try next time.”

He felt his heart crushed, falling back onto the seatrest, refusing to look at the stage. Maybe it was for the better, he kept convincing himself. Maybe whatever force had been leading him along the way could finally taste his defiance.

“Noah, that creature is nothing but a low life, if you want to hear my opinion-“ 

“I don’t!” Noah hissed, almost raising his voice. “Low life or not, do you just want me to give up on it?! How am I supposed to look… my friend in the eyes when I say I value his artificial life but at the same time ignore this one?” 

He was in no mood for rhetorics, stumbling over his inability to recall Leo’s name. 

“Then you shouldn’t be carrying it without a leash.”

“When I put a leash on you you act all fine like you don’t need it. Considering it is of your blood and flesh I had hopes!”

“Oh wow, if you two go on like this after the show, please do invite me to listen.” 

Noah turned to the blindfolded man by his side and Anubis growled. 

“Stay out of this.” 

“I’m offering my hand to your companion in times of need,” the stranger offered his hand and Anubis looked at it ready to bite it off. “I have no need to anything here, but my bidding right stays.”

Noah saw where this was going, and feeling the eyes drilling into his skull, he still asked: 

“What would you want in return?”

“Noah, don’t even think about it…”

“And what do you expect me to do?! Sit by? You have no stakes in this, this is my issue, so whatever price I have to pay to save two living beings from the mess I caused I’ll do it!”

The stranger grinned.

“We could make a deal…”

It was at that moment that Noah felt his head split in two with pain he could barely handle. Cold sweat soaked his shirt in an instant, eyes shutting closed and fingers trembling. It was as if pressure was applied to him so heavy it was crushing him, both physically and mentally. 

For the second time since they had met, Anubis opened his mouth to speak in a tone that humans were not meant to hear:

“ረቹልህቹ ⶴጎጮ ዐ፱ፕ ዐቻ ፕⶴጎነ”

“Oh my, spear your friend and the audience.” 

Anubis snapped to his senses, seeing the way Noah shrunk in his seat afraid to move, hiding from the god that abused his authority. More over, the entire theatre turned to look at the three in the back, terrified to face Death, scared to even whisper their thoughts out loud. 

The auction came to a halt and using this moment, Noah grabbed onto the hand of the stranger and whispered: 

“If your deal means no harm to me or anyone I care for, I agree.”

It was a stupid precaution, one that would leave a million loopholes for a god to abuse, and still he agreed, out of desperation. 

“We have a deal.”

The god raised his hand when Anubis pulled Noah back and shook him to his senses. Ignoring the two, the blindfolded man proudly announced his bid: 

“170.”

Through the shaking and scolding, Noah wondered what the number meant. Gold? Some other trade unit? The idea terrified him even more when the entire hall went silent, unable to grasp the scale of the bid. To the gods present it was a significant stake they could not challenge. 

“Such is the final offer, I believe.” The voice of the host laughed, as if to mock the broke crowd. “The scythe is yours, my friend.” 

The stranger nodded and smiled at Noah. 

“Find me after the show, I’ll hand over the item as agreed upon.”

A small whine-like sound came quietly from behind Noah when he nodded. 

“You are a fool, an insolent fool.” The Death’s voice was that of a defeated man, tired to argue. “Will there come a day you listen to what I say?”

Noah felt like shit, the adrenaline from earlier gone with his courage. 

“We will talk about it later. I’ll get you your missing creature. Remember that by your own new rules, that means you owe me.”

“Excuse me?” Noah hoped he heard wrong. 

Anubis raised his hand the moment the announcer took in a breath to present the last item of the night.

“You make deals with gods for services you can’t afford,” the god’s eyes narrowed with a glimmer of something akin to petty and annoyance. “You’ve used my services plenty by now, so let me remind you something.”

“Oh my! We truly are honoured to be graced tonight by the Death himself, the end of all and the owner of none!” 

Whispers passed by the audience as every eye turned to watch the figure of the deity that brought fear into the cold hearts of those above laws of nature itself. 

“I am a god, Noah.” Anubis did not need to make an offering for he knew no one could offer more than the one who’d oversee the end of all. He didn’t even say a word to the announcer to have the auction concluded without a single attempt at a dispute. “You seem to be forgetting that.”



Sitting at a small bench just outside the auction hall, Noah fidgeted with the buttons of his shirt. In the haze brought by his growing anxiety, he recalled how the god held his hands when his mind raced to the night he took his own life. Then, he thought back to the hint of distrust in the eyes of Death just the moment later. 

He really had made a grave mistake. He thought Death to be someone compassionate enough to forgive him for his humanity. 

In simple truth, it was impossible by the nature of things. 

Noah looked around at the gods passing by, giving him side eyes, not daring to say a word to him before rushing away. It seemed that seeing Death gave quite a few of them a fright. It also seemed that Noah never quite related to the sentiment. 

‘I really was a tad naive… and reckless.’

He met Death face on and insulted it, toyed with it and thought it to be a companion. In truth - to Anubis Noah was nothing more than a mistake shown grace. 

Mentally Noah prepared to be locked away upon their return, obediently and with a sense of defeat. 

“Get up.” 

Noah looked up to the God of Death holding a small cage in his massive arms. 

“Hold this.”

His tone was cold even when he shoved the item into his hands. Even when feeling relieved to finally be reunited with his creation, Noah could not master even a hint of smile. 

“Thank you.” 

He only said, feeling like another harsh word would really push him over the edge. Luckily, Anubis allowed him a moment to compose himself. Figuring the mortal would not speak unless asked to, Anubis growled and commanded once again: 

“Let’s go. We will fetch the other end of your stupid bargain.” 

“I’m sorry.”

It was a pathetic apology, and yet the quiet defeated tone made Anubis pause. He really wanted to shake the human until his wits would finally fall into place. Instead he said coldly: 

“If you are sorry, then think about your actions when we are back.”

Noah nodded, dragging himself behind the god who no longer offered to pick him up to make it easier to match the pace. 



“Where is the Third Master of the parlour?” 

Anubis had no patience to walk around the place he despised, so he pulled a random employee who turned out to be another golden female creation. Her featureless face reflected the god’s dark silhouette when she responded calmly:

“The master is in his suite.”

“I am asking you where it is.”

About to use his commanding tone, Anubis took a deep breath and watched the woman walk, indicating for them to follow. 

“Of all of them, you had to pick him…”

They slipped through heavy curtains back into the area where they had met with Panacea, and took a turn deeper into the dimly lit corridors where the doors were locked with the light behind them, inviting and tempting. Noah tried to get his mind off the surroundings quietly trying his luck: 

“Who exactly was it?”


“The master is at the end of this hallway, I can not follow further.”

The title did not make Noah feel better about his situation, but he still had hope it was someone he could find common grounds with. 

“You will escort us to the end, I have no desire to wander these hallways.”

Even despite Anubis’ growl, the metal creation did not move, simply adding:

“Artificial creatures are not to go beyond this point. When it comes to his preferences and commands, master Cupid can not be persuaded.”

Noah wanted to hit his head into a wall. Cupid, the god of love. The very God of Passion Anubis had warned him was among the owners of the parlour, and by proxy - the strongest gods left in the pantheon. 

‘How?!’

The metal woman excused herself, and Anubis stared at the flabbergasted human in delight. 

“I didn’t think it was someone like him!” Noah thought back to the blindfolded man who seemed to carry himself with ease of indifference. He thought back to his appearance. “But he was all the way in the back.”

“Love requires intimacy.”

“He was hiding his face with a blindfold!”

“Love is blind, Noah.”

He really wanted to disappear right there and then.

“He was dressed like a mess, in some ragged clothes!”

“Noah, love is blind and everlasting. And most of all, he has poor taste and an odd sense of matching no one else understands.”

He really was everything Cupid ought to be…

They stopped before the last door at the end of the corridor, and Noah hesitated when Anubis waited patiently for him to take the step and knock. 

The moment they were let in, Noah felt the dizzying scent of perfume in his nose and forced himself to steady. It was heavy, intoxicating and almost immediately dizzying. Still, he propped himself against a wall and looked at the man enjoying his dinner. 

“You are finally here!”

In the better light Noah took a good look at the love of Passion: the slender man had half his face covered by an ornate golden mask, fully hiding his eyes from sight. On his figure he had a rather interesting mix of styles: a bright purple robe opened to an orange shirt, accompanied by a pair of jeans. It really was one of the worst matches imaginable, and Noah understood the irony of it all quite well.

Before Noah could even say his proper greetings, wondering how to best address a god after spending so much time with one, Cupid jumped from his seat and rushed to his side, leaning in to stare at Noah’s face with his masked eyes. 

“Hm…”

Feeling uneasy, Noah saw in the corner of his eyes how Anubis made no effort to stop this, as if teaching Noah a lesson. 

“Is something wrong?”


“Why do I feel like we’ve met before,” Cupid laughed, taking in the man from every angle. 

Noah thought back to his past relationship and sighed. 

“If so, could you have picked anything but her cheating on me?”

Cupid burst out laughing, slapping Anubis on his shoulder much to Noah’s shock: 

“You really are full of surprises, old Friend.”

Anubis pushed Cupid’s hand from his shoulder and growled. 

“Woah, still holding a grudge for that thing with Osiris? Buddy, I told you it’s harsh to blame me for what love had done before I was even born.”

Noticing how visibly impatient Anubis was becoming, Noah held the cage in his hands tighter and finally spoke on the matter:

“Sir, I came regarding your help earlier.” 

Cupid grinned, getting into a playful spirit.

“I bet your master here has warned you against making deals with someone like me.”

“I believe you know Death is no master of a mortal who is still quite well and alive,” Noah did not intend to let them both be mocked. “Please tell me what it is that you want as a payment for your generous help.”

Cupid pondered, bending fingers as if doing complex calculations. 

“It was a hefty sum to spend, you see…”

Still with no idea exactly what was used for trading, Noah held his ground: 

“Tell your price.”

“A favour.”

Cupid’s finger landed onto Noah’s chest where his heat beat fast.

“I ask for nothing but a bit of assistance in a little endeavour I have been at for a while.”

“A while? Something so elaborate?”

“There is nothing elaborate in love, my friend. And there is nothing simple about it either.”

Cupid fetched a small box from his pocket and noticing how Noah’s hands were already busy with the cage, he slipped it into the human’s back pocket. 

“Here is half the payment, the rest upon completion.”

Somehow the weight of it all was settling in, and under Anubis’ watch he nodded, listening to the brief details of a “project centuries in the making, a game of love so bizarre and cruel it was as exciting as it was infuriating to watch”.

“So, what do I do?”

“You will go see whether or not the seeds planted long ago are ready for harvest. See if the mortal love transcending time itself has finally cultivated into the desperate agony of loneliness.”

The offer seemed to follow the one condition Noah had set: it would not hurt Noah or anyone around him he cared for, so he accepted and left the room with a piece of the scythe, his creation, an uneasy feeling and a sulking god of Death.

“You are on your own here.”

He had no right to demand otherwise, handing the cage to the god who was going to return him back into the world of the living. 

“I will do it quickly,” he promised, more so to himself than the god. “And it will be all settled and good.”

The god with a reputation for mingling with human feelings was not an ideal loan shark to have after him, so he stared at the piece of paper with a name and a location and felt uneasy greeted with a familiar scenery. 

Two blocks away from where he lived, he sat on a bench, waiting for Cupid’s long term target to finally emerge from his apartment. 

The name seemed unfamiliar, the address was suspiciously coincidental. 

The face of the assistant professor at the university was one he could not mistake for any other. 

His target closed the door behind himself and picked pace on his way to work.

Henry had a long day ahead of him.
