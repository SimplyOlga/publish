He didn’t seem to have heard correctly.

“Pardon?”

The phone stayed silent. Staring at the locked screen, Noah checked the call history, noticing how the last call was after lunch. 

He paced around the room, staring at the empty diary in his hands, wondering if the old mediation in his kitchen cabinet would still be fine to take. 

No, sometimes things should be taken slow and proper. He opened his laptop, avoiding checking the calendar, and left an online request for an appointment with his old therapist. Next week, on Monday, he would talk about it and request a few calming meds until a proper diagnosis is issued. Yes, he was a reasonable adult who would not let unreasonable panic take over him. 

In the cold air of the autumn evening, he pulled the collar of his coat up and walked through the alley towards a nearby convenience store: his apartment was still in dire need of essentials after his trip. 

‘Toothbrush, toothpaste, potatoes, napkins…’

Revising the list in his head put his mind a bit at ease, shifting him away from unpleasant thoughts of the morning.

‘Paprica, lentils…’

He paused at the traffic light, waiting patiently in front of the red, tapping his fingers onto the cut of the pocket in a tempo of an old similar song. 

“Ma’am?”

He lifted a bag placed on the concrete, a lot heavier than it looked, and settled it on his shoulder.

“Here, hold on.”


The older woman nodded with a smile. 

“Good manners are always a good look for a handsome gentleman like yourself,” she put her arm into the crook of his elbow. “And that I can tell even with my barely working eyes.”

A simple gesture, one to brighten his day: a selfish indulgence in making himself seem a better person. 

“You know, everyone is always so busy, they really forget just what things really end up adding up.”

“Adding up?”

She smiled, an old face worn out by wrinkles and time, before taking in a step, pulled harshly back by Noah’s hand. 

“Oh, not yet?”

“No, ma’am, it’s still yellow.”

“Ah. No time to rush.”

In the heavy traffic, Noah thought about how maybe he should live in a quieter neighbourhood. One where cars at least slow down before the yellow.




‘Shit.’

How could one forget the schedule of the one grocery store they visit every other week. 

Noah pulled his phone out, checking the confirmation for the appointment next Monday, relieved to have at least one thing under control. 

Passing by a bus stop, he walked down the steps into the underground tunnel, cursing the slippery steps after the rain he just missed by a bit. 

Things were not quite in his favour, but it could always be worse.

“Fuck!”

A man next to him grabbed onto the railing, catching his breath.

“One could kill himself here! For Christ’s sake!”

Rumbling, more rumbling, noise of a bus and never ending chatter.

He really needs to find a quieter neighbourhood before all this drives him into grave.



Another turn, a needless 10 minute commute and he finds himself staring at an isle of freshly baked bread. Yes, he thinks, this day could still get better.

He picked up a loaf and turns it to check the wrapping before pressing it into the slicing machine. It gets a little stuck, so he nudges it with his hand, a little more, there, right before the blade cuts through the crust.

“This will do.”

He picks the loaf and turns to leave, his leg slipping on a freshly washed floor, balance shifting, head falling right back toward the bread stand. 




“Young man, you really are lucky.”

Noah sat down, feeling blood pumping in his head. Fast. A panic attack. 

September 14th.

What was it about that day?

He pushed himself up despite the concerned looks of the passersby, mumbling about how ‘just a step off and his head would have been-’

He didn’t care, taking deep breaths, even deeper breaths, trying to push the anxiety out of his lungs. 

‘Insane.’

‘14th.’

What was it about September 14th?

He knew. But did he? His head hurt like crazy, heart racing in his chest so fast it felt like even his steps could not keep up as he ran through the street. 

September 14th. He didn’t know what it meant, but someone did. 

“Excuse me, Sir!” He pulled a man from a cafe. “What day is it today?”

“Why, September 14th.”

“Right, but… what day is it really?”

The man thought for a moment. 

“You know what, now that you mentioned it, I feel like there was something… Something about it.”

There is an instinct inherent to all living beings, a sense of will to live, a force of self-preservation. In that moment, Noah felt his own tongue twist in a bitter sensation of raw, unadulterated fear of the unknown.

“Right, yes,” the man smiled. “September 14th. Isn’t this the day you died?”



The door shut closed behind him as he grabbed the notebook and flipped it all over: every month, 14th. Not a single note, on any of the dates. 

Then, what was it?

He didn’t know, but someone surely did.

With shaking hands, Noah reached for his phone, checking again whether there was a registered call. Yes, he had checked before, but of course… It was there. An unknown number.

Beep.

Beep.

Bee-

“Hello?”

He asked quietly, afraid to hear the voice he heard in the afternoon. 

He didn’t.

Instead, a lively high pitched voice came through:

“Hello there, you reached the emergency hotline for all matters related to your last matters! How may I help you?”

“Hi, I believe I-”

“Oh. Oh my god.”

The voice on the other side of the line let out a gasp before something covered the speaker, only letting muffled sounds of chatter come through. It felt oddly… enthusiastic.

“Are you sure you are calling the right number?!”

“Well, I am not, but-”

Before he could continue the voice behind the receiver sincerely laughed, talking to someone else, only sounds of giggling coming through. 

“Unbelievable! He really did mess up!”

“Excuse me, madame,” Noah pushed, trying to simply get a single question through and an answer back. “I am sorry, but who am I speaking to?”

“Well, it would be fitting the procedure to speak in person.”

“Procedure? In person?”

The voice giggled.

“How long we’ve been waiting!”



And with that, the light dimmed and the call ended.

Noah fell asleep.
