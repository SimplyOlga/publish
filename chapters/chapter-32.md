“Should we follow?”

Noah asks, knowing Nephele can hear him from all the way in the back. She suggested they stay separated as they follow the man, justifying it to be less suspicious and harder to notice, but Noah knew she could sense he was not in the greatest of spirits since he came back from their outing to the parlour. Anubis still had not contacted them for over two days, but Nephele did show him a picture of the creature being safely reunited with the Janitor that she took for him. 

“Let’s wait a little, he won’t get far anyway.”

He knew Nephele was giving him space, but even her consideration made him feel alienated and alone. He really wanted to speak to Emmy, so he typed a message inviting her for a glass of wine. Before he sent it, Noah looked at Nephele as if asking for permission. His dependence was starting to dawn on him, dragging his mood even lower in the gutters.

“Go for it. We have no deadline on this job of yours, right? Granted, we don’t even have an idea what exactly we are doing.”

“Things will play out by themselves, let Love do its job naturally.”

Cupid’s words gave him no guidance and at first Noah considered finding a way to contact him again, but the moment he saw Henry’s face the pieces were once again aligning into a puzzle he was nothing but an unwilling part of. He had to find out what the link connecting them all was, allowing the events to play out, as instructed. 

He pulled out the phone and drafted a message.

[Sorry for the absence, I am back now. Was thinking if you want to grab a glass of wine tonight, to catch up.]

He sent it and wondered if it was enough to show his sincerity.

[I miss you.]

He really did. If anything, Noah wanted to have at least one person by his side who would have his back no matter what, no matter what secrets stood between them. He waited for a bit, but as no answer came, he figured Emmy was already busy with work, despite an early morning. He would message her later. maybe.

Nephele’s phone rang and she frowned, debating cancelling it before eyeing Noah. 

“Pick it, I will wait here.”

He sat in wait, thinking of how he really was alone. 



Emmy eyed the messages and put her phone back in her coat pocket. She would answer Noah later, already coming up with an excuse that would sound convincing. 

“Good morning.”

The moment Henry caught up, she picked her bag and gestured for him to get a move on. Henry fixed his glasses and hurried behind her. 

“I spent the night with the archive material, all I could find,” he dug into his bag, awkward while still walking, almost tripping over his feet. “Wanted to see if there is any lineage connection between your friend and Cecil.”

“And?”

She was about to grab some papers from her hands, but Henry hesitated, holding them to himself. 

“I found nothing. Well, I mean… Yes, I found nothing.”

Something in his tone made Emmy squint. Assuming he was hiding something, she stood still until he broke under her eyes. 

“The reason I assumed there is no connection is because I quite frankly could not find anything on your friend.” Henry felt conflicted, the exhaustion of the past night weighing on him after endless searches with no results. “No records of the last name registration that could match his birth year, nothing. I have a friend who doubles in private investigations, so I called him…”

“You set a private detective after my missing friend?!”

Startled by her tone, Henry hid behind his glasses, avoiding her shocked expression.

“I found nothing. No police records, nothing. Nothing criminal, no registration or driving licence. One could say he never existed.”

With a yelp he gave into the hands that grabbed him by his collar. 

“Except he exists, don’t piss me off. You said you’d help me find him and I went on about your weird obsession, but don’t cross the line of telling me my friend was hiding something like this from me.”

‘But he was,’ Henry thought, only silently nodding when she pushed him away.

“I hate when people lie to me, I deserve better than someone I’ve known most of my life to treat me this way.”

The venom in her voice made the man uncertain about their relationship, but she only took a deep breath and pushed her hand through her hair to calm down, relaxing her face.

“He is dear to me, I can’t lose him. I can’t start doubting now, not now. Not when I have no one else left.”

Henry wondered what her relationship with the man he saw at the bar was. Surely, they too, were close, but she refused to bring him up over the days they spent researching together. 

“If we go on like this, there is something I must tell you, and I expect honesty.”


Emmy stared at him, eyes shooting daggers like she did not expect him to make  such bold requests. Still, he stood his ground.

“It is a matter important for the search, related to the last time I saw your friend,” he knew Emmy was about to shout at him for keeping something from her, so he immediately added: “But for that I need you to tell me who the other man was at the bar that night.”

“What do you want with Noah?”

‘So that’s the name…’

She immediately latched onto the question:

“What are you implying?”


“He was there, at the cemetery. I simply make connections.”

Emmy bit on her lip thinking back to the messages she left ignored on her phone. 

“Where is he now and where is the man that was with him that night?”

“Noah is home resting, he is sick, leave him be.” 

Henry watched her think, a complicated expression on the woman’s face. 

“He loved Leo the way I do, don’t make this difficult for him, he is already collapsing from anxiety.”


Soft of heart, Henry would never bring someone already struggling into the mess they were in, but the image of the two men watching them from the gates of the mausoleum was clearly not just a vision of his tired mind. He needed to know what exactly happened that night, and while he could not trust himself and his blurry memories, he had to find another testimony.

“If not him, then that older man that was with him.’

The woman seemed concerned.

“His doctor?”

“I suppose so, the one that was with him.”

He could see on Emmy’s face the confusion at realising Noah was not alone that night to the very end. Even Henry could tell from aside that she was starting to doubt their relationship. 

“Can we speak to him?”

“You want to drag another person into this?” 

“Listen, if you want to find your friend, you have to do more than just scout through papers and wait for him to show up!”

Startled by his outburst, Emmy only quietly agreed and pulled out her phone yet again, thinking about all the secrets that were forming between them.



“So what did that clown say?”

It didn’t take much to figure out Nephele held a deep personal dislike for Cupid. Who was Noah to dispute it? 

“That once a very long time ago he placed a bet with Saturn, the god of time-”

“I know who Saturn is, Noah.”

He really wanted a smoke, and Nephele nudged him to move from the bench towards a coffee shop. She was growing tired of his sulking mood, thinking that her own bad spirits were always successfully resolved by a freshly baked piece of flaky pastry. 

“Cupid said he found a way to let love overcome time and death itself, setting it on the top of the pyramid,” Noah grimaced. “His words, not mine.”

“So the guy we are trailing is his subject?” She recalled the offputting colour his body was in her eyes, wondering just how exactly Cupid managed to achieve that. Gods had only so many ways to interfere with humans in the modern time, there was no way he could have done something on such a grand scale alone. The thought made her feel uneasy and she flipped her phone open and closed again and again while waiting for their coffee, put generously on Anubis’ card. Death could be holding a grudge at Noah, but she had little care about keeping business expenses properly documented. 

“I have no idea, but I have a weird feeling about him.” There was no one he could tell about it but Nephele. “Back then at the cemetery, they were together. I don’t know what this freak wants with Leo, but… Leo went with him.”

“He isn’t the kind of guy to easily drag somewhere.”

Leo had been a part of a swimming club in university, which translated into his sturdy build matching his decent height. The man they were trailing on the other hand was smaller and a lot lankier, looking like even Noah could push him down if… well, push came to shove. 

“I wonder if he told Leo something to lure him there.”

“Have you asked him?”

He hadn’t. He should have.

“Well, in the time I’ve watched him, quite a bit has become clear about him.”

Noah silently asked her to elaborate as he took a long sip of his coffee. 

“He really is, as you put it, a freak.”

She told him about Henry’s fascination with a man from nearly two centuries ago. A man who-

“Resembles your friend down to every little detail.”

Noah took a moment to process what he had just heard, his head aching at the implications. How come everything was aligning in such a way that he was once again caught not in just some light accident but in a scheme of two powerful gods that had lasted centuries. It sent a shiver down his spine. 

“And Leo just happened to look like that guy?”


“Unlikely. But the implication is not in our favour,” Nephele propped her head in her hands and voiced her concern. “It is possible that whoever made Leo knew well about the scheme and even took sides with the higher ancient gods for the sake of their own benefit.”

Noah’s fingers clenched over the armrest of his seat. He did not want to deal with any more gods than he had to, and the idea that people behind Leo’s situation were acting with them was a terrifying prospect. He had to talk to Cupid again, once the job was done he would find a way to corner the deity into giving him answers. The best shot would be to have Anubis by his side, but that was another problem to tackle. 

They really were not on the same page about things. 

His phone vibrated. 

[Sorry, I have some stuff tonight.]

He was about to shoot Emmy a message that it was all fine when she immediately followed with another. 

[Actually, I’d be down. But there is a condition.]

He did not like the sound of it, but Emmy patiently waited for him to agree blindly.

[I want to meet that doctor of yours that came with you last time.]

Noah really needed a smoke and a bit of ice under his collar - life was throwing one stick into his wheels after another, and he was at least hoping Anubis liked sticks.




“Noah is not a murderer.” 

She put her phone down, and Henry shook his head. 

“I never said otherwise.”

“Noah is a sweet man. A great man.” A smile on her face made Henry regret starting that conversation. “Don’t bother him.”

“Then it’s not him that I want to bother.”

Emmy considered his words and typed another message to Noah, showing Henry his reply. 

“I will meet that man you mentioned, but don’t get Noah involved. Stick to your end of the bargain.”

Henry watched her walk away with a bitter aftertaste of being wronged. There really was something in that Noah that Emmy simply refused to see. Or maybe, he thought, she did see it, but chose to ignore it. He put the papers back into his bag and decided to take some time to call his investigator friend one more time. 




Noah paced around the room they snuck into to watch over Henry’s office from across the campus park. Nephele hummed a tune with her binoculars pressed tight against the window, while Noah struggled to come up with a solution to a problem: 

How was he supposed to just drag Anubis to meet his friend the god clearly didn’t see worthy of his time when they just had a petty argument. 

The Death would be the death of him…

“Can I borrow your phone to call him?”

Nephele gave him a look.

“You think the God of Death carries a phone with him?”

“But I thought you called him back then, in Sweden.”

“Nope.”

It stung to know Nephele really did have someone to casually chat with over the phone, but the thought of Anubis trailing behind them the whole time Noah thought he had a mission of his own was also a conflicting revelation. 

There was only one way he could summon the god. 

Noah sighed and grabbed a pair of scissors from a table and pressed them against his throat, feeling his own pulse spike as Nephele watched him with curiosity and side glance at the clock on the wall. 

“Three, two-”

The door to the room was kicked open and the middle aged man with dark black hair clad in a long black coat stared at Noah with a heavy breath. 

“So you are stalking after us…” Nephele chuckled when Noah put the scissors down under the daggers already flying from the god’s eyes. Rising from her seat, she bid them farewell. “I will let you two sort it out, I will be back in 20. Let’s make it 30.”

She shut the door behind herself and the two were left in silence. 

“You were watching this whole time?”


“It seems to me - for a reason.”

Noah dropped into a chair, too tired to argue. Anubis fixed his scarf and leaned against a window. 

“I told you your friends are more peculiar than you might think.”

“You knew? About this guy?”


“I had my ideas. But it’s not just him, you know.”

There it was again, the truth hidden from him by his old friend. 

“She wants to meet you.”

“We’ve met before.”

Noah stared at the god in disbelief. Sensing his confusion, Anubis spoke calmly:

“Not in a way you think. This is not my truth to share.”

“But you want to, just be done with it.” Noah felt like he had no powers to demand answers from anyone anymore. He was alone. His friends were never as close to him as he initially had thought. It was all in his head, so if he was to find a way around it, a way to free them from the mess he pulled them in before he’d disappear from their lives - he selfishly decided to demand the truth. “Tell me.”

“Very well.

“There is a term my old friend Time had once told me again and again in different faces and under different names.” Anubis looked through the window and in his human face Noah could read hints of melancholic nostalgia for the times long gone. “Mother of Death.”

It was a chilling term, but Anubis went on.

“Time told me there was a miracle to humanity in their ability to surpass death by giving life. I argued that such was the nature of everything: a flower withers leaving behind seeds for its children to be born. However, Time disagreed, saying that only a human mother can succumb to such a state of grievance over her lost child that she too dies along with it.”

Noah thought about Selma and her empty eyes staring into the distance waiting for her son who’d never come back. 

“Only a human tinted with mourning loses a piece of themselves that makes me, Death, watch from the sideline. A Mother of Death is a woman who overcomes a loss of her child by losing a piece of herself. In the old days, they’d follow suit immediately after their children, but these days it’s less common.”

“So she hid that from me?”

“It is nothing but a title. I did not intend to worry you for nothing.”

Still, Noah looked at his phone, thinking of the time Emmy and him first met - young, full of life, only 18. The world was their oyster and they swore to stay by each other’s sides no matter what. And still, there was so much he did not know. 

He wondered if she hid the pain so well, or if he was too blind to see it. 

“I see.”

There was so much everyone hid from him. 

“It changes nothing between us,” Noah stood up and patted his pants to shake off non-existent dust. It was time to ask for a favour. “There is something I want to ask of you…”

Anubis sighed.

“You don’t have to, I get it, I am impossible to control, I am irrational and impulsive, arrogant and too… lively for you.” Noah chattered as Anubis watched him in silence, basking in the self deprecation of someone who had played him just an evening ago. “But I really… can’t do this alone.” 

 He offered his hand and tried to push the feeling of helplessness away. 

“Please. I promise that once it's all over I will die nicely and obediently.”

Anubis let out a laugh and there was something mesmerising in seeing the god show emotions unrestrained by his animalistic features. Noah held back a chuckle of his own, feeling sweat on his hand and shaking in his fingers. 

Shaking his hand, Anubis only added one clause:

“That will come later. For now, promise me your best behaviour at the dinner and a decent choice of the venue.”
