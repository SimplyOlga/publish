It was another long night at the campus when Noah closed his books with desperation. 

“I can’t do it anymore,” he whined, utterly defeated.

Leo looked up from his laptop, laughing. 

“No can do, bud. You owe me dinner, remember?”

Noah let his head drop onto the desk, catching a breath, eyes almost closing from exhaustion due to the exam prep. 

“How does that matter now?”

Closing his notes, Leo ruffled through Noah’s hair. 

“You better pass that exam, get the highest score, get that bag and only then will I let you buy me dinner - an expensive one, mind you.”

He always had that in him - a stupid way to elevate the spirit, to say the words that would not matter and yet could cheer him up to keep going. 

For they were friends. 

Until death do them part. 





Noah thought he heard it wrong. The pulsation in his head echoed like a deafening beat, making him wince. 

“I will not kill him,” Anubis explained, the patient facade crumbling slightly after everything that had just happened. “I can not kill him.”

A small triumphant note of mockery, one he could not resist while watching the way Noah’s anger gave way to confusion, to fear. 

“You claim you know him, you claim it is me who is overstepping in the realm of my own jurisdiction, and yet - you don’t even know what you are dealing with.”

Noah wanted to ask the obvious, but he feared the answer. 

“Leave now,” Anubis said, a command, not a request, heading back to the scales, almost ignoring the man behind him.

“Where is he?”

“Detained.”

“Can I see him?”

“I didn’t let you speak to him then, what makes you think I will allow it now?” The god looked back, eyes quickly brushing over the smaller figure: dustied, pale, tiny and defenceless in the massive room that was never meant to accommodate him. “Go to your room, I will be handling this alone.”

Noah felt his jaw clench, his hands shaking. 

“If you can’t kill him, and you detain him here…”

He remembered the words of the cats from when he just “died”. 

“What will become of him if he stays here, detained but not dead?”

A tint and a smile crossed the canine features, as if looking at the way Noah struggled with words gave him a sense of pride. 

“Death is not a simple process, and there are a lot of ways to do it. He can not be weighed, but that doesn’t mean there isn’t a way.”

Gone was the calm, the exhaustion and acceptance:

“You don’t mean to say-”

Words caught in Noah’s throat at the memories of small articles he read while waiting for Nephele to go out. Articles on ancient Egyptian burials and mythology. 

A name came to mind. A method. 

“I can’t let you do this, he isn’t dead!”

“That’s for me to decide, you know nothing.”

Noah felt like an insult was just thrown at him: 

“Know nothing? As if you do?! I have spent half my life with that man, I know everything there is to know about him and all you will do is dismiss my words just like that?”

He thought back to their campus life, to takeout dinners, to gossip and videogames. They were human, so why was the decision left to someone who could not even understand something so simple. 

“You will feed his heart to Ammit, won’t you?”

At the familiar name, Anubis’ ears perked up as if he didn’t expect Noah to bring that up. 

“Yes.”

Noah took a deep breath. 

“He can not be weighed, just like you,” Anubis’ hand brushed against the golden plates of the scales again. “So, all that there is left is to have his soul fed to her, disposing of it in a way that always works.”

If the scales deemed the soul of a human light enough to pass into the afterlife, they lifted the heart up, above the feather. In other cases, the souls were fed to the demoness who cut their existence entirely. 

“I can’t allow that,” Noah said, knowing his own words were pathetically creditless. Who was he to try fighting back? 

Finally, Anubis turned back to fully face the small mortal in front of him, one of the millions he had seen in his long life. The man was shaking, sweaty and exhausted and yet driven by that very will that would defy even the kiss of death. 

A faint sense of nostalgia crossed through God's memories, of days of the past, of the worship and souls that would greet him as a companion, as the last scholarly pursuit of knowledge: with a question, with an embrace, with an offering. 

He had missed the days when he could speak to a mortal and still yearn for something he had never felt before. 

Feeling a sense of obligation over the mortals he was bound to serve, he raised his arm and called to someone in the dark. Noah watched two shadowy figures emerge from the sides: their long bodies and limbs swaying as they stood waiting for a command. 

Inhuman, ungodly, utterly wrong and melancholic in a way that made his heart forget fear and cry out for them instead. 

The god walked down the podium, coming close to Noah and turning him to face the creatures he struggled to look at. 

“Look at them.”

Noah obeyed, feeling like even the drive of the anger in him was dimming as he slowly understood just what Anubis was trying to show him. 

The hand on his shoulder did not press hard, it was almost gentle as the god held him.

“They once stood in front of me in this very room, fighting for their lives, for the lives of their loved ones, just like you, Noah.”

It felt grounding to hear the way Death itself called his name, his cold hands on Noah’s back. An embrace of the end showing him how foolish he was for defying its orders. 

“They thrashed, they fought, they proved themselves to be different. Some of them even thought the duty of serving me was an honour above ‘simple death’. There they are now.”

He didn’t need to be told in detail: he knew the shadows felt nothing, he knew they did not even see him. They were trapped in an eternity of existence without existence. 

“Can you not let them go?”

“They don’t want to leave anymore. They lost their desire to… desire.”

Noah looked away, and Anubis, sensing the lesson he was giving was learnt, let go of the shaken man. 

“Only those with a soul, or authority, can open the way out of here. But even then, they have to be willing to leave.” Anubis looked over the two in a way that almost made Noah feel like he recognised their old selves. Perhaps, even Death was not immune to nostalgia and loss. 

“So, is this what will happen to Leo?”

“If you don’t let me save him from it.”

Noah’s nails bit into the skin of his palms.

“This isn’t fair, he wasn’t supposed to die there, you killed him!”

“He wasn’t supposed to die because he wasn’t supposed to live to begin with.”

Noah’s eyes shot wide before Anubis immediately cut him off with a wave of his hand:

“This isn’t something for you to even know, leave now.” 

Seeing how Noah refused to move, the god issued a command and the shadows started slowly walking to him, causing Noah to step away.

“You don’t know that! He is a friend, a human like me, listen to me when I am talking to you!”

Noah bolted towards Anubis and caught his arm in his, pulling hard to get the attention, only meeting with the apathetic eyes that seemed to say: 

“I already know enough,” Anubis stood calmly, catching Noah in his arms to avoid him from thrashing around away from the shadows.

“What do you think you know?!”

He could see the long inhuman arms stretch out to catch him, to take him away, to drag him into the depths of the underworld, so Noah only gripped harder into Anubis’ sleeve. 

“You know nothing abo-”

“You were born June 17th, at 11:32 am you took your first breath and cried out,” Anubis started, ignoring the way Noah’s eyes shot wide. “You have a sibling, a sister, and your mother is still alive. You graduated with grades below average and went for a degree in economics, graduating but not securing a job.”

As Noah’s arm went limp and fell from his sleeve, Anubis let out a chuckle, eyes narrowing into a smirk.

“Am I missing someth-”

“November 14th.”

Anubis shut his mouth. 

“I was born November 14th,” Noah simply stated, pausing to bask in the way the god’s mouth closed with a clank. “Oh… my… god…”

It was then that he let go, a laughter spilling from him in a way that he could not hold back: freeing, relieving, ecstatic. 

“You… You really have no idea how much you fucked up, do you?!”

Anubis let go, dropping him onto the floor and Noah only went on laughing, his voice echoing in the room. If only the cats were there, he knew they would have appreciated the moment as much as he did. 

“You are… incredible,” he struggled to catch his breath, pushing himself up to turn around Anubis to catch a glimpse at the face of utter panic that washed over the ancient god.

“Unbelievable,” Noah wiped a tear from his eye and climbed up the podium to look Anubis in the eyes, on his level. “How does it feel, not to know jackshit?”

The god stood silent, calculating, unable to answer. 

His hand rose to give a small wave to the guards behind him. 

“This changes nothing,” leaning in, the hound growled. “You ought to learn to behave and know your place when I do what needs to be done, mortal.”

The long arms of the shadow guards wrapped around his shoulders and dragged him out of the room where Anubis stood for a long while still, in the silence of the room, ears twitching at the phantom sound of a mere mortal laughing at him. Rightfully. 





It had been 2 days. Noah sat on the bed, listening to the shuffling of the suitors behind the door. Once he was locked in there, they were his only company. 

“Come out, sugar~”

“I told you, I can’t~”

He even spoke to them now, out of boredom. The voice on the other side of the door whined.

“But you are so delicious, what use is it for you to sit there all alone?”

He caught the apple he had been tossing up and down and let out a tired:

“I know right?!”

He looked at the perfectly red apple in his hand and felt his stomach ache: last time he ate was two days earlier, and even then it was a supermarket snack. He eyed the fruit but then sat up and threw it at the bowl of fruit on the desk, scattering pears and grapes around the floor. 

He would not eat what was given to him. Starvation was a better option, even if he could not actually die. 

As he fell asleep, so exhausted and malnourished he would not wake up even to the knocks of the visitors, he dreamt vividly of Anubis and Leo, of blood and the man’s exposed heart on the scales, dropping down, heavy. 

He dreamt of the sight at the cemetery, waking up coated in sweat, breathing hard. 

It was driving him insane, so by the fourth night, he curled into the blanket and cried, on the floor, wondering where Leo was and if he could ever see his friend again. 


On the morning of his fourth day under arrest, Noah walked up to the bowl of fruit, wanting to smash it only to pause. 

It was pristinely arranged, just like before: a bowl of fruit at the desk, without a speck of dust or juice from Noah throwing them around the days before. 

Someone had come to his room when he was asleep, to clean up. The thought was terrifying, but he only chuckled at the idea. Anubis would not lower himself to such a state, so there must have been someone akin to a servant, and if there was one thing Noah could do when needed it was using his charisma for personal gain. 

Quickly he found a piece of paper and a pencil, pausing to wonder what language he should even use to talk to whoever had been visiting him, wondering if reading was even within their abilities. In the end, he decided on the universal way of making a friend, scribbling a couple lines on the parchment:

[               :)           ]


Satisfied, he put the paper right between the fruit and rearranged them, less neat, enough to attract their attention next night. 

Now he was ready to sit another day in his containment chamber, but at least with a sense of some purpose, something to look forward to. 

He will get out. 

And not by the will of Anubis.
