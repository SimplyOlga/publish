It was, undeniably, a dog head. Sat on a set of broad shoulders it turned to look straight at Noah, the eyes expressionless, and yet terrifyingly wise. 

“I never make mistakes.”

The jaw did not move, the features long and sharp, close to a doberman and yet different. There was a hint of a wild animal in the way the eyes stayed locked on him, and yet a very human-like dignity to how the creature held itself. 

“There is nothing wrong with making one after all these years, Sir.” 

“After all,” the second cat chimed in, “You’ve been at it for so lo-”

“What mistake could I have possibly made?!”

Noah pressed himself behind the two felines that only giggled. 

“Him.”

The two only stepped to the sides, showing him to the hound that walked up to him with a speed that would have sent anyone running: if only Noah’s knees weren’t already shaking with realisation that this might have been something other than a weirdly immersive movie set. 

“Who are you?”

The creature leaned in, cutting the distance between them. The towering “man” was close to at least three meters, reminding Noah of the murals on the walls on the way here. 

“I’m Noah.”

“Well then, Noah,” the soulless eyes drew closer, studying the way sweat rolled down his neck. Dogs, as he knew, were sensitive to fear. He tried to compose himself, but only gave up as the voice came through again: “It seems to me that you are perfectly dead, so, what seems to be the problem?”

“Perfectly dead? What’s perfect about that?”

The man tilted his dog head to the side, just a little, in a manner unbefitting a properly dressed giant. 

“Death is natural. Death is what comes for everyone without exception, so, there seems to be no mistake here. You bothered me needlessly, yet again.”

He straightened up with a sigh, turning to leave when a pack of documents was offered to him. 

“Oh, but this time it’s different.”

“He called us, on the line.”

The dog ears twitched.

“You mean… That line?”

The cats grinned. 

“That’s impossible,” the dog growled in frustration, about to toss the papers away, when his eyes turned to a few lines on the paper. “That line is a joke, a way to rid me of your presence, so please leave.”

Despite his own words, the man read through the notes, as Noah tried to sneak a peak on his tippy toes. 

“What is this?”

“His files.”

The dog stared at him again, now a clear feeling of unease and confusion coming through the eyes that glimmered in the dark.

“This can not be. He is properly dead as one should be. Here, you,” he pointed at Noah. “What’s your death day?”

“September 14th,” the words escaped without him even realising, as if pushed by some subconscious force. He shut his mouth and covered his lips with a hand, afraid what could follow up. 

“That’d be yesterday,” the boss said triumphantly. “He is dead.”

“But he has no appointment.”

“Ugh!”

Tossing the papers back at the employees he walked up to Noah and pressed a finger onto his chest. 

“Stop this thing.”

Noah was about to ask just what he meant when a sharp cold sensation pushed air out of his lungs with a small cry. It felt like his entire body, filled with cold iron, was pressed down, weak and exhausted. Inhuman. Dead. 

“Now, let’s see.”

A hand, slim and cold to the touch, slipped through his shirt, through his skin, down between his ribs and over the lungs, only to catch the unmoving, resting heart, dormant after all the time of work. 

Noah felt like throwing up, but even his lips, already turning ice cold, would not open, would not move for anything but quiet breaths: more so out of habit than necessity. 

The hand pulled out, holding the heart: small and pathetic in the wide palm that engulfed it without much care but with professional attentiveness. 

“You… How could you-”

He tried to reach back for it, but the hound only supported his falling body the moment his legs swayed. 

“There will be no scar, no wound. Precision is the difference between a surgeon and a butcher, and I take pride in my craft.”

“No one is further away from a doctor than you,” one of the cats picked Noah up, holding him upright as the dog walked to the side, coming back with a small platter. 

“We don’t have the time for all the procedures,” the jacal placed the golden platter into the arms of the other cat and dropped the heart on it, reaching for something from his pocket. “I have a few extras.”

A tiny glass bottle popped open and a small feather was placed on the side of the plate. 

“Heavy,” the cat joked, rushing to the pedestal where the massive throne lit up in flames, revealing a silhouette behind. 

‘Oh my god…’

Finally, it was starting to make sense. Weakly, he thrashed in the arms that held him until the assistant shushed him: 

“Shh, this is the fun part!”

“I am… not dead!”

“No one is dead until pronounced dead by him!”

They really weren’t listening, so Noah tried to rise up, looking at where his heart was taken, placed on a platter on one side of the scales. 

‘Absurd. This is absurd!’

“Now, we will skip the formalities, since you only need a proof.”

The jacal walked to the scales, picking up the feather before moving to the opposite side. 

“Here it comes, the fun part!” The woman budged Noah to look where the judgement over his own life was about to take place. He was young, too young for this. And sane. Too sane to imagine himself judged this way. 

“You know, everyone is always so busy, they really forget just what things really end up adding up.”

“Adding up?”

The words of the old lady from earlier came back to him. 

Had he lived his life with enough to add up? What was it that was weighing his heart down. And why did he feel so much lighter when it was taken out. 

He fucked up, didn’t he?

“Wait, I beg of you…”

The cat held him, unyielding. 

“I am not done, I am- I mean I can still do more, please!”

Without looking at him, the hound put the feather above the scale. 

“Don’t worry,” the cat whispered. “He messed up.”

The hand let go, the feather falling down. 

“Everyone makes mistakes. Including him.”

With a loud bang, the feather hit the platter, pushing down. For a moment, as Noah held his breath, the scales moved, adjusting, dancing in the light, casting shadows around the hall until- 

BANG

BAM

The two platters snapped from the chains, falling down, blood spilling from the heart that resumed beating.
