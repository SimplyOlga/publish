The next morning, the fruit vase was filled anew, the note gone completely. Noah felt like giving up after throwing all the fruit onto his bed to check for another piece of paper, but he was met with nothing. Whoever it was, they had not responded, simply restoring the room to the default state. 

Pressing his face onto the cold surface of the glass on his bed, he took one of the apples and peeled it, less so out of desire to eat it, and more so to have something to do. He sliced it and left it by the fruit basket, as a peace offering to whoever might visit, wondering what he would do for the rest of the day since his only entertainment those past couple days was nothing but waiting for night suitors to give him their cryptic attempts at courting-

Knock knock.

Less of a knock, more of a light tap of something on the door from the outside. 

Nails. 

Noah shivered in the broad daylight, rising up, almost tumbling on his feet when his head spun out of exhaustion and lack of sustenance. He had figured that his biological condition was draining slower in the underworld, but the days without food were starting to catch up on him, so he walked to the door slowly, pulling the chair and carefully climbing up to look at who it was. 

Not a spirit. Not the empty corridor. Not the cats and not the god he wanted to see the least. 

He missed Nephele. 

A pair of eyes stared at him from close by, almost making him jump if it wasn’t for his lack of powers. 

“I know you are there.”

A low female voice, a slight tease to her tone. Noah leaned in again, taking a good look at the tanned face, long thick braid of dark hair and most of all the eyes that seemed to stare right back at him through the small window. 

“Would you show me your face?”

So, Noah thought, they really could not see him back. 

“Why?”

“Oh! The voice will do too,” the woman laughed, leaning against the wall, giving him a full look at her complexion. She was tall, as tall as Anubis, giving him an uneasy feeling about her importance. “I have heard rumours of you, human. Heard you being called many things, one more intriguing than the other.”

“Were they good things?”

“A “nuisance”, a “sweetie”, maybe even a “honey glazed cake”.”

He knew where each of them came from, or so he imagined.


He frowned at the titles, and the woman, sensing his disapproval, laughed:

“The last two are nothing to worry about, simple copycats with no manners,” she clicked her long nails. “As for the first… He often lacks tact as well, doesn’t he.”

He wanted to agree, but the way her eyes studied him through the door made him choose his words carefully. He asked for new allies, but he was not naive to jump into the hands of just anyone, especially if they looked and acted like someone with a lot more authority than him. 

“It was him who locked you up, right?”

“Maybe, or maybe I just like it here.”

She leaned back in, a little grin on her lips:

“But you would like to leave, isn’t that so?”

The timing of her appearance made Noah feel uneasy: did she know about the note, or was she just lucky in her assumption.

“I can’t just smuggle you out of the underworld, that would piss him off too much,” she fixed her brain and tapped lightly on the door again. “But we could go for a walk, what do you say?”

It really was a tempting offer, and more than anything, if she would really keep her word of just having a stroll, it meant one good outcome. Noah could already see the look on Anobis’ face when he would see him casually roaming free. 

“Let’s make a deal.”

The woman nodded, a hint of excitement in her eyes. 






Nephele stared at the man behind bars with an apathetic expression. Human lives didn’t matter much to her, she was simply doing her duty, but something about the man was unnerving even when she had her glasses on. 

Taking them off, she opened her bright eyes to look at the man who was not only lacking any colour to his soul, it was as if the very contours of him were ripped and put together in bits and pieces, like an imitation, an unnatural pattern.

“Like what you see?”

The man teased under her gaze and she shook her head:

“Not particularly.”

By the time her boss arrived, she grew wholeheartedly bored with waiting, immediately slipping out of the room the moment her employer deemed the conversation confidential enough to dismiss her. 

Anubis looked at the figure, the human switching his expression from friendly to that of disgust the moment the woman left. 

“I knew something in that place reeked, but I could not quite tell if it was a mutt or death, turns out it was worse - both at the same time.”

Anubis didn’t find the joke amusing, sitting down on a chair right in front of the cell.

“I will ask questions and you will answer.”

“Why would I?” Leo took a bottle of water left for him and spinned it on the floor. The only entertainment he had in the cell was not a way to show how little he cared for the conversation at hand. “You can’t force me to, you can’t even torture someone without a soul.”

Anubis waited, patiently.

“Have you told him?”

At the thought of Noah, Leo laughed, realising the weight of the situation:

“Let me guess, he thrashed and argued about my fate? How cute. Bet he hates you now.”

“I will manage.”

Leo grinned, feeling like with no leverage from the god of Death, he could freely spit onto the fresh wound of Anubis’ predicament:

“Must be rough. So many centuries spent thinking you know it all, assuming your own omnipotence only to be faced with utter helplessness.” 

It was formed as a question, and yet it held no such intent. A statement:

“You know what this all means, right?” 

“Care to spell it out for me?” Anubis stretched his arm and tapped his long claws on the metal bars as if reminding the prisoner of his position. 

“An age old question,” Leo stood up and walked closer, staring at the sitting deity from above, taking in the small superiority he held. “Can death itself die?”

Anubis looked at the inhuman figure with lack of any interest in the ancient riddles that had caused him enough nightmares already. 

“Everything comes to an end, and everything dies. Death is omnipotent, and therefore even death dies.” 

Leo nodded, content. 

“Don’t you think it’s time?”

Letting out a small chuckle, the god stood up, bearing his fangs. 

“I knew your lot had nerve, but it is bold of you to assume any of you know better about when it is my time to go than I do.”

“Oh, but you have already made an error in your judgement recently.”

Watching the way the god’s ear twitched, Leo laughed, hearty and sincerely amused. 

“One precedent might lead to a pattern, you know? How can you know for sure you won’t be dead yourself right about… now?”

Anubis held back a curse, judging the way his own muscles tensed at such a stupid provocative remark. 

Leo sat down on the floor, pleased with himself, a wide smile tinting his eyes that stared at the god without any shame. 

“I hope when you die you will have at least one person who doesn’t hate you around you, really. It would be a shame if even the very omen of your impending doom would celebrate your demise, grateful to see vengeance for the life of his poor young friend that you would reap with your own beastly hands from him.”

Leo made a mockingly pleading face and laughed watching the god leave the cell room in silence. 



In the hallway Anubis walked away from his usual chambers, heading deeper into corridors he never normally visited. A few doors later, he found what he was looking for - a small metal door that opened with a rusty squeak, letting a puff of heavy acidic air out. 

“Don’t open the door too wide! Oh, the temperature!”

A figure that emerged from the puff of unknown gas pulled him in and shut the door behind them.

“Ah, Monsieur! I did not think it would be you!”

The man patted his messy apron and unsatisfied with its cleanness dropped the stained cloth to the side, smiling at the visitor from under slightly burnt hair. 

“For what do I owe the pleasure?”

Anubis walked a little further into the lab, passing by shards of exploded glass and bits of organs he didn’t want to dwell on. Death itself was obviously not gross to him, but the actions of the man behind him would sometimes make even his stomach uneasy. 

“I have come with an offering of a subject to you,” the god picked a few notes and read through the hasty lines of French. 

At the prospect the scientist’s face lit up and he rushed to the god’s side, cleaning up a chair for him, watching the way the deity ignored it, inspecting some of his jars and exhibits instead. Eyes, hearts, gems and unidentifiable liquids: a collection built over centuries of work driven by both obsession and fascination each derived from the other. 

“If you offer it yourself, it must be something truly fascinating, I can already say I am in!”

Anubis finally faced the youthful face, admitting that a few stitches here and there needed a touch up. 

“There are conditions.”

“Anything you wish, Monsieur!”

Even in the obvious situation where it was the god who needed his help, the researcher eagerly nodded to the conditions that followed, not even trying to bargain or negotiate. 

“First, you are to keep all and any information about the subject and your involvement a secret from absolutely everyone in the underworld,” Anubis watched the man nodding, already packing his instruments up and putting on his bright mustard coloured outdoors jacket.

“And second?”

“You are to fix your face before you walk around.”

The scientist frowned at the stitches he saw in his reflection and agreed he would take care of it. 

“But Monsieur, what exactly is it that you have so gracefully brought for me?”

Watching the alchemist pull at the threads around his face to tighten it up into a mask perfectly concealing his decaying age, Anubis peaked his interest even further with just one line:

“A homunculus.”
