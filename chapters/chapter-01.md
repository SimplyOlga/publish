“A direct hit on the intervertebral discs, despite their relatively sturdy structure, poses a high risk of crashing through vital organs.”

The woman picked up a piece of bone encased in a glass box, turning it to the audience. 

“While it would require solid force, once a hit is secured, the shattering of the structure is highly impactful: both on the nerves and the lungs that could be damaged by the debris.”

“What about paralysis?”

Fixing her glasses, she looked at the student in the front row, still adding notes to his already messy notepad. 

“Paralysis is the most common outcome of an assault of this kind. It could be as damaging as a full paralysis of the entire body below the impact zone. In better cases, some of it can be resolved. That is, if the victim is lucky enough to make it at all.”

The class silently looked at the piece of bone in her hands, as she passed it to be inspected around the massive auditorium. 

“Such wounds don’t tend to leave behind witnesses. They leave behind evidence.”

A chatty couple in the middle row felt uneasy turning it around. 

“What you are holding there was once a human man, now it's nothing more than a piece of medical equipment and a part of a never solved mystery.” 

“Mystery?”

“Mhm,” she leaned onto the professor’s desk, hands crossed on her chest as if recalling something from many years ago. “It was ruled out as an unfortunate suicide. A man, found hanging from the tree with his neck broken by the ropes and his back hit into the tree so hard it was stuck on branches that left the very crack you are seeing in the vertebra.” 

The class went quiet. 

“It was suicide, yes. Or so it was ruled out. But really, would someone have enough strength in their last moments to thrash and push themselves so hard that they would… well. That’s exactly why you are here, right?”

She gestured at the audience. 

“One day you will be able to use what I teach you to make your own judgement and shine light on cases deemed unsolved. It’s been an honour today, and I will see you next week.”

The student in the front row, one diligently noting everything down, paused to look at the exhibit with extra care, sketching the outline and the placement of the crack on his papers. 

“Maybe you will be the one to tell me how it happened one day?”

He stared at the professor with a shy look of a student caught off guard by the one question not answered in any of the prep material. 

“I am just teasing, go have your lunch break.”



She packed her bag, wrapping the glass cube into a cloth and sorting through the notes while the students rushed out of the room to carry on about their day. 

“I thought you’d go too, for lunch that is.”

“Waiting, patiently.”

“Come here, my god you got tanned.”

She opened her arms to welcome a man stepping from the back rows of the auditorium, giving him a hug that spoke of months of separation. 

“You listened to the whole thing?”

The man nodded, picking up a sponge to clean up the whiteboard. 

“Teaching suits you.”

“It’s better than field duty, come now, I will treat you to a staff discount at the cafeteria.”




“Now, I thought you’d be here later,” the two took a table in the corner of a small area on the campus where the wide windows were blocked by the now dying autumn trees. “Noah, it’s not like you to be punctual.”

The man smiled apologetically, setting his fork down and picking his phone. 

“Thought I’d be today, for a change. Listen, uh, your lecture was great, I mean it.”

“But?”

Noah tapped on the table with clear unease, thinking how to better bright the subject up. 

“No buts,” he finally let out a chuckle, picking up his fork. “Today is all about your debut. To Miss Graham, the new forensics professor!”



“Yes, I spoke to Emmy, she was all fine. The job suits her.”

Noah pressed the phone between his cheek and shoulder, patting his pockets in search of a smoke.

‘Right.’

He quit two months ago. 

“No, no, she seems to like the dorms too, it’s temporary anyway. She said they’ll be back together soon, but I offered her to crash at my place anyway.” 

He nodded a couple times, feeling like he could at least use something like a drink, a coffee, anything to clear his parched throat. If not nicotine, then something with a lot of sugar. 

“I will tell her, yeah,” he was only half listening. “But listen, there was actually something I wanted to ask.”

How was it so hard to phrase. 

“No, no, nothing urgent. Just, I have this weird feeling like today I had something planned, or maybe it was an important date?”

He once again checked the date on his watch. 

September 14th. 

“No, I already checked, and not hers either. I don’t have anyone with birthdays in September.”

He listened to a long speech nodding with half boredom and half frustration. 

“My anxiety issues have been a lot better since the trip, I promise. Yeah, I will check. Maybe it’s just some minor plan. I will go through my messages and, well… How about a drink later tonight? Tomorrow? Great. See you then!”

Noah hung up, feeling like pressing his head against a cold wall of the campus. 

‘Maybe it was about Emmy’s lecture? No, but… What was it?’

He thought back to the dates of the anniversary of his last relationship. 

‘Not that either.’

What a weird thought: an obsessive idea that you had no origin or reference to. 




After returning home, he put the kettle on the stove and sat down in front of a big suitcase that was still half packed with souvenirs and clothes. But there was one item he was particularly interested in. 

As soon as the cup of tea was done, he dialed another number and set to unwrapping a few tote bags inside the luggage. 

“So, I was thinking about that date thing, you know, when I said I feel like today was some important date I was forgetting?”

Without giving much room for response, he tossed aside a shirt and a loose shoe. 

“I thought maybe it’s a loan payment, but that’s been automatic for a while. Then I thought it was the gym bill, but I did cancel it before the trip. So I’ve been really out of ideas.”

“I think maybe it was something in the passing. Was it my anxiety? You think it’s my anxiety? You do, I know you do. I mean… I am not angry, really. Just…”

His fingers finally touched the smooth surface of a leather bound notebook he was looking for.

“It’s hard to get back in the same routine after such a long absence. You know, back then, I was even wondering if I could just not come back at all. I mean, I could settle down there, find someone, get a house. I mean, I did come back, so don’t even start!”

He flipped through the pages with old dates: February, April, June. 

“Maybe it is anxiety. I will give the doctor a call again, if he still has my number even. It’s been a while.”

Finally, it landed on September. 

“I missed you, you know.”

September 7th.

September 10th.

September 12th.

September 14th.
