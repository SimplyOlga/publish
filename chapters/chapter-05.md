The room stayed silent, only the ringing of the shattered chains echoed in the air before the cats burst out laughing: 

“We told you! You made a mistake!”

The hound’s hands clenched into fists. 

“Weighing a living being! On the scale! Oh my, you really are as stubborn as always!”

“Shut it!”

The two muffled their laughs, with little intent to really hide the overflowing joy. 

“What is wrong with you!” The man grabbed the heart and walked up to Noah, whose skin lost colour and lips turned fully purple, only a slimmer of warmth inside him keeping his mind intact. With more force than before, the hand slammed the heart back into the chest, pushing a gasp out of his lungs, colour slowly returning to his face. 

“Oh my god, I though- I thought I was going to die!”

“You should have done that before coming here!”

“Well, I am sorry!”

Before he could catch himself, he spat the remark, met with utter shock from who he assumed was none other than the god of death. 

“I mean… I am sorry I am alive. I wasn’t planning on dying today, and I didn’t, isn’t that good?”

“Not really.”

Finally, a note of exhaustion crept into the hound’s voice, and he walked away, hands on his long muzzle. 

“So, he has no reservation?”

“No.”


“But he has a file, and basic information. And he has the date.”

“The date? You mean the date of death?” Noah finally managed to stand up straight, casting a look at the broken scales. He really did that. Was he supposed to pay for the damages?


“Everyone has a date of death. It comes with the date of your birth, one can not exist without another.”

Noah shivered, and noticing that, the hound growled. 

“Death is essential, death is natural. It is more natural than a lot of things you willingly accept: hair dye, carbonated drinks, blue food colouring. Death is ancient and death is eternal. Death is the only thing in the world that is not destined to die.” 

If anything, the explanation was passionate. But not what he needed.

“But I am alive.”

The hound thought for a moment. 

“We could fix that.”

Something in the eyes of the god glimmered as he took the step that made Noah back away: 

“Wait, no, wait a moment! You can’t just do that!”

“He can,” the cat next to him shrugged. “That’s his specialty.”

“I mean, how is this fair! You say I am here because I didn’t die at the time, and yet you were the ones calling me, saying all that weird stuff about my death! Sorry I didn’t get the memo earlier! Stay away!”

He rushed to the door and tried to pull it open, not noticing how the god behind him stopped. 

“Called?”

“We never called you,” the white spotted cat added with concern.

“The dates of the death are never revealed before the trial, and we never dial anyone ourselves.”

Noah turned, more confused than concerned. Why was nothing adding up today? How could he not even die properly?!

“You called me earlier, about 1pm. Listened to me whine, and then told me that today would be the day I’d die.”

All three shook their heads, in disbelief at his words. 

“What about the man then, the one who was creepily saying the same stuff, come on! You guys can’t even let someone die in peace?”

The dog growled, shutting him up:

“If anything, peaceful rest is what we do. What you say is nonsensical.”

“But it’s true, he isn’t lying.”

Something in the way the cats looked at him said they fully knew he was speaking the truth. He nodded in appreciation, trying to ignore the scowl of the god growing impatient. 

“That’s how I got the number, I called back and here I was.”

After quickly retelling the events of the day, emphasising the call and the ordeal about the date, Noah watched the god pace the room as the cats whispered among themselves, too serious to let anything be heard. 

“You are an anomaly,” the god finally placed his verdict, avoiding meeting the eyes of the felines who prayed he’d admit his mistakes. “It was not my doing. Your name is not in my books, and your heart is still beating after my touch. You do not belong here.”

“So, do we send him back up?”

In two steps, the god walked up to him and leaned to place his muzzle right into his face. 

And then he sniffed.

And again.

“He smells of death, he is dead. And yet…Keep him here. He is not allowed in the realm of the living. Not until I know what exactly went wrong and how he was marked for reaping today.”

“Wait, I can’t stay here! I mean, I am alive, no?” Noah watched the way the god of death struggled to dispute that. “Why can’t I leave?”

One of the cats explained, with an odd tone of compassionate patience:

“Like we said, when one is born, they are bound to have the date of death along with the date of birth. One can not start their life without a pre determined mark of when it ends: you don’t have one now.”

“The fact that yours was set to today and yet you lived proves that it was not intentional. It was wrong.”

The two nodded and went quiet. What more was there to say.

“So, until we figure out why your date is wrong, and what it really is, you can not go up,” the hound adjusted the lapels of his jacket. “You can not live unless you can die.”

“But sir, prolonged stay in the underworld is not recommended to the living, there are consequences.”

“Such are the rules, Sir.”

“I am aware, I made them.”

The dog rubbed his head with growing frustration that Noah almost felt bad for. How was he causing so much trouble by simply… being alive? He decided not to dwell on such deep thoughts. 

“Of course there are ways to bypass the rules, if we take inspiration elsewhere. There are stories of coexistence in the underworld of creatures from above and-”

“We aren’t doing that,” Noah grimaced at the thought. 

The cat shrugged. 

“Well, we still need to fill in your data before we decide what to do next, so you’ll have to stay at least one night.”

“But I have life above the ground, I can’t just vanish like this, and plus you just said I can’t stay here, or… well, something.”

“One night here won’t kill you.”

“I wish it would,” the hound’s comment was not received great.
