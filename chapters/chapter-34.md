It had definitely been over ten minutes since he had dragged the quickly packed kitchen knife over the palm of his hand, pressing it tight into the dusted fabrics of the garments covering the old dried out bones in the coffin. Trying not to look at first, he was still mesmerised by the crimson soaking through the old yellowed shirt that long fell into the crevices of the decaying body. 

Struggling to justify his own actions to himself, Henry tried to pull himself upwards, but his exhausted body, having bled out, only shook and slipped down lower onto the floor, a trail of blood following where the slash on his hand dropped onto a piece of coffin decoration. It hurt, but the pain only lightly echoed through his body, pushing a soft gasp out of Henry’s lips. 

He did this intrusively. 

The knife had cut through the flesh like butter, only pushing a little hiss out of him. He had held his hand over the body, closing his eyes to feel the way the man’s hands held his, small whispers of reassurance to tell him it would all be fine.

“This is how it was meant to be.”

Yes, Henry thought, they were meant to be together. Neither Death, nor Time themselves could keep them apart, and if the world had put Cecil’s loving words into Henry’s hands, Henry would show his love in return: with his hands bleeding the confessions onto the decaying body of his lover he missed by a century. 

“Life is something that can be nurtured back.”

If it takes his life to bring Cecil to him - he’d drain himself down to the last drop. 

Thinking about it, Henry closed his eyes, a smile unable to curve his purple lips. It was acceptance: the best outcome for him was giving himself to something more important than his life. He had been praying for a chance to hold that hand in his and feel the pulse of a beating heart under his fingertips. He had dreamt of the pleasure of hearing the voice he’d imagined so many times when reading the smallest little notes Cecil had left in the books of his private collection: tracked down by the historian over years.

But he was not selfish. 

Even if he could not live to see, to feel the touch, he’d die knowing he’d given himself to his love to the fullest.

A peaceful silence. An all encompassing darkness. A ruffling of fabric somewhere by his ear.
