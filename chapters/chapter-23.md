He was tormented by nightmares the whole night. From the dread of falling down and hitting his head in that cursed bread aisle, to passing out at the bar with Leo. From being strangled by his friend on the outskirts of the city where no one could help him, to having his head crashed against a wall by a god who was not even in a clear state of mind. 

He dreamt of his deaths that had happened and those that could yet claim him. 

In the night when Noah finally shook the sleep off, he sat in the bed leaning against the wall to cool his head off. He wasn’t afraid, he wasn’t shaken. He was perfectly calm, and that bothered him even more.

Thinking about how even the god of death wasn’t safe from nightmares, Noah stared at the window, watching the fake moon slowly drift among the dark clouds. What could a god be scared of? Was it some memory of the past or a threat to his future? Was it out of guilt and regret or due to something out of his control? 

Noah hated to sympathise with the deity after what the just got thought but touching the scratches on his face we couldn’t help but feel a touch of understanding towards the creature. 

For a moment a thought of knocking on the doors of that room in the dead of night came to him. Not because he wanted to know what the nightmare was, not because he could offer some kind of comfort to the being most distant from humans, but because the idea of having someone also tormented by their own mind by his side felt like a tempting idea. 

Still, he only shrank in his bed more and tried to calm himself into sleeping, inevitably giving up and an hour later, changing into his casual clothes and waiting for the first signs of daylight. Right before leaving he picked up the red uniform jacket and straightened it to be as neat as possible. There was no iron, but he made a do. 

Wandering a little around the palace, he walked into the ER, seeing the exact face he had been looking for, as if the Janitor was purposefully waiting there for him. 

“Good morning,” Noah approached with a smile of a man confident in all of his plans finally working out. “Now look what I have for you!”

He proudly presented the folded jacket to the Janitor:

“I found this while cleaning, and I thought it looked familiar, and it finally hit me later.”

The Janitor accepted the jacket, putting it on. As expected, it magically fit the shoulders twice the width of Noah’s. 

The Janitor didn’t say anything, but in the silence his eyes seemed to soften with gratitude. Noah tried not to think about it too much, and just acted as if there were no ulterior motives to his actions.

“I also went through all the work you assigned me,” he started, but was silenced when a massive hand pushed a freshly made cup of coffee towards him. While still conscious about the underworld food, he remembered what Ammit had said about drinks and graciously accepted it. Somehow having a simple cup of rather mediocre coffee right after waking up made him feel so alive, like a dormant longing for his old human habits calling back to him.

“Thank you.”

The Janitor only shook his head and pointed at Noah as if to say ‘no, thank you’.

What a sweet giant, Noah assessed. 

“Now, I was wondering if you could help me find the guard I was looking for. You know, the one that had quit suddenly. 

First the jacket, then a valuable employee - Noah decided against admitting his involvement with everything disappearing around the Janitor. 

The giant outstretched his arm as if asking for something, patiently waiting while Noah uncertainly looked around before patting his pocket and realising it was about the note. 


[          :)      ♥️    🚪❓🤝  🫂 🏠   ]


Before their escape, the guard added two more symbols, drawn poorly with a pencil. Two embracing figures and what Noah assumed to be a house.

“I have my reasons to believe they went to the outerworld, so I was thinking these symbols could be a hint to where they went.”

Once again, Noah did not consider this a lie - rather, simple withholding of information that was not necessary. 

The Janitor picked his small notebook and drew the same symbols himself, in a way that was definitely not coincidentally very alike the guard’s skills. 

“Did you teach it to communicate like this?”

The Janitor nodded finally opening his mouth:

“Selma.”




That was all he knew, that was all he needed. Noah sprinted to the cat’s office with the stupidest grin on his face. The Janitor didn’t talk much, and as he would struggle picking the right language in writing, Noah assumed he had difficulties with not blending them in all together. Considering he had been around the palace for centuries, also talking and taking in new guards, it was only natural to imagine the god picking a few languages too many. 

‘It is probably a name.’

Names were a thing that would go through languages mostly unchanged, so Noah grabbed onto that straw and rushed to the archive, already tasting the sweet taste of victory. 

All he had to do was get the information of how that Selma person died, get to the spot, find them and take them back. As easy as that. And, the final step - he would enjoy the bewildered look on the god’s face when he proudly shows him the “soulless” entity that clearly sought communication with people on its own.

‘Heh heh…’

He could only giggle to himself when thinking about how things would certainly start getting better finally.

He turned a corridor, just a short run away from the Cat’s office, in the right corridor when he hit full break on the way, noticing the tall familiar figure by the very door he was running to. 

Bad sign.

Noah was about to turn around and try to avoid Anubis since their odd interaction during the night, when the hound’s head turned towards him.

Considering he could still pretend not to notice, Noah turned around and started walking away.

“Noah.”

If he still pretended not to hear, surely the god would leave him be…

“Noah, get back here.”

Reluctantly, Noah turned and walked towards him, deciding to use his trump card - playing the role of a victim. His hand reached to touch the scratches on his face, eyes avoiding Anubis’.

“You called me?”

The god seemed reluctant to speak, clearly caught off guard by such behaviour. 

“Does it still hurt?”

“No, your remedy worked wonders, but…”

Noah shied away, on purpose. 

“But what?” Even the god’s tone softened with remorse.

“But I could not sleep the whole night thinking about… about… thinking about what happened.”

The god hissed through his teeth, clearly ready to say something along the lines of “you’ve literally been through worse”, but deciding against it. 

“I apologise.”

Finally, the conversation was fully under Noah’s control, so he turned around, with a triumphant grin on his face.

“It’s fine, I have excellent news for you.”

Seeing the immediate change in his face, Anubis felt like he fell for some stupidly obvious trick when Noah continued:

“I should be able to locate the escaped guard with no problem very soon.”

The god’s ears twitched in surprise.

“How come?”

“I got some help, from the Janitor.”

Anubis frowned.

“Have I not told you to be more cautious with the residents of this place?”

Reluctant to let Anubis have his way yet again, Noah shifted his gaze down, a small blush on his cheeks as he rubbed at the scratches:

“But you are a resident of this palace, and last night I trusted y-”

“Enough. I got the idea.”

Noah beamed at the sight of Anubis admitting his small defeats.

“Now, if you excuse me, I have some matters to tend to at the archive,” Noah tried to walk past the god to the room but a hand on his shoulder forced him to stop. 

“Aren’t you forgetting something?”

“If I forgot something, how would I know it if it’s forgotten?”

“Why do I think I was waiting here for you?”

Noah shut his mouth, mind racing through their last conversations trying to figure out if something was said he forgot.

“I will spare you the struggle. Last night I told you I have plans with you in the morning.”

“Ah. Right.”

Noah looked at the door bare three meters away from him and then at the hand on his shoulder. 

“Let me guess, it’s so urgent we have to go now.”





As it was very urgent, they left immediately. At the scales room, Anubis approached a door that seemed to have emerged purely on his request. When the two walked through gray fog similar to that of the gates, Noah coughed a little before taking in the new surroundings.

“Woah.”

The infinite bookshelves stretched beyond where he could see them, reaching the artificial sky and going above into the dark of the night sky where small oil lamps drifted slowly like stars.

“This is the eternal library,” the god explained as they walked. “There is all the knowledge humankind has ever accumulated, including unrecorded and lost fragments of its history.”

“So I can find absolutely anything and everything in here?”

The god nodded and Noah slipped a little to the side to look at book spines in languages he could not even recognise. 

“Don’t stray too far away. This place is not infinite, but it is so vast that even if I had dedicated my entire life to reading here, I would have barely scratched the surface.”

Noah was never much of a reader, but the idea of having access to absolutely everything all at once was making him a little giddy. 

“Are we here because you think there is some knowledge about my case here?”

“Not exactly. I made a few arrangements to show you something.”

The god paused as if unsure if he should add something. 

“But? Is there a but?”

“But I imagine it might be a bit hard for you.”

As unsettling as it was, Noah had to do what he had to do. In a way, after their talk at night, he wanted to see the world and what they were dealing with from Anubis’ perspecting a little more. Of course he could never understand how a god thinks, and be anywhere close to one, but he would take as much as was offered to him and listen to the explanations intently. 

“I can manage, show me.”

When they approached an empty desk in the centre of the infinite space, Anubis hit a small bell and without anyone appearing he spoke into the air:

“I am here for my reservation.”

Without any confirmation or anyone coming to assist, they moved further, soon coming to a door. 

“The library does not need staff, it maintains itself and shapes itself around the needs of the visitors.”

“Would that mean if I request something it would give it to me immediately?”

“Not exactly. To request something specific you need to know what you are looking for well enough to find your way to it. Let’s say you are looking for a book about gravity, but you don’t know the name of the term, nor the author of the concept. You have only an idea of an item falling down, and so you’d walk around circles for eternity because likely the majority of the tomes here would involve such a concept, albeit in the passing.”

“The sorting system here seems complex.”

“Only the library itself knows how it operates.”

They went through the door into a small secluded room where only a table and a bunch of boxes and jars were prepped for them.

“Are we here for a science experiment?”

“... Yes.”

Now Noah was concerned. 

Anubis only walked closer to the desk and beaconed him to follow, showing a big glass bowl set empty on the table. 

“I know you will feel conflicted, but this is something I thought would help you understand the fundamental law I have spoken of.”

Noah eyed the jars with suspicion, but he could only see clear colourful liquids. 

“What are we making?”

Anubis opened a couple boxes and showed him some powdered metals.

“A homunculus.”




At first Noah was about to slap him on the nose, or maybe even throw something at him at the suggestion, but he could not move because another force in him was overtaking the anger. 

Curiosity.

“But isn’t it prohibited? You said so yourself.”

“It is, and I want to show you why.”

It was an illogical chain of thought, but this could be Noah’s only chance to know: after all, who could judge a god for such a rule breaking experiment. 

“Alright, what do I do?”

Anubis guided him step by step, helping him measure powders and liquids down to a millimetre emphasising how every tiny mistake would lead to a failure. 

“Is this gold?”

There even was the tiniest crumb of gold in a separate spoon for him.

“A body is complex and unforgiving, easy to break and hard to recreate. And we aren’t even making a human.”

After two hours of calculations, Noah was ready to admit it was an insane process.

“If this is not a human, how complicated would a human be?”

Anubis didn’t answer and Noah understood the meaning well enough.


“Now comes the part I wanted to show you.”

The mixture in the bowl was close to sticky dough, unmoving and not even remotely close to a living being. 

“To give it life, life should be given in return as a moving force.”

Noah was about to protest when Anubis silenced him with an explanation:

“In this case we will use my essence as a substitute. As a god, my life force is eternal, so I have some to spare. Now let me see if I can find scissors…”

The hound was rummaging thought shelves, mumbling something with no result.

“Do you just need your DNA? Like a part of your body?”

“Yes. I was hoping to clip a bit of a nail, but it seems like-”

Noah pulled the sleeve of his hoodie over his hand, pressed it to the back of the god’s neck and rubbed hard, making the deity jump. 

“Here, your hair.”

Anubis stood staring at the small hairs on the fabric in shock. 

“Yes, I suppose that would do.”

Noah dusted his sleeve into the bowl, watching the black fur disappear into the dough as it shrank and bubbled up, as if boiling from the inside. It mutated, evolved, twisting and turning in a way that was so unnatural it made Noah feel repulsed. 

“Would having an essence of a god as a crafting material change anything?”

“That is what I wanted to show you.”

It was in that moment that the mixture stopped moving, settling down and only slightly expanding and shrinking again, as if-

“Is it breathing?”

It slowly darkened, and only as it dried up did Noah realise it was wet fur. 

“Oh my god…”

The creature, as small as an apple, was covered fully in rather thick messy fur. As it moved, Noah realized that it was suspiciously close in colour to his own hair.

“Oh…”

He looked at his jacket and noticed a few brown hairs here and there.

“Purely hypothetically,” he asked, “What does giving your DNA to a homunculus mean?”

The god looked at Noah and then back at the small brunette creature with a sigh.

“It will feel a certain level of affection towards you, as you two are bound. I hoped to avoid this so as to not cause you to form attachments, but it really isn’t the worst outcome.”

Noah stopped listening half way thought. 

It hit him.

“I am a father now.”

“For crying out loud, Noah, that isn’t the point!”

“It will see me as its father…”

Noah reached into the bowl and scooped the small thing placing it onto the desk. The fluffy thing with no distinct features adjusted on its chubby legs and looked up, tiny black button eyes staring at the two. 

“Ugh, it has your eyes,” Noah hated to admit.

The creature walked a step and tumbled over, unused to its mobility.

“Aw, it has your dexterity,” Anubis added.

The two watched the thing for a bit as it grew used to its body and even made a few small sounds.

“How long does it have to live?”

“With my essence, approximately 15 years.”

“Fifteen?!”

Somehow Noah expected a lifespan of a hamster, but this thing could pessimistically outlive him. 

“That is what I wanted you to learn, Noah.” The god’s voice turned solemn. “A creature of this small size would require an equivalent of energy proportionate to its body weight. If we assume it is a human, such energy would come from the very thing we associate with a soul.”

“A human heart?”

“Yes.”

So it would require one human heart to live.

“How long would it have then?”

“About three years at best.”

He had known Leo for a decade….

“Is the energy required… always proportional to the weight?”

“Weight and complexity of the structure.”

He didn’t want to know, but he had to.

“So how many lives would it take to craft someone like Leo, assuming he lives a decade?”

“By rough estimation…”

Noah could feel himself already regretting the question. 


“About 800.”

It was a hard truth to swallow, but Noah stared at the small thing on the desk unblinking.

“He didn’t do it himself.”

“I know, Noah.”

He gritted his teeth, mad that the god wasn’t even arguing with him. Gently he scooped the small creature into his hands. 

“I want to keep it. Can I keep it at the palace?”

Anubis eyed the thing and sighed. 

“As long as you keep an eye on it. It looks like it lacks intelligence anyway.”

“Now don’t be rude. It has your ancestry, surely I can teach it fetch or sit.”
