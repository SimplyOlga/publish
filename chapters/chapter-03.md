Dripping sound of water hitting metal. Darkness, cold. Noah held his breath, feeling like the walls around him were narrowing down, squishing him tighter, tighter, pushing air out of his lungs into the stuffy space he was about to suffocate in, when- 

“Here you are!”

The doors opened, his body tumbling into the light so bright he had to squint after staying locked up for what felt like ages.

“Sorry, sweetheart, we had no idea just where you’d be dropping! That does happen when you make a system and write a rulebook for something that ends up never happening.”

He looked up, seeing a silhouette leaning above him, a hand offered to pull him up. 

“Not until now that is!”

That perked up voice he had heard over the phone slipped an uncontained smile through the greeting. 

“Look at you! Really, what a peculiar predicament!”

She pushed him upwards, and finally, Noah took a look around the room: a round table, a counter with a coffee pot, a worn out couch, a dripping faucet measuring the time with every drop against the metal sink. All that a normal office break room would have, was it not inside a cave. 

A cold, natural cave, lit up by torches for the lack of better lighting. 

“So, what’s the name?”

The cat spoke.

Spoke.

Jumping to the side, he pushed himself into a wall, away from the woman who tilted her feline head to the side, amused by the act. Her bright green eyes stared at him, crescent in the curiosity she was openly letting out. 

“If you don’t want to say the name it’s fine. We have it anyway, Noah.”

“Oo…kay…”

He walked pressed against the cold stone wall to the side, keeping distance from the beast, measuring his steps over the tiled floor towards the door in the corner. Slowly, without provoking them…

“You have any hobbies?”

He jumped. 

“It’s a little sad how little we get to know about people, and if you come here as his mistake, surely you got a story or two to tell,” another cat, identical to the first in every way but the small white dot on the right ear, slipped past him studying his every move. “Come now, are you a murderer? A monarch? Are you cursed?”

“I am neither?”

The cats both frowned. 

“Alright, keep the fun to yourself.” 

Finally reaching the door, Noah twisted the handle and felt the door slightly swing open. 

“Um, I would not go yet, we haven’t done the cleaning in the corridor yet.”

A low growl echoed through the corridor and Noah slowly pushed the door closed, turning a small lock on the handle. 

“He has no appointment,” the white dotted cat stared at the papers in her hands.

“Of course he doesn’t, he never made one.”

“So, do we wait here, or…”

“I am sorry,” Noah finally caught his breath and straightened his posture. If anything, years of going through paperwork had taught him just how to handle bureaucratic hell. Potentially, literal. “But where am I?”

“Didn’t I say it over the phone? This is ER.”

“Emergency room?”

“Well, it is a room and you are an emergency,” the fully black cat shrug, while the other chimed in:

“We tried to get a better room, but since in thousands of years the hotline had not been called once, every couple centuries they freshly denied our requests.”

“T-thousands?”

How was hearing about thousands of years more shocking than talking to cats was beyond him, but Noah still pushed:

“So, why am I here? I don’t need an ER, I am not hurt. I mean,” he thought back to his day, “I did hurt my head back then, but… Wait, am I in a coma?”

“No, like you said, you are not hurt.”

“Then why am I in an ER?”

“Because you are dead.”

He felt some flaws in the logic. Mostly because he, as far as he was aware, was standing there quite well and alive. 

“So, what now?”

The two looked at each other. 

“We are not quite sure, but what we could do is send you to him.”

At the mention of him the two giggled.

“He messed up, unbelievable.”

“Seriously, undeniably messed up.”

The two grinned, cat ear to cat ear. Noah felt like whoever he was, he had no desire to meet him. He eyed the door which led to the “uncleaned” corridor, making his mind.

“If I meet him, would… he tell me what happened?”

The cats set the papers aside and gestured to the wall across the classic office door. Only then did Noah see, in the light of the torches, an intricate stone door, almost invisible in the general setting. 

“Well,” the first cat bowed, offering him to go. “He will try.”

“And we would love to see that,” the second followed up, with a grin.




They led him through a long corridor where torches lit up their path and intricate golden patterns adorned the walls between massive murals. Noah looked up, realising he could not see the ceiling in the dark, swallowing hard at the creeping feeling of just how small he was in that place. The cats walked fast, with a spring to their feet, as if in a rush to get to him, excitement in every word: 

“No, no, you can look at these later, no time, we should hurry.”

They wouldn’t let him look at the murals properly, but he could make out a few silhouettes of humans and what looked like giants, their shapes distorted and leading all the way to the dark of the upper levels where he could not make out their faces. If they had any to begin with. 

“So, if I may, who is this he you are talking about?”

The cats looked at each other with content grins.

“Our boss.”

That clarified nothing. 

“And what does your business do?”

“Well, we mostly handle all the boring stuff, he does all the fun things.”
“But today,” the cat with a spot paused as they reached a massive door at the end of the corridor, “He won’t be able to kick us out.”

“After all, we will be the one to fill out the report for his mistakes.”


The massive door decorated with golden lines that formed leaves and flowers unbefitting such a dark gloomy place, shifted open, slowly revealing a massive hall behind. The dark marble pillars supported chandeliers that shone brightly to enhance the grandeur of the place, a high dome ceiling streamed trails of water that glimmered in the light, falling down by the walls, twisting defying gravity. And in the centre, high on a pedestal, stood a massive throne, one befitting a king in dark RPG games Noah used to play. 

‘Is this all a set?’

He really doubted his own eyes, seeing how the furniture around the room must have really been at least twice the size of the regular.

“Sir! We need your presence, sir!”

Her voice echoed around the room, loud and disruptive of the peace around.

He could not hear who replied, only a thundering voice roared through the space, falling down on him in a way that made his knees shiver:

“I don’t believe I have any appointments with you today. Leave.”

He was about to turn and leave quietly and eagerly when the cat with a white spot shook her head, her hands pushing the doors closed, cutting his escape. 

“No, it’s rather urgent.”

“A once in a lifetime kind of situation,” the other added and the women giggled. 

“Enough!”

He heard the sound of fabric, the sound of shoes on the stone floor. Massive, whoever it was was massive. 

“I think I am fine with waiting a bit,” he leaned to whisper into the worker’s ear that turned to him before she did.

“No, no, today is a special day. Sir, we believe you made a mistake.”

The steps stopped. 

“A mistake?”

The voice shook, just a little, as if offended by the sheer implication.

“Me?”

“You, sir.”

The lights moved, the shadows danced, and from behind a pillar a massive figure emerged: tall, lean and dressed in black. 

“I never make mistakes.”

The black dog head turned to Noah.
