Noah felt like shrinking into a small grain of sand, never to be seen again. 

The hand on his shoulder patted him lightly a couple times before the familiar voice once again came through:

“How lucky I am. What a funny coincidence to run into you here, Noah.”

Once again, Noah barely felt a slither of luck. In the corner of his eye he saw a long dark woolen coat and a pale hand. No fur. 

“Indeed, how come you are here?”

After mastering the courage to speak, he looked at Leo who frowned with an odd sense of unease and displeasure, eyes locked on the hand on Noah’s shoulder. 

“Why, it is a funny story, albeit a long one.”

A hand reached out to grab onto the back of a chair at their table before Leo pushed it away towards himself. Now it was impossible not to acknowledge the anger in his actions, that only got a little chuckle out of the taller guest. 

Noah took a sip of his beer and swallowed hard, praying the alcohol would kick in quickly so he could let it take the wheel, tucking common sense and reason away until tomorrow morning. 

“Noah, do you know this man?”



How did it come to this?



It was seven years ago that Noah stood at the campus steps, hesitant to walk in. It was two weeks later that he made his first friend, thinking that life was finally becoming as normal as one would expect it to be. 

Campus life. Friends. Homework. Parties. A crush, a heartbreak, comfort, more parties, more homework. Normal life. Nothing out of the ordinary. Nothing weird. Not a thing. 

A life right out of a movie. A normal movie. 

He cherished those moments, he grew attached to them, he had let them become a part of himself: an undeniable part of all the normal things that had made Noah who Noah was. 

And Leo was a part of it. 

A man with notes from the classes Noah had skipped due to illness. A friend who would introduce him to girls only to later buy him drinks whenever he would get rejected. A partner in crime, a friend, a comfort, a concept of belonging. 

A normal part of his normal life. 




“Noah, do you know this man?”


A little hiss that betrayed anger, an unreasonable display of disdain only got a small chuckle out of the man behind Noah. 

“Do you, Noah?”

Less than anything in the world did he want to be in that place. He nodded. 

“He is a friend.”

“An acquaintance,” Anubis corrected. “A distant one.”

Leo looked like he was about to stand up, reminding Noah of the times he would get in bar fights. 

“This is a friendly meetup, apologies, but since I don’t seem to know you, I would like to ask you to leave your business with Noah for some other time.”

Even Noah, accounting for all his time in the underworld and above would not imagine talking to the God of Death with such open hostility. 

Hesitantly, he tried to look up, to see if the hound was calm enough, to see his face, but the moment he tried to turn, the hand on his shoulder pressed hard, pinning him in his seat up straight. 

“You don’t know me? Well, that just isn’t true, is it?”

A hint of confusion flashed through Leo’s face, immediately swapped back into irritation. 

“I have no idea who you are. Have we met before?”

“Ah, no, not yet. But you are wrong to assume you don’t know me.”

At the sound of a smile in the voice of the god Noah felt uneasy. Surely he wasn’t going to do anything… godly right there and then?

“In fact, everyone in this room knows me, everyone in this building knows who I am,” a little devilish grin came through the way he stretched the vocals, as if singing a praise to himself. “They just haven’t met me yet.”

“What-”

It was Emmy’s voice that cut the tension like butter over a block of butter. Cold, stone hard, greasy butter, that had already left an unpleasant aftertaste in Noah’s mouth. 

“What are you all gossiping about?” She winked at Leo, forcing him to loosen the grip on the chair he was still holding away from Anubis. “I came at the right time, no?” 

She pulled her hand towards Anubis, offering a handshake he reciprocated. 

“A pleasure to meet one of Noah’s friends.”

“Aquantances,” Noah commented, feeling like it was only going to get worse from here, so he pulled at the god’s sleeve. “Could I have a word?”

“Noah!”

Leo almost let out a cuss in a way Noah had never seen him do before. Whatever the frustration boiling in him was - it was highly unnatural, so Noah only nudged Anubis to step aside with him again, meeting stubborn but predictable resistance. 

“I am eager to meet more of your friends, Noah.”

‘You-’

No one would want to introduce their friends to literal personification of death, and Noah was not an exception. 

“We will talk about this at home,” he hissed, meeting with Emmy’s eyes as she smiled. Only then did he notice a face he had already seen before, something clicking in his mind.

The man Nephele had been trailing earlier peeked from behind Emmy’s shoulder, accessing the situation, eyes darting between the three men with an odd tint of… embarrassment and infatuation. 

Noah groaned on the inside, taking a deep breath.

Whatever was happening was weird and started the moment someone got too tired to sit outside. He swallowed a curse and probed:

“I thought you were busy tonight,” he asked while giving a fake smile to Emmy, who was delighted to see him make a new friend. She had always had too much concern over his small social circle consisting mainly of them two. 

“Oh, not at all. I was going to go out for a drink, but it turns out my companion stood me up for another date.”

Deep breaths. 

Noah exhaled through the nose, noting three things he could smell and three things he could see. Alcohol, tobacco, sweat. His own shaking fingers, Emmy’s smile, the tint of red on the face of her companion.

Deep breath. 

“But I am glad to find you here, and in such a wonderful company, none the less.”

He felt like the tension was about to snap. The weird mix of emotions around them - anger, desire, pride, fear - all cranked up to the maximum, was boiling up about to explode. 

He wondered who would be the first to snap. 

Leo’s fingers curled into a fist, knuckles white. Emmy’s patronising gaze on Noah felt more maternal than that of a friend: a mix of care and pride, tender love. 

It was someone else. 

A declaration of love. A call of passion. A reach out of desperation. 

“I have been looking for you for so long!”

He heard the unfamiliar voice, he saw the way the stranger’s face betrayed a smile of crazed obsession, he followed in the way those eyes behind glasses looked, but as he turned, his head grew heavy, his eyes blurry, and the world around spinned like a carousselle until there was no more. 

He was swallowed by the dark. 

His eyes closed and his limp body dropped onto the table knocking over the mugs. 




This time no one was greeting him, so he sat down on the couch in the ER for a bit, contemplating taking some coffee before he remembered the myths about eating food not meant for humans. Thinking back to the fruits in his room, Noah stood up and decided to take matters into his own hands. 

He was, without a doubt, dead again. 

After a few turns, navigating by murals, he knocked at the massive doors to the scales room. No one responded, but slowly the doors opened, letting him back into the dimly lit grand place. 

“Care to explain?”

He had no desire to stall, watching the way the massive figure turned to look at him, the reflection of the torches in his eyes making the god look ominously mischievous. 

“Whaever is it that you struggle to understand?”

“Why am I here?”

“Why do people end up here?”

Conversations in circles.

“Did you just… kill me?”

The god nodded.

“In front of my friends?!”

He chuckled, pushing a groan of frustration out of the human in front of him. Noah tried to calm himself down, looking at the scales behind the hound.

“So, now what? Am I dead? Like, actually now?”

The tall figure, standing by the side of the massive golden mechanism, trailed his fingers over the ornate details on the plates that stood propped against pillars. The touch, quick and familiar, felt full of tender intimate connection one only develops after centuries and millennia of working together. 

Noah almost felt ashamed for what he caused. Almost, but not really. 

“What happened?”

“I stopped your heart.”

Said with ease, the words made him shiver, going back to the feeling of anxiety he had felt ever since the “day of his death”.

“I will send you back in a moment, but I had to make this decision, for the safety of us both.”

The serious tone of the god made Noah listen, putting his frustration aside. 

“Why?”

With the quiet sound of heels on the stone floor, the figure drew closer. 

“All I can say is that you have quite peculiar friends, Noah. Perhaps, your case is a little more elaborate than even I imagined.”

Noah opened his mouth, about to question, when the unfortunately familiar feeling of a hand inside his chest pushed air out of his lungs. He wanted to curse the god for his lack of any tact when it came to shoving his hands into someone’s ribcage. 

Anubis pulled his unbeaten heart out and examined it with an unimpressed look on the already emotionless face, clearly ignoring the way Noah took in a harsh breath, holding onto the unnatural wound on his chest. Somehow, this time he took it better, and the thought of getting used to this only made him glare at the god harder. 

His legs swayed and he almost fell, a hand holding him in place, supporting. 

“Well…” Noah felt like spitting poison. “You have a spare scale?”

He could already feel his fingers numbing. 

“I don’t need one,” the hound squished the heart in his fingers, tight. “A human is not ready to be judged when they still possess what defines them as living.”

“And what is that?”
 
Noah watched as the god bared his teeth, bringing the heat to his face, jaw opening wide, pitch black saliva dropping on the floor. 

“The will.”

He didn’t need to ask ‘the will for what’. He simply dashed, pulling all his remaining strength. He thrashed in the inhumanly strong hold and kicked, hands reaching up, shaking feverishly with the burning desire to live. 

To persevere. 

To escape the literal claws of death. 

Watching his terrified face, the way Noah’s eyes shot wide with uncontrollable natural dread one must feel in the presence of Death, the god laughed, low and hearty, teeth clutching around the organ.

Noah felt his mind scream the moment they grazed over the surface, light, barely there, closing with a clank to the side: a dirty trick. 

Noah watched the way his heart hectically beat in the god’s hand, alive and screaming about it with every hectic, almost painful contraction.

Noah swallowed, feeling his back soaked with sweat. 

“What… What was that?”

Even with his heart torn out, he felt more alive than before. 

“It is your will, and until it is dimmed and extinguished, I will not have you anywhere near my scales.”

With force, Anubis pushed his heart back into the ribcage and allowed him a moment to breathe. 

“In the realm of the living only a moment has passed, you will be fine.”

Noah still had no idea why he did all this, but as he pressed his hand against his chest to feel the beat inside, he almost felt like he could trust the god’s judgement. 

*For their safety.*





With a harsh breath, Noah sat up, watching the people around him take a step back, making a bit of space. 

“Noah!”

Even before his vision returned to him, he heard Emmy scream with worry. He looked at her, recognising that half anxious half pissed expression she always gave him when he would do something stupid. 

This time it wasn’t on him, though. He took a breath and felt the obnoxiously vivid and yet welcome sense of his own heart beating in his chest. 

No matter how many times he had died, it was still unpleasant. 

He felt her prop him with her hand, helping him sit up straight, offering a glass of water. 

“What the hell happened to you?!”

The people gathered around them were starting to slowly walk away, annoyed or relieved to see him up and fine. Someone murmured something along the lines of lightweights being lightweights. 

“I am… Well…”

Anubis never gave him time to think about an excuse, so he just opened his mouth like a fish, trying to come up with something when a voice spoke for him:

“A light stroke, closer to something between passing out and entering a quick coma.” 

Emmy stared at his companion, her eyes full of worry. 

“A coma?”

“It is similar to asphyxia, but not strong enough for cerebral hypoxia.”

Emmy nodded, while Noah chugged down his glass of water, feeling his mind clearing up.

“I am sorry, but are you… a doctor?”

“Yes,” Anubis smiled. “I am his cardiologist.”

Ignoring the remark, Noah looked at Emmy, reassuring her he was fine. 

“I am sorry, it does happen sometimes, but I was told it is nothing to worry about.”

He himself was still worried. 

“My god, Noah, we thought you died.”

“No need to worry about that,” Anubis helped Noah to get up, patting dust off his back where he laid on the floor, hard, unreasonably hard. 

“Leo and I were about to call the ambulance when you woke up, oh god, Noah!”

She gave him a tight hug, but all Noah could see was the frowned expression on Anubis’ face. 

He looked around, realising something.

“Emmy, where did Leo go?”
