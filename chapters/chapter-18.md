Noah reaches up to his throat, the feeling of the knives slashing into the artery still fresh, a tingling sensation causing him to tense, afraid to move. It was over. He was safe. He was alive.

He just managed to-

He didn’t want to think about it. 

Stumbling on his feet, he stretched, trying to feel every muscle in his body working properly. Admitting everything was working fine, he disregarded the blood stains all over his clothes and passed by the coffee pot, the kitchen counters and the familiar couch of the ER he was now quite familiar with. 

‘It worked.’

He tried not to think about what would have happened had it not worked… 

Getting a hold of his racing mind, he walked towards the door and leaned in against it to listen: there seemed to be no one on the other side, so he slipped into the corridor, picking up a random jacket that was left hanging on the wall in the otherwise never used room. Walking through the corridors, hiding in the shadows in case anyone could walk by, he covered his disheveled state with the dark red jacket that looked like it was someone’s worn out uniform, and went on tracing his way back to the lower levels. Noah knew there would likely be guards there, but right at that moment it didn’t matter - he managed to escape. The locked door that Anubis thought would stop him was nothing when it came to the resolve he had in him to make things right. 

To make it right with Leo.

The second thing he noticed was the way the man’s face almost dripped down, skin sliding down his skull in an unnatural way, tearing on the sides exposing threads that held it together. 

“Ah. Apologies.”

Only then did Noah realise he involuntarily grimaced, for the person in front of him to pick at the threads around his chin and pull hard, the entire face coming back in one piece, as if nothing had happened. 

‘Fuck me...’

It was a grotesque sight, but Noah only straightened his face realising he had bigger issues on his plate. What was he supposed to do…

“You are late, but not like we are in a hurry.” 

The man gestured to follow him with a wave, and Noah stood reluctant, accepting only because they were going in the right direction. 

“You will need to pack some material, and try to clean up the scene quickly, I have a lot to still work on.”

Noah nodded, looking around counting doors, feeling like they were close to Leo’s cell. Suspiciously close. He looked back at the man in front of him, nervewrecking thoughts swarming in his head. 

Who was this man? 

Before he could act irrationally, feeling his hands clench into fists, the man stopped and rummaged through his pockets, pulling out a big bundle of keys. 

“Ok, will 20 minutes be enough?”

Noah stood there bewildered. This could not be so easy, could it? The man was already unlocking the very door he was looking for. Noticing his silent stupor, the researcher mumbled something before agreeing: 

“Fine, yes, it’s in a rather bad state, so… I will give you 40. No more!”

Grumbling something under his breath, he waved at Noah to get to work and closed the door behind him, disappearing somewhere deeper into the palace levels. 

Noah took a deep breath, the familiar stench of blood, burning flesh and his own vomit making his stomach curl, but he swallowed through it, walking up right to the table that still held the familiar figure. 

“Leo.”

The man opened his eyes, slowly, but immediately lit up seeing the face of his friend. 

“What are you doing here?”

“Take a guess!” Noah hissed, pulling at the leather belts to loosen them. “I am getting you out of here!”

Leo froze before smiling, the warm comforting laugh escaping him.

“You never change, do you?”

Noah shushed him, afraid anyone could walk in on them any moment. As soon as he got one of Leo’s hands out, they both got to work, freeing him a lot quicker. 

“Are you sure you want to risk it?” Leo asked, rubbing at his wrists. 

Noah watched the way the man ignored the open wound a size of a bowling ball in his own chest, replying confidently: 

“You got into this mess because of me.”

Leo paused, an odd emotion flashing on his face as he thought about the poison with which the words were directed at someone else not to be mentioned. Leo smiled. 

“Well then, let’s get the hell out of here, pal.”

That was easier said than done. Watching the taller man stand up with a hole in his chest, Noah bit on his tongue. That would be a lot harder to hide, both in the underworld and above.

“Can you really just… leave like this?”


It seemed like only then did Leo finally realise what was amiss, touching the edges of his exposed chest. 

“I feel fine but from experience, there might be issues above the ground. I think this place is keeping me in stasis, but it might get nastier above the ground.”

Noah did not want to think what the “experience” Leo mentioned was. Noticing the concerned look on his face, Leo laughed:

“Don’t worry, I won’t die from this. But it would be best if we closed this first.”

Then Noah wanted to scream out of frustration. What did he expect from him even? 

Leo jumped down from the operation table and started rummaging through boxes and glass bottles on the table before picking up a few small metal chests that were sealed with wax.

“They were going to ship them out today, that guy mentioned someone coming to pick them up. Noah, you are one lucky son of a bitch.”

Somehow thinking about all the lucky coincidence he had been having made him uneasy, but watching Leo’s fingers break the seal he could not resist walking closer to look inside to see a beating heart. 

“This should be enough.”

But Noah could not look away: it was small, unreasonably small, he could fit it in his one hand. It kept beating while locked away in a dry box, every palpitation strained and unnatural. But most of all, it was the colour and the texture of it - it was not made of flesh, but of something… almost liquid. The smooth surface reminded him of droplets of quicksilver, gathered together to resemble a shape of something it could only vaguely imagine.  

“Let’s not leave this behind,” Noah finally found his voice, thinking of the time Anubis’ jaw almost tightened around his own heart. The memory sent a weird feeling over his body, and he closed his eyes trying to shake it off. “No, let’s put it back in.”

Leo smiled, watching the way his friend took the box with his shaking hands. 

“What do I do?”

He had never performed an open heart surgery, so naturally a few tips and tricks were in order. He was about to rummage through the books left behind by the researcher, when Leo’s hand pulled him back, gently. 

Knowingly. 

“It won’t be too bad, let me guide you.”

Noah stared at him with eyes wide open. Thinking back to the “experiences” Leo had mentioned, he wondered just what had the man done to himself before in attempts to understand what it was that made him different. 

“Have you done this before?” Noah asked, hoping to not get an answer. Leo only smiled. 

“Here, take it and put it in, it would be easier if you do it since there is no mirror here.”

Holding the small heart in his hand, he felt how cold it was, compared to the time he felt his own beating heart put back into his chest by Anubis. His felt hot, living, beating. Leo’s was… an imitation. 

“Yes, align the pipes upwards, it only needs to vaguely connect.”

Noah followed, hand slowly diving into the open chest to try aligning it with where the lungs should be, only wondering how it would even connect. 

“How do I conn-”

He could not finish the thought when the flesh above his hand moved, growing something akin to hyphae to connect to the heart that allowed the tendrils to slip right into it, securing the organ in place. 

Noah wanted to curse when the small parts brushed against his fingers, but held it in, pulling out to watch the heart stay in place. 

“Yes, that feels better,” Leo took a deep breath, as if finally able to breathe. 

Noah wiped his hand over his borrowed jacket. 

“What about the wound?”

Leo touched around the edges and thought for a moment.

“There is some spare flesh in those jars, can you give me some, and you see the tin on the table?”

Noah followed his gaze and saw a small sewing kit left behind by the man with stitches.

Handing it to Leo, he closed his eyes, feeling like passing out at the sounds of what he did not dare imagine.
