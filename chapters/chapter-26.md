By the time the “sun” rose, he had not had a wink of sleep. He shifts on the bed, feeling like shit, staring at a piece of paper with a name written on it - a name he is afraid to forget if he allows the sleep to take him for even a moment. 

After a while, Noah finally crawls out of the bed and changes, hesitating at the door. What was he even about to do? What could he do?

Whatever it would be, he had to do something, so Noah opens the door and mindlessly walks to a place he remembers well. 



The Emergency Room door opens quietly and he looks around in hopes to see the one he is looking for, but instead a quiet squeaky sound grows closer as the small creature rushes towards him.

“Oh there you are, I am sorry I left you for…” He had no idea how long it had been in the underworld. “For some time…”

The creature ran circles around him until Noah picked it up and ruffled its hair. 

‘I should really give it a name…’

Looking around he saw that the room had new additions to the simple boring kitchenette: a makeshift ladder was added to the wall leading to a small structure consisting of ledges, hiding spots and soft beds all over the walls. 

A whole playground for the creature to explore. 

“Wow, did the Janitor make this for you?”

The creature only let out a sound resembling a bark. 

 With newfound appreciation, Noah set the creature on the counter and made some coffee, deciding on waiting for the arrival of the god, killing time by braiding the creature's fur. 

About an hour later the doors finally opened and the creature jumped down to run circles around its second favourite person. Somehow, Noah even felt an itch of betrayal he quickly pushed to the side. 

“I made you some coffee, but it is probably cold by now,” he said, watching the giant take a cup regardless, picking up the creature to settle it on one of the wall structure beds. 

Unsure how to bring up what he came there to say, Noah watched the gentle giant play with the creature for as long as seemed appropriate. 

“I met Selma.”

The god looked at him before everting his eyes in a silent understanding.

“I am sorry, I could not bring her back.”

The Janitor shook his head a mumbled a few words, as if trying to pick the right language before settling on one simple word:

“Frid.”

Noah had no idea what it meant, but watching the way the Janitor’s face eased into a calm smile, he realised that whatever bond the two shared also extended to keeping the last goodbyes in each other’s hearts. 

He wanted to say he hoped the woman would meet her child in the afterlife, but too much uncertainty weighed on him. 

If Selma never accepted the judgement, what would become of her soul?

If her child was a deserter who had abandoned his mother and land, would his heart pass the judgement of the scales?

Where were the two of them now? 

What was there, after the judgement? What could he hope for, for them, for himself, for Leo…

The creature barked, jumping on his lap to distract him from the all consuming void of his thoughts. 

“I know you are short on manpower, but I will do my best to help.”

The Janitor nodded, putting the cold cup of coffee into the microwave. 





“What is it doing here?”

Anubis eyed the creature in Noah’s arms with unhidden displeasure. 

“He is as much your creature as he is mine, take responsibility.”

Noah set the pup on the desk in Anubis’ room and found himself a chair to drag closer. 

“Alright, what are you doing here then?”

“Well, you are sort of also responsible for me.”

Before the god could let out a sigh of growing frustration, Noah went straight to the point:

“I have a question and a request. And I know you will get annoyed with both, so I ask you to be patient, and in return I will be more compliant in reaching a compromise.”

Clearly set aback by such a diplomatic approach, Anubis listened and instinctively nodded, not paying attention to the growing amount of inky paw prints on his paperwork. 

“Let’s hear the question first.”

“What would you say to me asking you to let me take…” he struggled to recall the name and looked at the marker writing on his wrist. “Leo upstairs, to the realm of the living.”

“I would say it’s a bad idea.”

“Elaborate.”

“You requested only one question.”

“It’s not a question, but a request for elaboration. A polite request. Please, and all that.”

Anubis took the papers off the desk, pushing the creature to the side. 

“He is property of someone else. The same way this thing- Did you even name it? Fine. The same way this thing seeks you out, is the same way your friend will inevitably be compliant and biased towards his creator.” 

“You think he will try something?”

“It isn’t outside of the realm of possibility. As long as we know so little about his purpose, it’s simply irrational to allow him to go there. Especially with you.”

“But here he will disappear!”

“In there, there is a chance he will be deemed a failure, and taken.”

Noah shut his mouth, thinking back to the time Leo cried to him: 

“Don’t give me away.”

“They want me to die. I don’t want to disappear.” 

Anubis stood up and poured a glass of water from a pitcher to his side. 

“Now what about the request I will inevitably reject?”

Noah had no time to bicker, so he got to the point:

“I want to go upstairs and talk to my friend.”

Handing him the glass, Anubis went into a deep contemplation. 

“The one from the bar?”

“You remember her?”


Somehow that made Noah feel uneasy. He would not like the God of Death having his eyes on yet another of his friends. 

“I told you before, you make peculiar friendships-”

“Oh, for crying out loud, spit it out, won’t you?!” Noah smashed the glass into the desk and picked the started creature for moral support. “What is she? A vampire? A ghoul? A zombie?”

“Don’t let your imagination run so wild. She is human, very much so.”

Noah squinted, sensing a “but”.

“But?”

“She simply doesn’t tell you some things about herself, and I find it amusing how uneven your relationships are in such a department.”

Noah could not understand that. What could she be hiding from him that was of importance even the God of Death could sense after meeting her once. He wanted to know, but prying into her affairs didn’t seem fair…

“She has the right to be who she wants to be in my eyes. Regardless, this isn’t about her or me, it’s about…”

“Leo?”

“Leo. It’s about Leo.” He ruffled the fluff to calm himself down. “She has no idea what happened, and she deserves to know.”

The god crossed his arms and Noah could see where this was going.

“And what will you tell her?”

“I don’t know… I assume she won’t believe the truth. But at least, I want to know what she thinks, I… need to be there for her.”

Anubis eyed him, as if waiting if the man had more to say before agreeing: 

“Fine, you can go. I will escort you.”

“Can I not go alone?”


“You want to thin down the campus population even more?”

“Ok, I will be ready in 10.”







Nephele tagged along, reminding Noah of his debt before running to the grocery store in preparation for their “cozy family meal” as she called it. 

Noah sat on a campus bench with the hound by his side on the grass. 

“Purely theoretically, if she was to believe in the whole homunculus part, would-”

He did finish, the quiet growl bringing him back to rehearsing his everchanging speeches. 

“Noah?”

She noticed him first, walking up to the bench and grabbing him into a tight embrace before he could even say anything. 

“Emmy, hi. I was-”

“Where the fuck have you been?!”

She shook him, staring into his face with red eyes. 

“Did you have an incident again?”

Reluctantly, Noah nodded, building up a start of a long chain of lies.

“The trip messed up my medication, so I took a couple days at the hospital.”

Her grip on his shoulders softened, and Emmy’s eyes turned from red with dread to red with sorrow. 

“Noah… I didn’t know what to do…”

It was his turn to grip her tight. 

“You both just vanished on me, I had no clue what to do, I spent nights calling you both, calling everyone I could.”

He felt like shit listening to the sobs of his friend. 

“Where is Leo, Noah? Where did he go? Do you have any idea?”

Biting his lip, Noah thought of the god next to him listening to his every word. 

“I don’t know…” He wanted to tell her the truth, but none of it made sense, until he finally found a way to be honest at least a little: “I am so scared, Emmy. I am so, so afraid.”



She told him that she had contacted the police and the search for Leo is ongoing, and Noah promised to stay in touch, while warning her he could be taking time to relax at the hospital. Knowing his previous medical records, she reluctantly agreed, not letting go of his hand even for a moment as they sat there letting people pass. 

“I feel like the world is slowly going crazy, Noah.”

She stared blankly at the building she once dreamt of teaching in, and her empty gaze reminded Noah of how Selma stared at her home long gone, waiting for a miracle that was her last remaining straw of life.

“Everyone around me seems… crazy? I don’t even know what to say!”

She looked at him before letting out a nervous chuckle.

“Three of my colleagues, older men, died last week. What a crazy fucking week. It’s like death is looming over us…”

Noah swallowed, hearing the hound next to him shift on his spot. 

“I am afraid, what if… What if Leo…”

She squeezed his hand so tight he had to shake her a little. 

“Leo isn’t dead, Emmy.”

At least he was glad he could still recall his name.

She nodded, her face hardening. 

“Yes. Fuck, this was stupid of me. What an asshole would say such a thing…”

He could not blame her. He only wished he could tell her the truth, but the god’s words came haunting him. Emmy was hiding something from him, and it left a new wave of bitterness in his mouth. 

“Emmy.”

“What is it?”


“I am here for you, you know that, right?”

She was on the verge of tears. 

“Of course. And I am here for you.”

How he wished he could just tell her, could just ask her… He was so tired of all the secrets they both had to keep from each other. 

“Noah, this dog should really be leashed.”

She chuckled at the way Anubis looked away, pissed. 

“Is he yours?”


“My roommate’s.”

“He’s cute.”

She reached out to pet him, and Noah prayed to one specific god she would not lose her fingers. The hound reluctantly let her. 

“Right, I need to get back to the mess that the university is in.” She stood up and straightened her coat. “With half the staff gone, it’s a mess.”

She promised to keep him informed and squeezed a promise out of him to stay in touch as much as he could, giving him one last hug before running back to the campus. 

“I don’t care what she isn’t telling me.”

The hound looked up at him.

“I have no right to judge her where I am now. Let’s go home.”

He picked the leash Anubis was holding in his teeth for him and together they walked away. Away from the hustle, from the people who had little to care about the affairs of the undead and away from the one person who watched the two friends with an uneasy feeling. 

Henry saw the two part ways and eyed Noah with a bitter feeling of betrayal. 

He remembered the man from before he lost his consciousness there at the cemetery. He knew Noah held a lot of secrets Henry wished he could pry out of him. Instead, his eyes shifted to the woman walking to the main building in a rush. 

He caught up and called out to her. 

“Sorry, you are Emmy, right?”

Henry knew a smile would not fit the occasion, so he let his face sink into a naturally grim expression he had been carrying lately. 

“You are… Oh, an assistant from the archaeological department, right? Henry, was it?”

He nodded, only growing more irritated that she remembered him. 

Remembered and yet never told him anything about that encounter. About that man. 

“I heard what happened to professor Verrone, you must still be in shock…”

Henry nodded. In truth, he did not care one bit.

“The place’s a mess, right?”

They had to bond over something, he knew they had to take it slow, he could not rush… Every part of his common sense told him he should not rush, and yet-

“I heard about your friend. I am sorry too.”

Emmy shut her mouth, her brows twitching as the same expression came back to her face: a look of someone who’d been holding back tears for too long. 

“I am sorry,” Henry continued. “This isn’t my place to speak, I did not know him aside from that one time we met a week ago. But… he reminded me of someone whom I hold very dear, so when I heard the news, it…”

Emmy just nodded, like she was unable to listen to more of it. 

“He will be back, I promise you.”

That is a promise he was ready to make. That was a promise he would see to the end.
