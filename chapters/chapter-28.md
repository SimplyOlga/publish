He laid out a couple new outfits and forced the God to sit in the chair as he presented them. 

“Do I have to be here?”

“You are the only one who knows what’s appropriate to wear when meeting gods.”

Anubis clicked his tongue, but didn’t leave, shaking his head at a bright blue sweater he had dismissed last time already.

“I am a god too, and yet you don’t seem to be concerned about that too much,” he mumbled. “Stop fussing over it, their lot doesn’t care.”

Something about the tone of the god made Noah pause. ‘Their lot’ sounded so… alien, spat out with a hint of disgust. 

Perhaps, he thought, it made sense. By the sound of it, the gods who still stuck around gathered together, and yet Anubis had never even remotely mentioned any of them, or looked like he held any communication with them. Noah fidgeted with the collar of his new sweater, his eyes darting to quickly look at the figure in his armchair. 

‘Have they… ostracised you?’

He didn’t dare ask the question, thinking of how Anubis said some of them preferred death to the current state of the world. 

‘Have you dealt the final blow yourself? Did they come to you for judgement?’

The thought made him lonely, and he wasn’t even the one who’d lived millennia losing his age old friends to his own hands. 

“I guess I just want to show my respect.” As soon as he said that, he bit his tongue, realising how much of his sentimental spirit slipped through, making the hound’s ears turn towards him. “I mean, not like they will respect me much, that’s a given, but maybe they could at least see me as one making an honest attempt.”

He kept on rumbling, hoping the more he talked, the more he could shake the pathetic wobble in his voice. Anubis watched him silently, before nodding towards a simple black shirt, one that would match his own. 

“Just wear black, as long as you are with me they would not do anything to you.”

“It’s not my safety I worry about,” Noah started, pulling his t-shirt over his head before trying on the new purchase. “I mean, even if they kill me, wouldn’t it just send me back to you?”

Anubis growled.

“Can you not?” The god rubbed at his eyes, the sight of Noah’s blood all over his room still fresh in his memory. “This isn’t just some escape card.”
Noah’s hands paused at a button. 

“What do you mean?”

There clearly was something he was missing. 

“You said I can’t die.”

“I said we don’t know your actual time and reason of death. Say you decide to smash your head into a wall in front of me now, or piss me off enough to bite it off, just theoretically. What guarantee do we have that this isn’t your intended death?”

Noah shut his mouth with a loud clank.

He had never considered that. Immediately, he felt cold sweat on his neck where the sensation of the knives piercing through the artery came back in a phantom pain. Without realising it, he reached up to touch the sensitive skin, eyes unfocused and breathing heavy. 

“Noah!”

It took him a moment to snap out of it, the hands of the deity on his, holding them away from his neck. 

“Focus, listen to me, don’t think about it.”

His fingers tried to reach to scratch at where the piercing wounds would be, but Anubis held him back. 

“It’s alright. I know why you did it, and I will… I will make sure it won’t come to it again.”

He allowed Noah a moment to catch his breath, letting go of his hands only when the man’s pulse under the touch slowed down. 

‘I could have really died back then.’

Anubis closed the remaining buttons one by one, calming him down:

“You still don’t need to be afraid of them. There is an unspoken rule among those who hide away around those parts - they are to avoid everything related to me.” Noah was about to add something about how he should not be so critical of himself, but Anubis pulled at the shirt to shake him a bit. “I mean it. Abuse my authority and speak in my name. It will come with reluctance to cooperate, with disgust and prejudice, but it will come with fear and obedience.” 

Noah watched the way the god’s eyes waited for him to come to his senses. He nodded. 

“Alright. If you say that’s the best way.”

Finally, the god stood up and eyed the human.

“Yes, this will do. Just… Please remove the tag and iron it properly.”

Admitting in the mirror he looked quite dashing, Noah allowed his mind to finally wander off as he hummed a tune looking for the iron. He undid the buttons yet again, dropped the shirt off and got to work, finding an odd sense of comfort in such a casual activity. 

Maybe today would go fine. Maybe nothing will go out of the ordinary. 

Anubis returned to the room without a knock with a visibly distressed look. 

“We are postponing the visit.” Before Noah could argue, he raised his hand and silenced him with a light growl. “Noah, have you any idea what powers you might have messed with?”

That was clearly not a conversation to be had with an iron on, so Noah turned it off and put it away. 

“Elaborate.”

There was something in the eyes of the god bordering with fear, that looked over the human as if searching for clues, answers. 

“You show up at my gates, tell me you were contacted, only to later drag a homunculus to me-”

“Please, at least refer to him by his name.”

“You don’t even remember it yourself! Ugh,” the god dropped into the chair and Noah really felt like whatever happened was serious. “Do you ever feel like your life is just a little bit too full of coincidences? To an annoying degree.”

Noah felt his fingers shake. That was something he had been meaning to bright up a while ago, but seeing how Anubis glared at him in suspicion made him back away. The massive figure stood up, walking up to him until there was nowhere to back away: Noah’s back hit the wall of his already small bedroom and he shrunk under the intense piercing gaze of a predator. 

“Noah, what did you mingle with?”

“I didn’t do anything! Didn’t I tell you, I have no idea how things ended up this way!”

He wasn’t going to fall for this, he had to stand his ground, but the way the god silently judged him, brought back the fear of their first meeting, the coldness of his body when his heart was ripped out from his chest. Whatever trust they had been forming was frail enough to crumble in a single moment of doubt. 

“I understand.”

Finally, Anubis backed away, eyes still on Noah. 

“Get dressed and wait for me.”

“Are you not going to explain what just happened?!”

Noah quickly grabbed his half ironed shirt and ran behind the god who only stopped him. 

“I will be returning down for a bit alone.”

“That’s not an explanation!” First, Noah tried to shake the hand that got in his way, but admitting he did not have the strength for it, he simply slipped under, standing in Anubis’ way. “What took over you, what the hell was that?!”

The deity did not seem to soften, only staring at the figure with a calculating look. 

“How the pantheon of remaining gods who hide in their fear-stained little corner never dare say a word to me, avoiding me for centuries, only to show up at my gates the moment you of all people decide it’s time we paid them a visit?”

Noah would really love to know that too…

“You think it’s me?!”

“Your life has been surrounded by way too many lucky coincidences, Noah, and you know it well. Don’t lie to me.”

“Well, there have been a couple here and a few there…”

The god sighed. 

“Wait for me here.”

“No way!”

Even if it took another hour, he would not just let the god take the reins of this situation in his hands. Even if it was another suspicious coincidence, if it really was related to him, wasn’t he the one who needed to be there?


Sensing his thoughts, Anubis clarified: 

“I have no idea why they are there, and they know they aren’t welcome. Do you really want to risk it when you don’t understand what forces are pulling more and more danger your way?”

“I do. And if you don’t take me with you, I will join you in my own ways.”

They just talked about it, and he really would not want to pull the same stunt again, but the god’s ears twitched in a way that made Noah relieved: he knew his persuasion was starting to work on Anubis. 

“Fine. But you don’t say a word unless I tell you to.” 

“That seems excessive.”

“Noah, if they know about your situation, ask yourself - how come?”

All of the potential answers were one worse than the other.




Walking through the underworld corridors, Noah really wished Nephele was around. Somehow she always gave him a sense of comfort when surrounded by things beyond his control. Instead, he tightly squished the fluffy creature in his hands and carried on behind the god. 

“Must you take this thing with you?”


“It’s for moral support. Plus, you really need to spend more time with it. It likes you.”


The creature barked and licked Noah’s hand. 

They passed by the murals and opened the heavy doors to the judgement hall, Anubis’ tall figure scaling up to the proper size. 

‘Bet they would not imagine him on a leash…’

Noah tried to joke to himself to shake off the anxiety, but it was not working too well, so he focused on the fluff he was holding instead. 

One by one, the lamps in the hall lit up, dispersing the endless fog and bridging up the outline of the still broken golden chains. On any other occasion he would poke fun at Anubis, but that night he held his mouth shut as instructed. 

Two tall figures turned in their direction, long white gowns dragging over the dark stone. 

“The Host is unfashionably late.”

A low female voice sounded like its owner wanted nothing less than to be there.

“The Host never had a chance to set the time for this meeting.”

Caught between two storms, Noah felt like a pathetic abandoned surfboard, dragged by the dangerous current, dreaming of the shore. 

“To what do I owe this pleasure?”

Anubis took a step, shielding Noah from the eyes that immediately fell upon him.

“What is that?”

“I believe the duty of the Goddess of Wisdom is not to ask questions, but to answer them when requested, Minerva.”

Noah could see her hands shake with anger, lips tightly pursed as she took a deep breath.

“You recently visited the library.” Her tone was cold and she turned to stare right at Noah as he peeked over the god’s arm. “I’d love to know why.”

From what Anubis said, he would have never guessed the library had an owner. Surely Anubis had requested official permission for their visit…

“I do not believe myself to be obligated to report my every move to you. The library is not your property and has never been.”

The woman was starting to seethe with anger, and in a way Noah could understand her: even when uninvolved, he could feel how annoying Anubis was. 

“You used ingredients that I had left there.”

“They are of public use and I paid my fees, what basis do you have to come demanding more of me?”

In the light of the room, Anubis’ silhouette felt overpowering, his dark figure casting deep shadows into the endless fog that surrounded them as a reminder whose territory they were on. 

Still, the woman smiled oddly relaxed. 

“I just happened upon the list of the materials you used, and how interesting it was: how come it was the precise formula for something you know well enough is frowned upon.”

Instinctively, Noah hid the fluffball in his pocket, hoping it would sit quietly. He really should not have taken it along, thinking of how the Janitor gently handed it over to him.

“I have the rights to it, as well as a reason. Do not mistake my actions for the questionable choices of your own kind.”

Anubis gestured towards Minerva’s companion and as the figure turned, in the light of the dancing flames Noah saw her face reflect it: smooth and metallic, made of old dimmed gold. 

Minerva scoffed.

“Since when do you seek such company. Whatever my brother has done is not for you to comment on. I asked a question.”

“And I answered - it is none of your business.” 

Now Noah could really tell what Anubis meant by none of the other gods liking him. In a way, however, he was starting to side with Death, feeling like everyone was needlessly up in his business. 

“You’ve never created life, and now you suddenly need one. How peculiar. I wish to see it.”

Anubis was starting to lose his patience, but feeling Noah pull on his sleeve, he knew he had to come up with a decent enough lie to get them to leave them alone. He could not say it was disposed of, that would bring even more questions. He could not say it was the creature in the human’s hands, or they might consider it his weakness. 

Noah felt the god hesitate and the woman in front of him took a step to the side, leaning in so close her eyes stared right at him. Feeling the fluff in his pocket move and thinking about Leo in the dungeon, he took a step forward, made up his mind about an idea he’d get scolded for later, and opened his mouth:

“Master, is it my turn to speak?”


He wrapped his arms around Anubis’ and the room went heavy with silence.
