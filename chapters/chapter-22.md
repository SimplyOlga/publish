All he had to do was not piss the god off. The only thing he had accomplished so far was taking his stuff without permission and never giving it back. The red jacket that was previously left hanging in the room was now with Leo, back in the cell. 

How was he even supposed to know, it was just a simple ragged piece of clothes left behind as if no one needed him. Especially not a god. 

‘How does it even fit…’

He thought back to putting it on and then realised that it fit both him and Leo perfectly desire a significant difference in their complexion. 

Magic godly uniform. That he took and never returned. 

The Janitor frowned, assessing the mortal in front of him, lifting his hands to grab the man and pick him up like he weighed nothing. 

‘There we go.’

In a way, Noah was already used to it - but since they were in the ER it felt like it would be very awkward if he just respawned there as soon as the Janitor was done crashing his bones. 

He closed his eyes and sent his regards to Leo and Nephele, and a curse to Anubis. 

However, he was only gently placed down to the side. When Noah opened his eyes he was set away from the spilled soapy water, safely to the side from where the Janitor got to work. 

‘Oh.’

He did overreact a little, but such was the way of doing things around here. 

With a bit of guilt for causing the mess and also misjudging the god, Noah watched the puddle mopped away before he finally spoke. 

“Mister…”

He didn’t even have the name. He added Nephele to the list of beings to curse, realising there was a decent chance a god would not like to be called a janitor. 

“I’m sorry,” he opted for a neutral way to seek attention, jumping right in front of the worker. “Nephele sent me to you.”

The Janitor turned to look at him, eyes patiently waiting for elaboration. 

“She said you know some of the locals, and I need to find someone.”

He pulled the note out of his pocket and handed it into the giant’s hand. The god struggled to unwrap the small piece but when he saw the drawings his brows twitched as if it hurt him deeply. 

They stood in silence, with Noah wondering what he could even say. 

“Do you know who left this in my room?”

The Janitor nodded, his eyes softening as if reminiscent of something nostalgic. 

Still, he didn’t say a thing. 

“I need to find them, I was wondering if you know their name, or let’s say… how they died?”

It was a bold move but the Janitor simply handed him the note back and nodded. Without saying a thing. 

A broom was handed into Noah’s hands and he almost flinched, surprised how gentle the man was even with his massive size. 

“You want me to clean? Sure, I mean I’m not bad with cleaning.”

The Janitor nodded, finally speaking with a deep but soft voice:

“Understaffed.”

When he nodded towards the hand in which Noah still held the note it became clear: the Janitor did in fact know the guard he was looking for, and even that they had left the underworld. But the question was - how to get him to talk?

“Ok if I do their duties for the day, will you tell me more? I want to bring them back, wouldn’t that be nice?”

The Janitor looked conflicted but still nodded, his face oddly peaceful for someone who could easily snap Noah in half with one hand. 

“Great, then um… where do I go?”

From his pants pocket the god pulled out a tiny notebook and a pen, quickly writing something down. Upon inspection when it was handed over, Noah realised it was a list. The issue was that it was written in a script he couldn’t even read. 

Internally Noah cursed himself for being monolingual: the only linguistic achievements in his portfolio were a few words of German. 

“I’m sorry, can you maybe write it in another language?”

The Janitor looked flustered, quickly getting to work on another page, handing Noah yet another unreadable page: this time in something resembling Chinese. 

“Right. Maybe there is another language we could try?”

Then it resembled something he had seen before, and still it was impossible to read. Noah was certain Anubis would find this amusing once the news would inevitably reach him. 

Thinking for a moment, the Janitor ripped yet another page out and handed it to Noah. 

‘Oh.’

“Yeah, this works.”

It was a list of 5 Arabic numbers he was literate enough to read and a few small scribbles resembling a mop, a duster and a smile. 

Straight to the point. 

“So I should just clean these rooms, right?”

The giant nodded. 

“Perfect. You will have it done in no time, Sir!”



On his way out, Noah decided that the best way to get information would be to reach the minimum level of affection with the god. Yes, his track record with deities was not the best, but it was only because his ulterior motives were not strong enough from the start. 

This time he was going all in. 

Diving into a familiar corridor, he walked all the way back into the dungeons, finding the cell he had just left a bit ago. The idea of seeing Leo so soon after what had happened was scary, but he was willing to be the one to take the first step. Granted, out of the two he was the only one with such an ability. 

“Hey there,” he awkwardly opened the door that was left unguarded. Noah did not know why Anubis had put such newly found trust in him, but he could not complain. “Sorry, I can leave if you don’t want to see me.”

He saw Leo sitting exactly where he was left: on the small bench in the corner of his room, staring at a wall, moving only when he heard the familiar voice. 

Noah was unused to such quiet treatment, with how usually Leo would always be the chattier one among the two. 

“I wanted to check in on you,” saying it somehow felt wrong after thinking about ulterior motives earlier, so he added: “Rather, I came to take something.”


Leo leaned back, head hitting the wall in a way that screamed he had no powers to argue or even ask. 

“I mean…”

This was too difficult, so Noah felt like the right approach was sincerity.

“I have a plan. I will get you out of here.”

As it made Leo think of their escape earlier, he only chuckled, not commenting.

“No, I mean it. I mean not just from here, but from your entire situation.”

The way Leo’s eyes showed no reaction was heartbreaking, but Noah only pushed further:

“I will not lie, it will be difficult and I have no idea if it will even work, but… if you would allow me - I want to try.”

“Why?”

Finally hearing his friend’s voice made Noah walk up and lean against the bars separating them.


“Because I don’t want to be the reason you die. And I don’t want to be the reason you live either. I want you to have a life where you’d have so many reasons to live you’d struggle to pick one.”

There was a small laugh that escaped from Leo.

“Every day since I’ve known you I could not decide what I wanted more: to keep you safe or to strangle you with my own two hands.” Leo looked down. “I tried that earlier, didn’t I? Impressive it took me so long.”

Noah disregarded the comment.

“I don’t care about that. Tell me that when you are reborn to live your life as you wish. Then you are free to come strangle me. Actually, no, stay away from me then. You should live a life without thinking how to kill me ideally.”

An ideal scenario with no clear steps on the way. A rough climb to the top. 

“I don’t intend to give up, Leo. So please, give me a chance.”

Leo could not say anything, so Noah added one last thing:

“And please give me that red jacket I lended you.”




With the red jacket tied around his waist, he got to work. Noah did not intend to give it back to the Janitor immediately, no, that would have been too suspicious. So, instead he would hold onto it until he is done with cleaning to both use it as his excuse to snoop around places and to spill the lies of how he accidentally found it while on duty. 

A perfect plan.

The first room was the hardest to find since he had never noticed before that all the doors in the palace had numbers on them. It took a while to trace everything back and figure out where the certain sets ended and the new continued. After what felt like half a day he finally knocked on the first door marked in his to-do list. 

“Come in!”

The familiar voice made him rather relieved. 

Behind the door was a small but quite cozy office space, resembling one you could find above the ground. A few potted plants, massive bookshelves filled with file folders and two desks. 

“My, it’s been a while, Noah.”

The cat with the white dot on her ear waved from her desk, the other following shortly: 

“I want to say you’ve grown since, but really it doesn’t seem so.”

The two chuckled, inviting him in. 

“Tea, coffee?”

“No, sorry I am on duty.”

The two exchanged glances that spoke of hunger for gossip they had not yet obtained.

“Duty?”

“How interesting~”

He knew he would get bombarded with questions, so he stood his ground stern.

“A fair trade: an answer for an answer.”

The feline eyes lit up. 
“Oh, Noah, how did you know-” 

“-that that is our favourite game!”

The two rose from their seats and rummaged through a drawer, pulling out something that closely resembled the scales in the room he first met Anubis. It gave him an uneasy feeling on instinct, but the scales were so small he thought it was alright. 

“Game?”

“The rules are simple. One side tells something, information, a story.”

“And then places a weight on the scales to measure how much of it was new and of interest to the other party.”

The cats showed him two small weights. As he received one it felt… weightless, as light as if there was nothing in his hand at all. 

‘Makes sense, they think they have heard it all already.’

“And then we change?”

“Indeed,” the second cat added, weighing the piece in her hand. “You tell us something interesting and we will hate to satisfy your curiosity in return until the scales are balanced. 

It felt like a reasonable idea, but he still took a precaution: 

“If I go first, is there a way you tell me so much I will have to pay back for what’s exceeding the limit?”

The feline eyes narrowed as they smiled.

“You sure are cautious.”

“No, Noah, whatever you tell us we will match without putting you in debt.”

“After all-”

“-What you can offer us is a lot more limited than what we can offer you.”

It was somewhat rude but undeniably true, so Noah gripped the weight and went on with it. He spoke of the events since he escaped, of what they did in the overworld, omitting some details, feeling the weight in his hand starting to pull down. He got all the way to the moment he made the deal with Anubis and ended on the request from the Janitor.

“And here I am. But your office seems quite clean already.”

“There is some dust here and there, it won’t take you long.”

They did not seem to care about his part-time job one bit the moment the weight was placed on the scale dragging it down with force. 

“Your turn,” Noah felt relieved to see his information was worth something. 

“Ask away.”

“We wouldn’t know what it is you really want to know.”

At first he wanted to take out the note and ask them about the guard, but since he had already pretty much got confirmation from the Janitor that he knew them, it felt like a waste. Instead, he decided to think in advance: 

“If I come to you with a name of someone who has died, would you find information on how and where they passed?”

“Naturally, the very files behind your back are for the exact reason to document deaths that pass through our judgement.”

Noah looked around thinking that while it was a hefty collection, there was no way it would cover all of the deaths in the history of deaths. 

“It might not look like it, but there is every single one of them in there.”

“Except one.”

Noah knew what they meant - his file was not there yet. One of the cats pulled a binder and fetched a freshly looking stack of papers that was not filled in yet with anything except his name.

“We are still waiting to figure out what to put here.”

Somehow it felt like looking at his own freshly dug grave. 

Noah nodded, promising them to die some time soon and let them know. 

Still, there was some weight left to the scales so he thought of his spare question. The possibilities of some actually useful information were intimidatingly limitless, so he settled for something trivial:

“What is Nephele’s favourite homemade dish?”




After a bit of dusting and getting on good terms with the cats for later by watering the plants and getting them some coffee, he walked through the corridors towards the next room. 

432, 433, 434.

435.

He knocked lightly on the door, putting the red jacket on in case the resident of the room would try to throw a fit. 

Just a minute later the door opened, but only slightly.

“What are you doing here, Noah.”

So it was Nephele’s room.

“I am here as a janitor in training, to clean.”

The woman hissed, narrowing the gap in the door even more.

“I keep telling him not to touch my room, I will clean myself.”

It would be a lie to say he was not curious to see her chamber, but she made sure he could not even peek in.

“I have to do something at least if I want to really tell him I did the job.”

Nephele could be heard cursing in a language he could not understand before she tossed him a singular mug. 

“Here. Thanks for cleaning, Mister Janitor-in-training.”



With the second objective marked, it felt like his job was going suspiciously easy. He once again tied the jacket around his waist and went on looking for the next number. 

The room did feel a lot more extravagant just by the ornaments of the doors alone. It was definitely occupied by someone with more status, and Noah dreaded the possibilities, all the while his curiosity was getting the better of him. 

After not hearing a response for a while, Noah took the initiative into his own shaking hands pulling out the master key given to him by the Janitor. It fit, as expected, perfectly, allowing him to slip in while anxiously giving the last fair warnings:

“Coming in, coming in. I’m just a janitor, here to clean.”

No response came thought so he closed the door behind himself and looked around the massive suite a lot, a lot more extravagant than the room he was locked in. Featuring a separate open wine storage and a kitchen that was mostly stacked with sweets, the main room stretched to continue behind a golden partition leading to the area with a massive conversation pit all draped in red velvet and silk. His eyes scanned over the decorations on the walls picking up on the familiar item: two ornamental daggers propped on the wall above a bouquet. One of those daggers was the ceremonial blade Ammit had given him earlier to assist in his escape. Wondering how it even got back to her room and whether Anubis and her had an argument about her involvement Noah thought it would be best to at least give his gratitudes. 

He took out the flowers from the vase and trimmed them, changing the water. Once that was done he ran back to the yard and carefully inspected the flowers there to find the ones that Ammit seemed to like. Thinking back to seeing the researcher who purposefully sought out new ways to die pass out in the yard, he approached the grass and the weeds with newfound attentiveness. 

‘Even if I die I just respawn, no?’

Still, he tried to be careful not to touch things that were not what he was looking for. 

Back at the room he put the new set of flowers on the middle table and picked a card from her desk to write a small message. It took a while to figure out just what to write. 

‘Thanks for helping me kill myself.’

That didn’t sound right, so he opted to just write ‘thank you’ leaving the rest for some other time he sees her in person. 

After tidying up pillows and blankets, wiping counters and organising scattered jewelry, he took in the sight of his job well done with pride and a bit of envy. 

He also wanted a nice room… 

Noticing the time was getting late, he rushed to the next room, only knocking a few times before impatiently putting in the master key. Oddly enough, the room was so clean it almost looked like it wasn’t even lived in at all. With concern, Noah prayed he wasn’t going to get startled by some ghost or spirit. 

‘I hope the room is not haunted. No one has died here, right?’ 

Realising the stupidity of his throughs, he diligently changed sheets, swiped the floor and puffed the pillows before setting for his last room. 

Somehow, he felt like he really didn’t want to enter the remaining room with the ominous single digit plating: 

1

Who could possibly reside in such a place… 

Without even knocking, he put the master key in and walked in loud and proud. 

Only to be met with silence. 

‘Is he actually working for once?’

Somehow considering all the time Anubis spent ruining his life, Noah had already started doubting he had anything going on for him at all. Still, the massive room looked empty and left in a slightly dishevelled state as if the owner had only rummaged through things before leaving. The messy surroundings did not match what Noah expected, but he still diligently, and with open personal curiosity, started picking up papers and pens, setting them on a massive work desk. None of the writing made sense to him, so Noah did not dwell on it too much, moving on to inspect Anubis’s kitchen and finally - the bedroom. 

Out of sheer politeness he knocked softly on the door and opened it to inspect the darkness of the room primarily occupied by a massive, positively gigantic bed. 

‘Wow, ok, no one needs a bed this big, man…’

Considering the height of the god it made sense, but still it was almost embarrassing how small Noah felt next to it. Submerged in the darkness of the late evening only moonlight slipped into the room through the small cracks in the curtains, so Noah reached out for a small lamp left behind in the living room and lit up the oil, placing it on the bedside table before getting to work. 

Initially work implied changing sheets and refreshing pillows, but the diligent worker he was Noah added an extra workload - snooping thought the God’s drawers out of curiosity. 

Clothes, papers, some accessories. Noah inspected them without much interest, taking in a shirt to press it against his body before swirling in front of a mirror. 

‘Tacky…’

The thought that crossed his mind came out in a form of a yelp when a hand grabbed him on his neck, pushing him right into the wall, face painfully scratching over the stone bricks. 

A low growl came from behind and in the corner of his eye Noah saw the massive figure behind him in the dark bearing his teeth at him. 

“Wait, wait, I’m sorry! I was just curious!”

The hand on his neck shivered and suddenly let go, forcing him to fall down catching his breath and rubbing at his scratched cheeks. 

“Noah?”

The man watched the deity retreat, and once again in the dark, when the moonlight illuminated the beastly body, Noah was reminded - in front of him was a monster. 

“I am sorry…”

He felt like shit, the pain itching on his face and a terrible sinking feeling of guilt settling in his chest. 

The god seemed frozen in the spot, shaking his head as if waking up fully. He picked up the small oil lamp and set it next to Noah. 

“Did I hurt you?”

Noah was about to say something incredibly rude, but instead he shrank on the spot shaking his head. 

“No, it’s fine. Sorry, I was just going to clean, I was asked to clean, I didn’t know- I didn’t know this was your room… I’m sorry.”

He kept apologising even when the massive hands took hold of his face to turn it towards the light, the dark eyes examining the scratches on his face. 

“I am sorry, Noah.”

How was it that just one word suddenly made the human feel like the rest didn’t matter. He stared at the god in disbelief, feeling like he did not deserve to hear such a sincere display of… remorse, guilt and a bit of something else he was not sure how to describe. 

“I was having a nightmare. I didn’t realise it was you.”

A god having a nightmare was a terrifying idea, so Noah allowed the hands to pick him up and sit him on the bed where Anubis opened one of the drawers he hadn’t shamelessly rummaged through. Yet. 

“I am fine, it’s just a scratch.”

Anubis paused, a small tin in his hands. 

“I am glad I did not wake up to seeing your blood on my hands and walls.”

Noah shivered when the god said that kneeling in front of him, their eyes barely on the same level. 

“Handling mortals requires care and restraint I was not naturally built with.”

His fingers scooped a bit of ointment from the tin and pressed against a scratch on Noah’s face making him hiss. 

“It is rather cruel: to be placed on duty that should require one to be of comfort to those who depart.”

In his voice Noah heard a hint of melancholy brewed over millennia. 

“I tried at first to make myself a little more…” He could not figure out the right words. “I wanted to be an equal to those who only saw me as a being beyond their comprehension.”

Noah stayed silent, feeling like he could not interrupt what the god likely had not had a chance to say in centuries. 

“So I changed to be what they expected. A ruler, a judge. An executioner.”

There was a lot Noah felt like saying to Anubis, but in that moment he felt like he was finally seeing a glimpse of the other side of the story. And that terrified him. 

Anubis closed the tin and looked at his work: the eyes studied the lightly bleeding marks on Noah’s face with the same expressionless eyes that held centuries of unspoken grievances. 

“I know I am difficult to… everything. I am difficult to understand, so I won’t ask you to understand me. But please, understand the law of the nature that is all I have left.”

“That isn’t all you have, though.”

Noah gestures around the room, thinking back to all the people and beings that walked the corridors of the god’s palace. 

The god hesitantly nodded, finally rising up with a cough as if waking up from his dazed state fully. 

“As apologetic as I am, I have to tell you you should know better than walking into someone’s sleeping quarters in the dead of night.”

There it was - the same scolding demeanour of “I know better”. Just then, Noah let it slide jumping down from the bed but only after bouncing a little on the soft mattress. 

“Well, I am almost done here so if you want me to leave lend me a hand with these massive sheets.”

Never in his life had he imagined the literal god of death helping him do laundry but in that moment Anubis looked like he would do anything to apologise for the incident so Noah watched him struggle with pillowcases wondering why he didn’t ask him for something even more unbefitting. 

“Tomorrow morning I have something to show you.”

Anubis watched him back his janitor cart with sheets and eyed his red uniform. 

“But please dress somewhat… more accordingly.”
