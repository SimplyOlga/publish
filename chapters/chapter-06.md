
The corridor he was led through was a lot less gloomy than one leading to the scale room. The cats walked slower, allowing Noah to take in the interior of the dark brick walls and detailed pillars. By the time he finally saw some daylight and jumped towards a window, the two only softly explained: 

“This isn’t your sun.”

The blue leaved trees stood swaying in a big courtyard, lightly dancing in the wind, illuminated by the oddly cold hue of what looked more like the moon. 

“Are we underground?”

“According to your myth, we are. But it isn’t really so. After all-”

“How would there be sun underground?” 

The two paid no might to Noah’s confusion, urging him to move saying he could explore the yard later if he wanted. If he would allow it. 

He still didn’t even know his name.

A bit earlier, before leaving the room, he tried his best to find out:

“What should I call you?”

The hound looked down at him, clearly in a bad mood. 

“Why would you ever call me?”

“Don’t mind him,” the white eared cat pushed her employer on the elbow. “Working with the dead does wonders to one’s inability to communicate with the living.”

The jacal’s eyes narrowed in a more expressive way than ever before:

“A mortal is not entitled to such knowledge,” he leaned in, teeth baring in the light of the torches. “Not like he could handle it even.”

He straightened up, adjusting the collar of his shirt before really, fully opening his mouth to speak, for the first time that night: 

☚☗◡⟥⦕⪓𐀶

Noah squinted, feeling an odd tickle in his ears. It sounded like speech, a language, and yet it was not audible, more so a sensation. 

He decided not to even attempt repeating it, shutting his mouth under a triumphant stare of the god. The divine ‘I told you so’.



“You don’t need to worry about your stay here,” one of the cats pulled him from his thoughts. “To us - human life is most precious and worth protecting, as ironic as it might sound.”

‘I guess…’

After all, Noah thought, they would have killed him if that was the solution to all the problems. 

“So what will I do now?”

“You will wait for us to fix things.”

“For you to find my… death date?”

“Yes.”

“And also,” the other continued, turning a turn into a deeper part of the endless building, away from the light of the beautiful yard Noah only caught a glimpse of. “We need to find out just how this happened.”

“One mistake is fine, but if we don’t know the origin of it - a mistake can become a precedent for a pattern.”

The uneasy feeling from the morning had returned: the memories of a creeping sense of something being amiss right before his eyes. He wondered what it could have been, what could have made things all so wrong. 

They led him to a small room, assuring he would be safe here but giving one last warning: 

“Mortals are highly uncommon around these parts, so there are a lot who would find you amusing against your best interests.”

“You best lay low, we have not made your presence public, mostly because he would rather keep secrets than publicly admit he made a mistake.”

The two giggled, imagining their employer wondering what to do with the broken scales before the next appointment. The dead never waited. And they did judge. 

“I will stay put here,” Noah assured them he had no intention of getting into more trouble or finding out just who other residents of the place would be. 

“That is wise. If anyone tries to get you out by knocking, do not leave.”

“This thing here,” one of them pointed at a small window barely a size of Noah’s hand on the door, “will help. If you hear knocking but don’t see anyone, stay inside. We will pick you up in the morning.”

Somehow that made him more uneasy. 

“What does it show that it… differentiates?”

“Well, to move and live one has to possess a soul,” one started.

“It’s just that some have souls that were originally not theirs”, the other finished. 

Noah thought about it for a moment: in a lot of mythologies people believed there to be a concept of a soul, in Egypt, if he recalled correctly, it was the perseverance of the soul that was the goal of a proper burial. The idea of creatures that walked around with souls that weren’t theirs made him shiver: he did not wish for his to be next. 

“I will stay indoors.”



The room to his disposal was comfortable, albeit outdated. He had a massive bed and a side couch, a washroom with a tub and… less modern facilities than he would have liked. On a side table some fruits and water was left for him, so he assumed he was not going to stay here for long. 

Looking through a window, he admired the small yard with blue trees and tall grass he had seen before, spotting a figure down there, kneeling around weeds in a long mustard coloured coat that stood out in the scene almost comedically. ‘Was it another human?’ he wondered, unable to see if the person had a human or an animal head. He watched the figure rummage through the plants, so immersed in the matter that they did not once in the twenty minutes or so of Noah’s observation straighten or stand up. Noah pressed his forehead against the glass, wondering if everything in the eternal land of the dead was so long and boring, but the moment his head hit the glass, the figure downstairs collapsed, dropping on the ground motionless. 

Noah stared at the body for a while, uncertain what to do. Were they dead? Wasn’t death normal here? How was he potentially more alive than that person?

Before he could even shut his agape mouth, two silhouettes approached the figure on the ground, leaning to look over it. ‘Silhouette’ was the most flitting word for them, as the slightly blurry dark creatures stretched their inhumanly long arms and picked the body up, holding it in their outstretched arms like it weighed nothing. Hastily, they took the person away, without much commotion to follow. 

Uncertain how to feel about it, Noah took a mental note to be careful around the grass outside if he was ever to make it to that yard he was now a lot less excited about.

Depleted of options, just about ten minutes later, he crawled under the duvet and fell asleep, feeling like the adrenaline rush from before was wearing off, leaving him fully exhausted. Under the sheets, he felt oddly at peace, considering what just happened, but the human mind was one thing, and the human body - a whole other. 

KNOCK

Even in death he was not left alone. 

Slipping from under the covers, Noah shivered at the sensation of the cold stone floor under his feet and walked to the door, already reaching for the handle when he froze on the spot. 

‘Oh.’

The word of the cats came back to him and he looked at the small peeping window with uncertainty. On one hand, he wanted to see nothing, unwilling to be a victim to cliche jumpscares. On the other - the thought of there being no one was somehow a lot worse. 

He brought a chair from the corner of the room and climbed to look into the window positioned for someone clearly not of his height. 

No one. 

KNOCK

‘Oh come on…’

KNOCK KNOCK 

‘Go away, please go away. Please, please…’

KNOCK

He climbed down and sat on the chair, staring at the door, patiently waiting for the visitor to leave. 

It took another minute of banging before the silence resumed, leaving him unwilling to return to bed. Instead, he placed a bet on the amount of midnight suitors he was about to get.

There were five to follow. Some knocked on the door gently, almost intimately. One scratched. The other banged for so long Noah gave up and banged back, scaring the creature away immediately. 

‘Best to let them know who they are dealing with.’

After a while of quiet, he crawled back to bed, thinking he should ask for headphones or at least a good book if he would not be getting any sleep anyway. 

Knock knock. 

‘For crying out loud!’

He stomped to the door and stepped on the chair peeking out just to see the good old nothing. 

About to go back to press his head into a pillow, Noah paused as a voice came through: 

“Hey, we are here to pick you up!”

“Come out!”

The cats. 

‘Oh…’

A realisation struck him - he had no idea if the two had souls or not. 

“Noah, open up now, we don’t have all night.”

He sat down, contemplating: surely they would be a little more convincing, if it was really them. He was about to talk back, asking for details, but the idea of talking back to whatever horror it could be did not sound good at the moment. They clearly instructed him to stay, but… they never said how and when they would pick him up. 

‘Surely they have a key.’ 

“You’ll have to open the door, Noah. We don’t have a key.”

‘How disgustingly considerate of them,’ he pressed his face into his hands in desperation. 

The night was still young outside, and held with the excuse of ‘I was scared’ he patiently ignored the two. 

“Noah, Honey, this isn’t very polite.”

He sat quiet, frowning at the nickname. 

“Sweetie, open up.”

Even his ex never called him like that, knowing he disliked it. 

“We will take you to have breakfast, Honeybun!”

Where would they draw the line-

“Let’s go enjoy it together, Tasty~

‘Okaaaay no.’

He pressed the chair against the door in a way he had seen in movies, quite certain it would not stop anyone, not even a human. Sulking on the bed, he counted cracks in the stone ceiling above when the voices finally gave up after a long while:

“If this is how you play, we will return, Mouthwatering~”

The sweet silence returned. 

He turned to try to doze off when another knock finally crossed the line that had been edged for four hours. 

KNOCK

“Just fuck OFF!”

He ran to the door and gave it a bunch of loud bangs, like a child throwing a tantrum, until his mind was dizzy with his blood pressure spiking. 

The creature behind the door stayed silent. 

‘Did it lea-’

“I will pretend I did not hear that.”

At the sound of the Hound’s voice Noah bit at his lip and took a deep breath, sitting down for a moment to process what he might have just done. 

He really did not want to look into that small window. 

“Will you ignore me now or will you actually let me in?”

Defeated, he pushed the chair back in place and stepped up to look.

No one. 

It felt like a wave of relief cleared his mind until the familiar question popped in his head. 

‘Would he even have a soul?’

“I am waiting.”

‘Surely not.’

“I was told not to,” Noah gathered his courage and whispered, knowing full well the creature heard. After all, the god had long enough ears to put to good use. 

“You ought to be joking…”

Somehow the sound of frustration was convincing enough on its own, but Noah valued his life (if he still had one) more than his personal bias. 

“I will see you in the morning, Sir.”

“Open the door, this is an order.”

‘Come on…’ Noah felt like crying. 

“In the morning…”

“Now. This is urgent, why am I even convincing you?!”

“Please, be patient, it’s a good quality.”

Silence. 

“Open the door.”

“No.”

“You… This is about your way to go up into your own bloody world!”

“Please, have some manners and let me sleep.”

“...”

“...”

“You are dead next time I see you.”

“I thought that was the whole point?”

He heard the most frustrated groan he could imagine a god could make before footsteps outside retreated. 

He was not bothered afterwards a single time that night. And yet, somehow, he could not find rest in himself to sleep…
