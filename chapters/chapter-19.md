The shadow guided them through corridors and up many stairs, exiting to the inner yard where Noah pulled the hood of a hoodie he found in his room earlier. He had no opportunity to get any clothes from home, so all he had in the underworld was just some clothes left for him by someone, and since Anubis had absolutely zero idea of Noah’s preferences, size, and granted absolutely anything at all - the hoodie was way too big on him. 

Leo looked around, as if admiring the scenery of the blue leaves dropping on the purple grass. It really was a pretty sight, but Noah could not get his anxiety to calm down - they could be seen anywhere, put on display. 

He caught up to the shadow, pulling at the long arm until it looked at him. 

“Do we have to go this way? We really should not be seen.”

The guard only tilted its head, as if only listening to the sound of his voice with no meaning behind the words. 

Noah cursed, but he had no choice but to follow the being that walked confidently somewhere, the small note from the fruit basket still clutched in its hands. 

Noah felt uneasy watching its back, but sometimes it would turn around to check if they were still following, showing an odd display of involvement bordering with… concern. 

He tried to think it was concern, that was a better option. 

As they slipped into another corridor, the hallways became more spacious, adorned with murals like the ones surrounding Anubis’ chamber. Instinctively Noah looked around, afraid there would be someone guarding the place. Least of all he wanted to bump into the god again, the weight of their conversation still fresh on him.

Ever since the dread of what they did in the lab room settled, he had been watching Leo from the corner of his eyes, wondering, questioning silently. 

Who and why made his friend?

It was a question he tried to shake off, but the implications of all the secrets held from him were cutting deep into his heart. 

Leo had never spoken of his family in almost a decade they had known each other. Not a single photograph of him as a kid was ever shown, not a single memory of childhood shared. Leo had always been… just Leo. And it was enough. It should have been enough. 

Why was it not enough… 

“It looks like we are here, no?”

Caught off guard by Leo’s voice, Noah snapped out of it, seeing the shadow guard staring at a door ahead of them. 

“Surely this isn’t just it… right?”

There was no way the exit from the underworld was just a door, unguarded, standing there like an average office door. 

The words of Anubis came back to him: the residents of the underworld could not simply leave unless they had a soul or authority. Someone like Nephele would regularly go out for work, so in a way it made sense that over millennia the need for keeping guard over the exit might have been dismissed. 

Still, it was too easy. 

But they had little to care about unless they tried. 

“Ready?”

Noah felt Leo’s hand on his shoulder, feeling like they were back in the days when Leo would push him through the doors to an auditorium before an exam he would be barely prepared for. 

“Ready.” 

The shadow guard by the door watched the two, its head suddenly blurring and shifting, as if trying to speak, unable to make a sound. 

“You are coming with us, such was the deal.”

The guard calmed down, extending its arm immediately, without hesitation. Inside its hand it still held the crumpled note, now passing it to Noah who put it in his pocket for safekeeping. 

“I will read it when we are on the other side, alright?”

The figure’s head blurred again, ever so slightly, as if in agreement. 

Feeling the cold of the metal door handle, Noah swallowed: he was the only one out of the three of them who could open the door still. He pushed at the lock and took a deep breath when it clicked and the handle turned. 

From the crack in the door, warm air poured in: like the heat of a sun in summer, like warmth of an embrace of a loved one, like the feeling of belonging. 

First, Noah nodded at the shadow guard, encouraging it to go. It hesitated, trembling like it was expecting it to hurt, but after a moment, it slipped into the white fog of the unknown, hand letting go of Noah’s. 

Next Noah had to let Leo pass: without him the door could shut closed, trapping him underground forever. 

“Finally, let’s leave this place behind, Noah.”

Noah tried to smile back, but his eyes shifted to Leo’s chest where under the uniform jacket bloodied threads were keeping his chest together.

“Yes, I will see you on the other side.”

With a confident step, Leo passed through, leaving him all alone in the room. 

Why was he hesitating? Why was it so difficult? What he was doing was right. It was selfish, but it was right. 

So why, why was he pausing. Why could he not take that step, as if waiting for someone to stop him, to tell him what to do, to explain things so he would not need to make decisions. 

He was scared. 

He was scared of defying the laws, and scared of blindly abiding them. 

“Noah, come back down.”

It was the voice he didn’t want to hear, but stood there waiting for. 





The moment Anubis closed the door to the lab, he allowed himself the moment of weakness. Sinking against a wall a little, he caught his breath, finally realising he was overheating from the stress piling up. 

Ouroboros. 

No, worse. 

Ouroboros was a symbol of life as a cycle, going through birth inevitably leading to death only to live anew. An eternity playing by the rules of life and death. 

The infinity ouroboros was a cursed mark of defying the very principles of nature. 

The snake that looped itself in pride of its immortality, refusing to die, only stretching its life in an endless cycle with no end. It was a direct insult to Death as a concept. A celebration of life with no meaning to its significance. The light without dark, the high without low. 

He growled, feeling his own fingers tremble at the abomination in his lower cell room. Whoever created that thing had gone through an unspoken level of sin: it must have taken an unbelievable amount of sacrifice, material and attempts to perfect such a creation that only served as a mocking message to the god of death. Or so he hoped. 

The other reasons for the birth of what should not be born were unknown and it made him feel… truly afraid. 

Catching his breath, the god straightened up, knowing that whatever the meaning behind it was, he had to at least make a few things right. 

First, he would speak to the mortal: their needless arguments were only adding complications to the already elaborate case they were in. 

Second, he would keep an eye on him. Not knowing the reason for Leo’s existence, the only hint they had was that he spent potentially all of his life around Noah. So, the odds were not in favour of it being a simple coincidence and the prospects made the god pick up his pace, walking the stairs towards the living quarters. 

On the way he thought back to their arguments, fully convinced he had been in the right, but admitting he could try to gain more of the mortal’s trust in simpler unrelated ways. First, he decided to give Noah a better room, maybe even allow him free roaming around the place. Second, he would take care of his food better, cursing himself for forgetting it. 

He was deep in thought when something felt unnatural. Rather, it felt familiar. 

The hound’s nose took in the air around the corridor, tensing at the familiar scent. 

Blood. Noah’s blood. 

He rushed to the locked door pulling at it, noticing how it opened easily without a key. The sight in front of him made the god freeze in place, feeling like for the first time in centuries he was shocked by the sight of lingering death: bloodied sheets, two knives tossed to the floor. 

His mind raced in panic, trying to make sense of what had happened, eyes darting around the room as he picked up the bloodied shirt tossed to the side. It was still lightly warm, it smelled of Noah: of blood mostly, but under it were the notes of sweat and tears, and of something else… burnt flesh, vomit, quicksilver. 

Throwing the thing away as if burnt, the god finally realised what had happened and in the moment of shock he was half angry half… impressed. 

As soon as he left the room, he ran, following the scent of blood he had smelled before that one night when the mortal decided to venture alone outside, only to be found at the sight of a car crash with his forehead bleeding. 

‘You insolent…’

He cursed, catching his breath as he ran into the yard, already guessing where the men had gone.

‘Please, please…’

He wanted to scream, hoping his voice would reach far enough to stall the man for just a moment when he dove into the corridor where the stench of blood was stronger. 

He was close, he was still here. Anubis only picked up pace, seeing the open doors to the room where the gates to the overworld were. In a way, he wanted to stop, too scared to actually see if he was too late, but he did not stop, only freezing when he saw the small figure watching the way the gray fog swirled around him. Alone. Lost in thoughts.

Afraid any word could scare him away, Anubis paused, taking in the silhouette that looked so tired, so worn out and abandoned.

He didn’t know what to say to make him believe anything Anubis had to say, so all he could do was to simply call out to him. 

“Noah, come back down.”





Noah jolted at the sound of his own name, turning to see the one he had been running away from. Instinctively, he thought he should be dashing through the gates towards freedom, but something made him pause, looking at the way the hound’s chest rose in deep ragged breaths after he ran so fast to catch him. 

“Noah, I am sorry.”

There were no specifics as to what he was apologising for, but it sounded sincere enough to make the man doubt himself. Afraid to sway, Noah looked back at the fog, thinking back to the days before all of this mess had started. 

“What for?”

Anubis knew he had to speak, that elaborate speeches to avoid direct answers would not work now. He struggled to even understand why he would care so much, trying to justify it as taking care of the mistake he had made. It made little sense, so he decided to let go of that excuse as well. 

“For things that are out of my control.”

It was the truth, and it hurt more than lies and excuses. 

“Don’t apologise,” Noah chuckled. “After all, I was never supposed to be there. And remember what you said when I just arrived?”

Anubis watched the way the man took a step, half disappearing into the fog. 

“You never make mistakes.”



Anubis ran to the door to catch the swirls of fog. That was all he had left after the vague shape of the man vanished through the gates, leaving behind only the scent of blood and a bitter feeling in the god’s mouth. 

He knew that going through the fog would not mean following right behind him, so he took a moment to take a deep breath, feeling like the day was only getting worse and worse. 

“Nephele.”

He called out, allowing the woman a few minutes to catch up to him. As the red hair peeked through the door, looking around curiously to assess the situation, she finally bowed to the god. 

“You called.”

Now, where would he even start to explain what had happened. 

“I know what happened.”

For the first time ever Anubis was happy to have the inner gossip line directed by his subordinates. He tried not to think of exactly how the story was portrayed by the Cats. 

“Do you want me to find him?”

Initially that was his idea, but as he had calmed down, Anubis reconsidered his approach. 

“No, not directly. I want you to go take a look at something else first.”
