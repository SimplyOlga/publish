“Death comes on time, and death does not discriminate.”

Nephele looked over the crowd passing by, leaning over the railing of the campus rooftop. Neither of them were allowed there, but no one needed to know. Somehow, having already died put Noah’s desire to follow simple instructions even lower than before. 

Maybe he did have authority issues. 

“Sometimes, however, death can be influenced by various forces.”

“External?”

“And internal, both.”

Anubis laid on the floor and peeked over the edge of the roof, leisurely and half-bored. To him, Noah assumed dozens of lives passing by were nothing but miniscule grains of sand in the flow of time over the millennia he had lived. 

“Are you saying someone can just… not die?” Noah wondered if he was any close to the same situation, but he could not at all imagine his resolve to live being so strong as to defy the basic law of life. “Like, they refuse to die?”

“Sort of, but it’s never as simple as that. And therefore every case deserves personal attention.”

Noah looked at her profile, noticing small scars littering her face, right under the inhumanly dark eyes that now stared at the crowd. The pitch black irises swirled like clouds of smoke, as terrifying as they were mesmerising. 

When she first took her glasses off, he decided not to ask about it, sensing the gaze of the hound next to him. Now, he wondered just what it was she could see. As if sensing his question, she explained:

“I see humans as colours, different but there is one rule they follow. At the end of their lives - they dim and lose the brightness.”

“Like ghosts?”

“In a way. But you think of ghosts as souls stuck in the realm of the living. People I am looking for are the opposite - men without a living soul, clinging to their existence.”

‘So, like zombies…’

He didn’t voice the thought. 





Henry woke up with a headache he washed away with medicine and a lot of water. Not fully, but at least he could get dressed and pack his work bag before heading to the campus just a little late for the meeting. 

“Apologies,” he slipped through the door finding a seat in the back of the auditorium.

He pulled out his notepad and diligently noted every part of the seminar on difference in pigment in parchment analysis and reconstruction. Two hours later, he left the room in an uplifted spirit, thinking about the last night.

His hands on the smooth surface of the coffin, fingers tracing over the old relieves and letters cut centuries ago. 

His forehead pressed against the cold stone of the statue above: features sharp and precise, except for the smooth curls embodying the face he was never destined to see in the flesh. 

“Are you listening, Henry?”

“Apologies,” he fixed his glasses and chuckled awkwardly. He had too much on his plate and even more on his mind. 






“Still no?”

“No.”

“What about that one? That guy looks pretty dead.”

“He is hungover.”

Noah felt like the endeavour was pointless. It’d been three hours and they still have not seen anyone even remotely… transparent. 

Sitting down on the ground, he propped his face onto his hand and looked at the peacefully sleeping hound. 

“Can’t he just like… sniff our target or something?”

He thought back to how the god sniffed him in the scales chamber, feeling embarrassed. 

The dog opened his eyes but remained inconspicuously quiet.

“We will continue tomorrow,” Nephele put her glasses back on and picked up the leash, watching her employer rise up with a yawn and a stretch. 

Somehow, Noah admitted he liked this form of the god of death more. 

“Why do you even think they will show up here?”


“Like I said, we have reasons to believe you attract death. Give it time, it will show itself.”

Noah patted his dusted knees and accepted the leash, getting immediately pulled away. 

“And how does that help me with my case?”

Nephele gave him a lightly surprised look. 

“No one ever said this was to help you. You wanted to go upstairs, this is the only way you can - under my supervision.”

The precautions still made little sense to him, but for now he decided not to argue: he felt great, more alive than he had before, so he simply decided to let the two see for themselves that he is perfectly fit to return back to the realm of the living without being babysat. 





“It’s… spacious.”

Nephele took off her glasses and tossed her jacket onto a chair already full of clothes. 

“I didn’t expect guests.”

He did not expect residents of the underworld to follow him all the way up to his place, but the alternatives, according to Nephele, were ‘either your place, or the eternal rest in the realm of the dead’.

He really wanted to have one night of sleep. So, he diligently changed the sheets in the guest room and checked on the fridge. 

‘Fuck.’

His cart full of groceries had been abandoned when he passed out in the bread aisle. What happened there even, did anyone care to call an ambulance for him?

“Any preferences for dinner?”

The woman reading a random book she found on his shelf looked at him with her dark eyes:

“Souls of the damned.”

He took a deep breath, calming himself.

“Pasta will do,” she chuckled.

With a nod, Noah noted down some essentials in his note app and finally looked at the biggest issue in the room:

“What about him? Dry or wet?”

The hound growled from the freshly made bed. 




After some persuasion, he was allowed to go alone, but ‘only down a few blocks’.

Noah mumbled all the way through the elevator, feeling like a child, overly monitored and restricted. Still, he took a deep breath of fresh night air and allowed himself a smile. 

He was alive. 

Until proven otherwise, he was alive. He was there, on the doorstep of his apartment building, doing his daily chores, in the primitive and familiar routine of a living human being. 

Carefully, Noah took the three careful steps down the small steps, recalling what Nephele said as he was leaving:

”Death will try to claim you, but as you don’t have the death date, it might find itself unconventionally confused. Don’t go far from us, within a couple blocks should be fine.”

It made little sense, but did not sound very nice. So, he tried to avoid the bread aisles and slippery stairs. 



At the store he picked them toothbrushes, milk and cereal for breakfast, a piece of raw beef for the god and some various frozen meals. It was better than nothing until they figure out what to do next. 

Eggs, milk, pasta, cream. 

He walked slowly, keeping an eye for sharp objects and suspicious individuals. 

‘So far so good.’

After Noah paid and packed everything, he let out a sight of relief. Maybe it wasn’t that bad. Feeling a little cold in the night autumn air, he shivered, checking his old commute tickets. He still has his pass with a few trips, so he stared at the nearest bus stop through the store’s window, feeling exhausted. This was better, he should really not be leaving those two undead alone in his apartment for too long.

Relieved, he jumped into a bus and closed his eyes, catching a break, feeling ice cream melting in his bag. 

A stop. 

Another. 

The unfamiliar landscape. 

He checks the screen only to see the wrong number. 554. Not 545. 

‘Fuck.’

As they passed another street, Noah felt the familiar feeling of anxiety creeping in. 

In just a moment, something bad would happen, he could feel it. 

And so, right on cue, with a loud BANG something hit the front of the bus.
