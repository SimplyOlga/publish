There was an unusual tension in Soleum’s body as he once again looked over the prepared items for the dive into the darkness. He was very well aware of the job: Sekwang High School was a setting for one of the most popular wiki entries in the history of the fandom, of course he has a decent knowledge about the darkness. A deeper knowledge than anyone could have... 

He was now even giving away a few tips to his roommate, not without a back thought of course.

Having gone back to his room to prepare for the dive, he once again assessed the items he was going to bring with him, laid out on his bed under the pillow. Each and every one of them was a carefully picked item of immense power that Soleum believed could be his escape route if push came to shove. Among them was his biggest bet. 

Wrapped in his Daydream uniform tie was the small plush of his Good Friend, still unused. While most of the memories involving it were not the best, it still held the power to serve as a medium for an entity if he got desperate. In a way, Kim Soleum was thankful hadn’t  got to use it at the park. Life was already a nightmare: having to share it with a stress of worrying about a darkness in your pocket would have only added to it. Gently, he placed it back under the pillow and tucked himself in the blanket, forcing himself to fall asleep, with the lights on as usual. 


As weird as it sounds, waking up to a ghost story was relieving. In a way that knowing exactly how the events were supposed to play out and seeing them go according to the script put Kim Soleum at ease. That was until the whole sequence of events with agent Bronze, his colleagues and a mess of exchanging tags. Now, after lying on the ground dripping blood on the cheap school linoleum and saying tearful goodbyes of reassurance to Bison and Bronze to be finally left alone, he was back on track with his plan. 

With his actions timed by the sweet candy in his mouth, Kim Soleum had no time to hesitate putting on the Sekwang High School uniform. He knew all the exploits he needed, but still, there was the dread of the fundamental attribute he was lacking for success.
 
Courage. 

The dawning realisation hit with the regret: if only his visit to the tattoo parlour was at a better timing, if only he had better items, if only- 

No. 

There was no time to mellow: there were things he had to do no matter how it made him feel. Still, aware of what was to come, Kim Soleum rearranged his thoughts, sitting down in the corner of the infirmary, taking out all of his items from the pockets. A couple of cheerful stickers (too few to matter), the “we can help” button for the most dire situations, the blood stained knife from the set, the wrapped Good Friend vessel and finally, the main culprit of his regrets - the set of temporary tattoos from the studio. Picked by the Host. 

‘Ugh.’ 

There was no telling what effect it would have. Initially, the plan was to get the one quality Soleum was lacking the most - courage, bravery, borderline delusional valour. And now, he was put to a choice: should he trust in the blind choice Braun handed him. The parlour was known to only provide positive effects through their work, even if they might be insignificant or at times useless with the person’s pre-existing set of skills. 

Right now, with what he was about to put himself through, Kim Soleum needed any help he could take. If the tattoo was to prove useless, the Good Friend was right there. Calling in for a backup from a darkness to fight the main boss of the school was a decision he calculated way before diving in.

Carefully, he took one of the tattoo sheets in his hand and examined the design: a tangled up camera film spilling out, tied into a knot at the end. Gently, he picked at the corner of it, peeling it off. 

It ripped in half. 

With a panicked gasp, Soleum looked at the tear: it cut right at the knot. 

‘No, no please!’

Using a damaged item in a ghost story was one of the stupidest ideas one could have. There was no saying whether even something as safe as the Moonlight tattoo work would not turn on him after he ripped it. 

But once he calmed down and looked at it again, picking up another sheet of the temporary tattoos, Soleum noticed something. Upon closer inspection the end of the tear was weirdly neat, clean, a perfect cut. He looked at the untouched design on another sheet: there in the dim light of the infirmary lamp he saw it - the tattoo was perfectly split with a cut right at the spot where the film was tying into a peculiar shape. 

‘It’s two pieces!’

Lightly touching at the edge of the tattoo he thought was ripped, he saw that it came off easily. So, he thought, it was intentional. 

‘Now what…’

Clearly, the tattoo was intended for application directly on the skin of a user. The fact that it was split could only imply it had a shared effect between either multiple parts of the body, or perhaps…

With nothing better to try, feeling the size of the candy in his mouth decreasing with every second he spent thinking on it, Soleum slapped the tattoo with the film on his arm, feeling it stick immediately. 

Then, he picked up the plush from his pocket, still wrapped in his tie. Of course, a thought of trying it on a student appeared in his head, but there was still a chance something called a “Good Friend” might end up a less haunting ally if this worked. 

Unwrapping a part of the bunny’s belly now peeking bright pink through the black tie, Kim Soleum carefully attached the second part of the tattoo right onto the fabric. Surprisingly, it stuck right in. 

Right then, he felt the overwhelming sense of… something indescribable. The best way to put it was - a channel opening. Yes. It was as if his mind just saw more, heard more, and all of it was through the lens of a secondary camera. 


[What a late call, Friend.]




A darkness didn’t need sleep. A darkness in a human body was, unfortunately, tied to human needs. 

Braun’s sleep schedule was not perfect, not with the work at Daydream, and still, he took having rest seriously to avoid complications with the fragile body he had to inhabit. When he was off the clock he was not to be bothered. 

There was no exception to the rule. 

So how did it come to it that suddenly, his eyes shot open and he sat up in his bed, in full darkness, more alert than ever. It was as if a phone rang in his skull. Not in a way a sound would jerk him awake, more so in a sense of “need”. Someone was “calling to him”.

With a deep breath, shaking the sleep off, he closed his eyes.
 
Darkness. 

Not just in a sense of absence of light, but also in a sense of the realm embracing his now more connected body. Around it he felt corporeal human warmth. With his senses sharpened by the new line that seemed to have opened, he heard a voice that wasn’t audible. An emotion, a thought. It was not shaped in precise words, not spelled in a way that could be put through speech or painted. It was a sheer communication through understanding. 

‘I am alone.’

‘I am afraid.’

‘I need help.’

‘Please, work.’


Braun smiled. If this was a call - he would recognise the breaths on the other side. If it was a letter - the handwriting was unmistakable. If those emotions were put into a painting - he’d trace down each and every brushstroke with the precision of the artist’s moves. 


[What a late call, Friend.]



Kim Soleum jumped at the voice in his head. The words were not said out loud: they were echoing in his head alone and yet he could somehow pinpoint just where they were coming from. The toy. 

“Mr. Host?”

[I see you found yourself trapped in a darkness at such a late hour. I am sorry your rest is interrupted.]

‘Ah, right…’

“I am sorry I called you so… late. I was not sure how this would work.”

[Believe me, I would not have picked a call I didn’t deem worth picking, Friend.]

That was… weirdly reassuring. 

[So now please, do tell me what bothers you, Mr. Roe Deer. After all, it seems I am not exactly privy to seeing the full picture.]

Kim Soleum hesitated. Of course the Host’s complaint was clear: the fabric of the tie wrapped around the toy must have not allowed him to see things in the base sense of it. 
Still, he had no intent of letting the Host see him. It was obvious that Roe Deer’s identity was something of high value as a bargaining chip he could use. If for helping him in this darkness Braun was to ask for something in return - then he at least had something up his sleeve. He would tackle the issues as they come up.
 
“I am in this darkness and I have a theory I strongly believe, that would allow me to get a higher grade essence than the original manual suggests.”

Dismissing the Host’s near direct request, he hid the wrapped plush in his chest pocket and picked up the rest of his things. It was time to move. 

[Oh, once again, you seem so knowledgeable, Friend.]

There was a light vibration of Braun’s chuckle that went right through Soleum’s head, leaving goosebumps on his arms. He shrugged it off. 

“Yes, and I am facing two issues with achieving that ending.”

[And you call for the help of your friend in the time of need?]

Soleum hesitated, about to leave the infirmary and step outside. He did need help for this. He had to do what it takes. 

“Yes. Please.”


Silence fell on the line.

[I am at your service as always, Friend.]


There was a weird sense of sudden realisation that crushed the heavy weight of Soleum’s shoulders. A sense of clarity. Something that could be called an eureka effect.

It all came to a perfect conclusion. Every question had an answer and every issue found its solution. It was perfect. 

Braun was perfect.
 
‘This might just work.’

No, he thought. 

‘This will work.’


“I want to host a show.”


Ringing silence hung on the line. Still, Soleum waited patiently with no regard for the candy melting in his mouth. 

[Consider me your loyal audience, Mr. Host.]

Opening the door wide, stepping into the empty hall of the school corridor, Soleum carried on, in an unspoken communication they had established. 

‘I have never done this before so I was hoping you would help me.’

[My, oh my. There is nothing a loyal entertainer would love more than to talk someone so enthusiastic through their first time.]

Soleum disregarded the comment. 

[But you have to first sell me on the premise of the show,] there was the sweetness in Braun’s voice that coated stronger than the aftertaste of the nostalgia candy. 

“I want to capture the audience: first, by hiding from their sight until it’s the right time to appear,” just as Kim Soleum said that, the familiar sense of weight dropped on his shoulders. In the faint scent of cologne he recognised it - Braun’s coat. Just then, it felt as if all the lights in the corridor were avoiding him, hiding his presence in the back shadows behind the scene. The realisation that Braun’s powers could reach him here, in the darkness, with the Host away, was as comforting as it was purely terrifying. Just what sort of a connection did he establish. Was it the tattoo, or was it in some way the effect of the Good Friend toy amplifying it into something… unknown. He swallowed nervously, but walked through the crowds of teenagers on the floor of the grade he was not a part of. Hidden away, it was easier to reach his first stop: the locked door to the storage room. Guarded by a couple high schoolers. 

‘Then, when the crowd doesn’t suspect a thing,’ he continued to Braun in his mind. ‘I want to approach them in a way that makes them feel an immediate sense of trust in me.’

[Mmm,] Braun murmured in content. [Perfect setup to get the guests to open up to you and your game!]

The lights before Soleum lit up again: he felt himself seen. He felt himself observed. The eyes were on him, expecting, waiting, attentively anticipating. 

‘I wish to convince them I am about to give them the best performance they have ever seen.’

“Hey, you should not be here, kid,” one of the high schoolers started. 

[I believe you have all the talent for that, my dear Host.]

Soleum looked at the teenager, and mumbled a response:

“Seniors asked me to fetch a ladder from the storage room. May I take it with me.”
Immediately, the kid chuckled and stepped aside. 

“Ok lol.”

[A perfectly executed act, a small skit to warm the crowd up for the main part of the show.]

Kim Soleum walked to the storage room, closing the door behind. It was all aligning perfectly. In his hands he now held the key to his success: The name tag decoration. 

Time to proceed with the show. 

[But what is this great finale you have planned, Mr. Host?]

Braun’s voice felt lazier, like that of a viewer thoroughly enjoying the show. 

‘It would be rude to spoil it, wouldn’t it?’

Braun laughed.

Noticing the emptiness of the corridor as he left the storage room, he was prepared to head for the last stop on his way out of the darkness. Once again hidden from sight, he avoided a group of Daydream employees with flashlights and climbed the last staircase on his way to the auditorium. The announcement for the graduation ceremony echoed in the school, but to Soleum’s ear it felt more like a side cue from the set manager. After all, he was acting now and it would be a shame to break his role or disturb his performance. 

[Perhaps, a hint would be nice? A tease for your audience to stay intrigued?]

Kim Soleum considered it carefully. Braun’s voice felt clear to him, without the effect of the TV speaker his words had back when he was contaminated. Still, there was no telling what side of the Host was best to play along with: human or contamination. A living being or a power driven shadow. 

Perhaps, it was both. 

“The great finale of this show will show liberation, salvation, joy of freedom,” he said out loud, approaching the wide doors decorated with graduation posters. “With triumph over evil, a brutal death of oppression, the fall of a usurper.”

He opened the doors in one swift motion, the lights of the theatre room shining bright on him, every eye of the faceless distorted audience peering right into him. 

It would have been the most intimidating, terrifying experience, was it not for the goal he had in mind, supported by the guidance of his co-Host.

‘There is the audience I have to win over.’
He walked right towards the stage, ignoring the murmurs of incomprehensible language trailing him from every side. 

[It looks to me like you have all it takes to have them bent to your every rule. I am anticipating a grand show, so I will take my seat in the back now.]

It felt like the receiver on the other side of the line was put on hold: the call still going, yet the Host gave the spotlight and his full attention to the performance. 

Grabbing the microphone, Kim Soleum prepared himself. 

‘Teenagers are a highly impressionable audience.’
