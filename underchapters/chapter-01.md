Part 1: The amusement park, first meeting

Getting dropped into a world of ghost stories is one thing. Getting stuck with a company actively exploring them - is a whole different story. 

The murderous trial at the subway, a tag with a ghost for three days, clowns creeping at you from paintings: for Kim Soleum it just kept getting worse and worse. It was all due time to accidentally find himself fully separated from his squad, left alone in a darkness now calling him a good child, denying him any escape from the world of “joy and fun”. 

Holding onto a bunch of items he found in a hurry around the local gift shop, he gripped hard at a small pink plush before laying it carefully on the floor. Soleum was desperately trying to recall all the parts of the ritual. The good friend was his last resort to getting out of this in one piece. Connecting himself to a darkness, binding himself to it… after all it didn’t matter, he was in too deep to care for such details. Whatever would get him through. After all, what’s the point of the Wish Ticket if you are dead half way through the goal?

Soleum let out a sigh before wiping sweat off his face, dripping from under his mask. What does he have to lose? Kneeling before the toy, he carefully rolled his tie around its soft body, wrapping the bunny in what he hoped would serve as a conduit for the connection.

Right, now to light it up… Soleum leaned further in above the toy, rising the spoon full of salt to his mouth-

“That’s one elaborate setup you have here.”

Losing his balance over the startle, Soleum’s knees gave up and he barely held himself before tumbling down. The salt in his mouth burnt his throat, causing the man to cough, desperately trying not to throw up. 

He jolted to the side, fully expecting a hit on his head, a grip on his arm, anything. The mascots… He didn’t expect them to catch up to him here. No, they couldn’t have. Instead he was greeted with a wide smile. 

“So, you must be our lost little employee.”

Sitting in front of him was not a mascot. A blond man towered above him, watching intently as Soleum struggled to push himself upright. 

“Need a hand up?”

The man’s eyes seemed filled with compassion bordering pity. Soleum sat up next to the man, glancing over the long black coat, a well fitted brown suit and slicked back hair.
 Pausing to take in the dark brown eyes that were intently staring right at his mask, almost sending a chill down Soleum’s spine. He felt seen, investigated, dissected right there on the spot.

“I am fine,” Soleum felt like his own position was humiliating enough, taking a hand from someone he didn’t even know was not making it any better. 

Did he say “employee”, Soleum recalled. Could it be… 

“Are you another employee? I thought it was just us-”

He stood up, watching in the corner of his eye the figure rise up. Standing up straight the man gave off an eerily calm energy, all the while being in this cursed themepark that drives you crazy if you aren’t lucky to die quicker.

“Well,” the man started, his hands calmly tucked into the pockets of his coat. An employee wearing casual was so unusual it only seemed to fit the madness of the darkness they were in. 

‘Could he be one of the people stuck here since the last time?’ Soleum thought.

 “How can one not answer a call so polite?”

“A call?” Kim Soleum shook off the dirt from his suit and looked around checking if his possessions were still on: the briefcase was just to the side, the ritual items still on the floor under the curious inspection of the “fellow employee”, his mask still firmly attached to his face. Wait a minute… Did the blond man not have his mask on?

“Love the setup you have going on here,” he pointed at the plush doll still ominously lifelessly dropped onto the floor in the centre of a makeshift pentagram.

“Oh…” Soleum felt a mix of embarrassment and concern. How was he going to explain casually attempting a ritual that was definitely beyond knowledge of a barely month-in employee?

“Please, don’t mind that,” he continued, trying his best to sound non-chalant and confident. “I was considering attempting a ritual.”

By his logic, if the man in front of him was indeed an agent - he had to be a lot higher in the hierarchy and more experienced. There was no way of hiding the truth from him. A rookie would also dismiss it after a couple curious questions. In the worst case scenario, however, if the man was something conjured out of the darkness itself, some sick form of a local employee or a trapped alien ghost… The ritual and excuses would be the least of Soleum’s concerns.

“A ritual? You seem to know quite a lot, Mr, Roe Deer,” something in his calm voice clearly gave away fake amusement.
“Oh well,” what was the point of circling around it? As one employee to another, he continued: “Choosing between that or dying here…”

If there was one mutual understanding between anyone associated with the fieldwork at Daydream it was about the expendability of one’s life.

“I didn’t expect the company to attempt a rescue of a new employee, so my last resort was a less conventional method.”

Soleum did his best to sound stern in his voicing: playing a survival instinct driven maniac was his best shot. At the end of the day everyone knew: it is at the closest to your death that you give it your all.

He could feel the man staring at him, studying with a leisurely expression.

“Oh, and what’s that?” 

Kim Soleum paused for a moment, thinking what the best wording would be to test where his “colleague” stands. 

“Befriending a darkness.”



The man, clearly set back, stood silent for just a moment, his eyes shot slightly wider, as if taking in what he just heard. 

“Oh,” he whispered. “Oh my-”

A wide grin spread on his face, quickly covered with a palm of his hand, still peeking through the corners of his eyes.

Soleum felt a pinch of anxiety at his already racing heart. Did he mess up? Was this superior going to take this at face value and report him to the company? He now had to figure out where to go from here, potential excuses rushing through his head trying to set up an escape path for any possible outcome. 

Meanwhile, the taller man walked further, leaning to pick up the abandoned little plush rabbit off the ground. Fidgeting with it in his hand, he walked back to Soleum. 

“So, you say Daydream sent you?” a pathetic tactic to avert the direction of the conversation elsewhere to avoid saying too much…

“Yes,” the man casually answered, still squishing the slightly dusty toy in his gloved fingers. “The name’s Braun.”

Braun beamed a smile at Soleum.

‘This is absurd. Is this a test, a joke, or just darkness playing a trick?’

“I thought using our real names is not advised…” Soleum started, once again taking in the casual attire and the absence of Braun’s mask. “Speaking of, where is your ma-”

“Oh, Mr. Roe Deer, you must have dropped this!” Braun cut him short, extending his arm forward with the toy, basically pushing it into Soleum’s chest. “It would be a shame to leave something so cute and small behind!”

Just something about his demeanour, the casual way in his every move, made Soleum feel wary and alert. Almost like his intuition screaming at him that being alone in this might have been better…

WIth the thoughts racing in his mind, he did not notice the man stepping closer and reaching out to his face with his hand. Startled at the touch, Kim Soleum almost jumped out of instinct.

“And speaking of masks,” Braun started. “I wonder what’s hiding behind this one…”

Soleum felt the long fingers just barely scratch his face with the warm leather of his gloves, creeping underneath a corner of his deer mask, ever so slightly lifting it up. The connection between the dark material of the mask and his own nerves sent an electric twist up his whole body, a tight pinch of pain numbingly spreading all over him. Out of reflex he slapped the hand off his face, a heavy breath escaping him as his eyes widened in shock at the feeling. 

He knew that the masks had a strange way of developing a connection between themselves and the wearers, and he had heard that it was never a good idea to force a mask off someone’s face. Not like he ever intended to try, but now he understood just what it felt like: almost like your actual skin being penetrated involuntarily, alien cold and heat spreading into your veins at the same time. It felt almost like penetration aiming for your own essence. He felt like the man’s fingers almost scratched at his very soul…

“Please!” he steadied his breath, but knew very well that his voice still sounded agitated. “If you wish to talk without the masks, we can do that outside of this darkness.”

There was a line between the workplace harassment he just witnessed at the park firsthand in his temporary team, and raw violation of the rules assigned to every employee on recruitment. It felt… more personally violating to have your essence bared to a darkness.

Braun stepped back, with a somewhat apologetic shrug of his shoulders.

“Hm,” he smiled looking at Soleum with a sort of interest that would make you want to hide out of sight. “I suppose then I will need to sort this out quickly.”

In his arms Soleum spotted something dark. A mask, he guessed, disregarding the odd shape. One again, some weird instinct at the back of his head screamed at him. What was it again…

Braun pulled the mask to his face, the shape covering the entirety of his upper face. As he turned around Soleum could see his own reflection in the pitch blackness of it.

As if a smooth screen of a tv was facing him. It would be impossible to say where Braun was looking, the entirety of his eyes covered. A small bright face popped in the corner of the “screen”, almost as if to look at Kim Soleum. A small smiling emoji. 

And there it clicked. 

Tall figure, blond hair, friendly borderline patronising demeanour, and finally - an odd TV shaped mask unlike any other in the company. 

The Host. 

They called him so for the combination of all of the above. And also for his upper standing in the rankings. It was as if the man was a local celebrity, a folktale, almost a ghost story among the employees to be whispered during lunch breaks.

The man was a legend. It was not exactly known how and when he came to the company but what Soleum had heard made him question just how he had never been recorded on the wiki. Just a few stories made Braun sound like a weird ammature attempt at a power creep insert, so Soleum disregarded it as either empty rumours that over glorified the man, or a figure of questionable significance to the flow of his exploration. Who could have thought he would find himself standing right next to The Host. 

It was believed he had cleared multiple A ranks stories and was mostly sent as a fill-in for the elite teams. Encountering him on duty was almost associated with a bad omen of higher ups expecting the story to have closer to singular survivor. Rumours had it not only was he close to his Wish Ticket by now (“He had to!”, “For sure!”), but his work methods were less of assistance and more of cleanup, less of exploration and more of- “Darkness abuse”, Soleum recalled someone say in the newbies group chat.

“Please, hold on to that plush for me, Mr. Roe Deer.”

For a moment there it almost looked like the mask became one with the man’s face. Braun’s back straightened, his figure towering over Kim Soleum even more, now with the pitch black “screen” for a face the man looked purely intimidating. His wide smile only sent a shiver down Soleum’s spine, adding to the unsettling feeling of being stuck between the Darkness of the amusement park, relentlessly set on keeping him here, and the infamous pride of DayDream. Somehow the obvious choice felt less so in the moment.

“Shall we get out of here?” 

Braun extended his arm as if in an invitation to dance.

Soleum turned around and grabbed some of his belongings from the ground, shaking dust and dirt as much as he could from his suit. Now next to the agent he felt ashamed of his appearance. Was he going mad with fear even bothering about something like looks at that moment? 

Soleum looked at the extended hand and instead grabbed onto the plush in the other. 

“I will hold onto this then, Mr. Host.”

Braun chuckled and gave a last look around the room before stepping out of the building back into the bright sunlight of the amusement park. In his black coat he felt very out of place in the bright setting of cute and cheerful toys. But the unchanging emote on his screen kept slowly looking around, as creepily as was befitting the nature of the place. 

No matter what he had heard about the man, Soleum had no intention to let his guard down. No one in this company was sane, why would someone as presumably powerful as Braun be any better. If anything, this felt like an even more exhausting part of this exploration was starting. 

“Do you have something in mind for our escape, Mr. Host?” 

Braun turned around as if caught in thought. 

“Not particularly. After all I was never intending to get out with anyone but myself,” he said in an ominously cheerful tone. 

So, the rumours were true: rescues were not his specialty. Then what is it, Soleum tried to reason. If the exceptional employee was to be sent alone into a darkness it only brought up records from the Bureau, but Daydream never operated with the idea of closing their profit generators. Why would he be here?

Ah.

Loot. 

It had to be. 

When the darkness just went through a cycle of activity with the set amount of players, it would make sense to quickly send in a couple capable employees to quickly scout potential items around the restoring area. At times the said look could also be custom equipment or even personal belongings of elite employees.

“So, Mr. Roe Deer,” Braun’s voice called him out from his thoughts. “Any ideas? You are a lot more knowledgeable than I am. I put you in my care.”

His smile felt equally genuine and fake at the same time. Almost like a smile of encouragement and patronising over a little kid. A bloody rabbit, the blue dragon, and now this. One, two, three forces Kim Soleum wished he didn’t have to deal with. 

Oh.

Wait.

“In fact, I might have a thought.”

Soleum walked to a nearby info stand and looked at the map.

Yes. This made no sense, and therefore it was clearer to him than ever before. 

“If you are done with your matters, Mr. Host,” he said to the man who looked somewhat bored waiting for his decisions. “I would love it if we could both leave this park.”



“So, Mr. Roe Deer, you believe there to be a third area to the park despite there clearly not being one indicated?” 

Soleum was already feeling somewhat frustrated. He didn’t really need the senior’s help to escape, he hoped at least, so now him following around was starting to feel annoying. After all, wasn’t Braun here on some other job of his own? And now his attention and indulgence felt weirdly patronising. 

“Yes, after all, we arrived in three teams, each set to arrive at a different set of attractions. Then how come there were only two areas available to us?” 

Kim Soleum seemed to be thinking out loud. 

“One red, one blue, there still had to be yellow, for the yellow group. This just makes sense, doesn’t it?” he turned to look at Braun who seemed to be listening patiently to his every ramble. He nodded with a smile. 

“Admittedly, you are not wrong, my friend. However, where do we find it and why has it not revealed itself to us.”

“It’s fine, I already know where it is,” Soleum replied with a confident smile. 

Braun chuckled, as if in admiration over his confidence. 

“Then, lead the way, friend.” 

However, instead of walking in his determined direction, Soleum stopped in his tracks, causing Braun to abruptly comply. 

“There is another matter at hand…”

Literally, he thought, at hand… His new tattoo. There was something ominous about the attention he was getting from the blue mascot. Of course, it was better to be treated as an esteemed guest here than in the red area, but he was already certain that this tattoo of his will cause issues to his escape. Braun, however, should be free to leave. The Host gave him a quick analysing look, as if trying to read the situation. 

“I think the mascots here want me to stay in the park as a resident member. I have already completed the rides, but I was rewarded with this, for being a good child.” 

He pulled the sleeve of his jacket up, showing the Host his tattoo. After all, having a witness to the origin of the mark would also be beneficial for his explanations when they leave. If they leave, that is. 

“Socius, interesting,” Braun grabbed his wrist with a bit of force, turning it around, probing at the skin with his fingers. Soleum squinted at the feeling, but didn’t object. After all, it was a way to check if the tattoo had any unpleasant surprises upon contact with someone else. There seemed to be none, so Braun let go of his hand.

“This seems to be to your advantage, Mr. Roe Deer. Your luck is impeccable.”

Kim Soleum shook his wrist, feeling light numbing pain where the man gripped it, hoping it wouldn't bruise on top of the already blue font. 

“Never thought of getting stuck in a murderous park all alone as a form of luck, Mr. Host,” he added the title in a borderline mocking tone. He never thought getting on friendly terms with a senior at the company would make him so… displeased, to say the least. 

“Well, first of all, you aren’t alone. Your luck brought me to you, at the right time at the right spot, just as you were about to ‘call for a friend’, am I wrong?” 

Soleum had to admit, the timing was one of the most bizarre things in his life. Second to, of course, winning that first prize at the pop up. If Braun really was on a clean up and looting mission, there was no telling how frequently such would be dispatched. And to his blue area directly. 

Kim Soleum felt a little embarrassed at his outburst in that department. 

“Second, the mark of membership, as you call it, will likely give you more privileges as you might think of misfortunes. Has anyone told you that Darknesses intervened, my Friend?” 

Braun’s eyes squinted as he smiled at Soleum expecting an answer that would make him proud. Like a teacher, about to continue the lesson regardless of just how smart the student thinks himself to be. Kim Soleum, however, was eager to use this as an excuse to shake off suspicions of his own knowledge after the ritual incident, the part of which he still carried in his pocket in a form of the plush.

“I am not sure I am following, Mr. Host,” he said quietly, still rubbing on the tattoo. In a way, this was a great chance to learn. 

Braun’s smile grew suspiciously wider. Was he not buying the unknowing act? 

“Darknesses exist in their own realms inside one global scope of the matter of Darkness itself. They are one and yet unique and independent. They are not a hivemind, yet are able to detect the presence of each other, interfere and possess elements of each other’s stories.” 

The way he put it made sense and yet didn’t, Soleum thought. So while he perfectly understood how it was born through the mentions of one ghost story in another on the wiki, it was fascinating to see the “in universe lore”. Faking ignorance was also fitting here. 

“So, are you saying that this tattoo might not only put the local darkness into affection towards me, but also assist me elsewhere?” 

It was always nice to confirm his suspicions, even if he had no reasons to trust Braun on the spot. 

“It would be interesting to check it out later, don’t you think so, my Deer Friend?”

“I will keep you posted, senior,” Soleum concluded, continuing his walk. “But now, this tattoo attracts the attention of every dragon mascot around.”

Just to prove this, he spotted a mascot and started approaching, having warned Braun to stay behind to avoid any potential complications. After all, The Host could have been long added to the unwanted children list for all he knew. 

Kim Soleum approached the Blue Dragon holding a bunch of balloons. For all he knew, they could have been filled with organs or acid, but he thought himself safer now that he was both outside the red zone and with a membership title. 

“Excuse me, could I have one, please?” he politely asked, extending his hand so that the tattoo would be visible. 

The Dragon showed no hesitation or interest in the mark, already extending his paw with a balloon as if knowing by default that Soleum belonged here. 

“Good child,” it said, leaving a small pat on his head before turning back to its duties. 

Soleum waddled the balloon in the air, checking if it was safe.

“Red would suit you better, friend.” 

Soleum spun on his heels, almost bumping into the mascot out of startle. Braun was right behind him, having completely ignored his instructions. 

“The red area was out of balloons,” Kim Soleum gritted through his teeth. 

Braun smiled. 

“But at least it seems like your plan to befriend a darkness has worked,” he nodded towards the mascot which was now keeping its distance from the two. Was Braun really on a naughty child list, or-

“Have to admit, your methods are as peculiar as they are intriguing. But please, friend, refrain from showing such kindness and affection to just any darkness.”

Soleum looked at Braun, who seemed distant in his thoughts. 

“Well,” he handed the balloon to Braun, “I do not intend to check how many darknesses will jump on the first opportunity to be my friends regardless, be certain of it. Doubt there will be a line forming any time soon.”

Braun laughed quietly, letting go of the balloon and watching it return back to the hands of the mascot. 



Together, they walked to where Kim Soleum expected (hoped) the entrance to the yellow area would be. 

“Alright, so now, I need you to keep an eye and tell me if any mascot is around, I should not be seen doing this.”

Just as Soleum was about to start breaking the structure not expecting any help from the senior, he heard a shuffle behind himself. Then, something huge and heavy engulfed his whole body. He sensed a subtle scent of cologne and coffee coming from the warm coat around him.

Trying to turn around he struggled to keep it on his shoulders. Even now it was already dragging the edges on the ground due to their noticeable height difference. 

“What?” 

Braun looked at him, inspecting what had to be a rather ridiculous sight. Instead, he nodded.

“With this on, for some time, you will not be seen by the mascots at all.” The man leaned closer, almost bumping Soleum in the head with his forehead, squinting his eyes. Way too close, Soleum swallowed. “Even I am having a hard time perceiving you, friend. Be at peace and do what you must.” 

With that Braun walked away and leaned onto railing, as if deep in his troughs.
 
‘Custom equipment,” Soleum thought. The senior equipment had to be quite high level. And it was fitting for it to be a form of an escape tool, considering the man seemed to be doing solo missions. Adjusting the coat to sit tighter on his figure, Soleum went to work. 


It took quite a bit of hard work and Kim Soleum was getting annoyed at the lack of assistance, but as soon as the entrance to the yellow zone showed up, he eagerly turned around to share his discovery just to notice Braun once again right by his side. 

‘He is awfully good at hiding his presence. Does he even need the coat at all? I, on the other hand, would have so much use for it…’ Just as the thought went through his head, he felt the coat taken off his shoulders. Suddenly, a chill went through his back as the warmth was gone. Braun out the coat back on, and then extended a part of it as if offering Soleum to-

“Get in,” the man said with the enthusiasm of someone genuinely on a walk at an amusement park. “We both need to be hidden if we want to break some rules, right, Mr. Good Child?” 

The emote on his screen face popped a wink and Soleum sighed in defeat, somehow fitting himself under the coat, uncomfortably squished between the heavy fabric and the man. This was still better than the red zone, he had to remind himself like a mantra. 

“Shall we?” 

Braun led their walk as they stepped through the debris of the yellow entrance, now in the area resembling an abandoned resort. Behind them, a chill creeped straight through the coat onto Soleum’s back, but he had no way to look behind where he was. 

“They are behind us, not yet noticing our little mischief, Friend.”

Just the way he said it was so eerily calm, Braun sounded borderline amused. 

“They know, but they can’t see,” he continued. His voice felt more soothing, almost like he was trying to set Soleum to focus, soothing his worry. “Keep walking, no matter what I can take care of the pursuit.”

Soleum didn’t need to be told twice, he saw what he needed. 

“There,” he pointed at an exit machine. Now all he had to do was figure out how to restart it and scan his ticket. Quickly, he made Braun get them right to it. “Just give me a moment.” 
Braun didn’t say anything, patiently giving him the time, while Soleum was certain their pursuit was getting closer. With shaking fingers, he opened the panel and started fidgeting with the hardware. 

“Onece again, Friend, your knowledge is amusing to me,” Braun remarked, leaning closer to examine his swift work with some loose cables. With one last click, the entrance lights lit up. 

“Done!” 

He could not believe it, pulling up his ticket from his pocket, feeling the lone plush still tucked in there. Soleum scanned it quickly, seeing the gates open, turning then to face the senior.
 
“Mr. Host, your ticke-”

But before he could finish the sentence, the strong gloved hands pushed him through the gates. 



Soleum landed on the cold ground of the conference room, taking in a long breath of stale and yet so welcoming air. In front of him, his colleagues were startled to see him.
