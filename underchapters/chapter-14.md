Disconnected, connected again: his consciousness was still a mess, trying to make sense of the feelings going through his own body. The touch on his wrists felt so close and yet so distant, bringing him back to reality and sending his mind floating at the same time. Desperately, Soleum grabbed onto the gloved hand and squished it with as much force as he could. It hurt, it must have, he thought. But was a darkness ever concerned with such trivial matters? Was a darkness capable of feeling physical pain? What about psychological? Everything in his mind has become one giant tangled mess of thoughts passing through at an impossible speed. Trying to grab onto one, he let an endless stream of questions rush through his head: why was Braun’s hand so warm, so human, why was his mind drawn to  what would otherwise mortify him on the spot. Why were his hands reaching into the deep pitch black darkness around the unknown, dangerous and forbidden. Why couldn’t he bring himself to get away, untangle himself from this madness and run for his life, desperately looking for an escape from where he himself led the remainder of his humanity. 

[What’s on your mind, Friend?]

There was a terrifying ability in Braun’s voice to pull Soleum from his throughs and leave him exposed. The same way a professional talk show host would be able to pry even the darkest secrets out of their guest, put on display in the bright lights of the stage for everyone to see. But now it was just the two of them, and all of Soleum’s carefully tucked in secrets could slip out with just one word, one touch, one careless breath. There were no lights, no audience, just the piercing eyeless stare of the pitch black screen, reflecting his own face back at him: dishevelled, tired, with a hint of something neither of them dared to address. 

Replying was pointless. At this point, they have both been deeper into each others’ mind than was humanly possible. Kim Soleum has seen the deepest levels of Braun’s very essence, and Braun has heard every single unspoken thought that would otherwise never surface for anyone to hear. There was a connection between them so sick and twisted it was starting to feel natural. 

Gently, Soleum let go of the now exposed polished wood of Braun’s TV head, feeling the tickle of his void-like blood stick to his fingers unwilling to let go. 

“Does it hurt?” 

How weird was it to realise, openly and without attempts to deceive himself, that those words were born out of genuine humane concern. 

[What exactly?]

What exactly? Wearing human skin? Living outside of your own darkness? Having yourself stuck in the loop of desperately trying to rebuild yourself barely leaving a dent? Unable to pick one question, Kim Soleum remained silent, his eyes studying the black screen until a familiar smiling face popped up on it. 

[It does.]

Lying is as natural to an actor as waking up in the morning and starting the day with a cup of coffee. Braun’s very essence was that of an actor, that of a liar, and now even his words could not catch up to his own expressions. 

 “Why did you leave?” 

Why would a darkness go for such a step? A darkness is bound to the role, the scene and the play it is set to perform. Just how painful must it be for Braun to break through those limitations? Was existence as it is so miserable…

“You said it yourself back then at the restaurant, that as long as the core of the darkness remains intact it can nurture itself to health, so surely-”

Braun’s grip on his hands tightened, bordering on pain, but Soleum continued through the itch of it. 

“Surely you can get back to how it was with time, after all you are still here and still yourself?”

The happy face on the screen flashed and vanished, Braun’s emotions hidden behind the thick cover of silence. 

[It is exhausting to live according to rules that you take for essentials just because you have never known better, isn’t it?]

Kim Soleum felt his skin shake in a wild tremble. As if Braun’s hands dug deep into his mind and swirled around in his thoughts, intrusive yet as gentle as if the entirety of Soleum’s mind had been mapped out and memorised by those fingers long ago.

Was this a direct provocation? A challenge? A push to come to senses?

Like a tight string unable to bear the tension, something inside him snapped and in a rush, Kim Soleum pushed himself to his feet, dashing away from the couch. Trying to catch his rapid shaken breathing, he saw Braun’s colossal figure rise up. Now, in his real form, the Host really reminded him just how the whole world he was permitted to stay in, was truly not his. It was not human. It was never made for a human to be in. 

“I want to get out.”

Not a request. His thoughts would no longer be shaken. 

“I want to go home.”

Braun crossed the distance between them in one step. Everything in Soleum’s body screamed to run, to flee, to cut this connection they had at whatever cost, and yet - he stood unmoving, covered in the shadow cast over Braun’s shoulders. He has been kept safe, as promised: untouched by the shades of the studio, comfortable, his wounds healed and his mind left at peace and yet the scratching feeling deep in his chest was so strong it almost felt like something was going to rip him from the inside at any moment. Something inside of him, as if a whole different voice, was tired of calling to action and instead, took upon itself to rip through his ribcage. Feeling his heartbeat fasten, his breaths shaking, Soleum grabbed onto the fabric of his shirt, trying his best not to bed in half in the pressuring sense of panic washing over him. No, not now, he had to stand his ground. If it was Braun’s doing, conscious or not, he had to fight back. 

“I want to g-”

[What is home, Friend?]

Braun’s voice felt unchanged, calm, comforting, and thence so much more intimidating.

[What is home to you, humans?]

Carefully, as if to not make any abrupt movements, Braun walked a small circle around him, inspecting, taking in every breath and shake of his exhausted body. 

[You are not in your senses, Friend. You need to eat, you need to res-]

Cutting him off, Kim Soleum grabbed onto the fabric of Braun’s sleeve, pulling him closer, so close the tip of his nose could almost touch the cold dark screen.

“Home is where I belong. This is your domain, not mine, let me go…” as if realising the anger behind his own voice, Soleum shivered, adding softly, “please…”

Braun didn’t move.

[Humans are fascinating, Mr. Roe Deer.]

Slowly, Braun knelt down on the floor in front of him, bringing his screen to Soleum’s eye level. Gently, he wrapped his arms around the man’s waist, holding him in place, feeling how strong the small frame under his fingers was shaking with every pained breath. 

“I want to go home…”

There wasn’t any anger left in those words. Instead, all the more hurtful to Braun, they held no emotions behind, empty and uncaring. 

[You asked me if it hurts, Friend. It does.] The hands slowly drew circles on Soleum’s back, as if comforting a child who was hurt by their own mistakes. [For hundreds of nights I met humans of fascinating background, emotional structure and history. The moment they stepped into the studio I knew their affairs, their fears and dreams and all the little buttons to press to get the best of the show I could. But you know, it hurt back then already.]

Once again, this annoyingly calming effect of his voice got Kim Soleum to freeze in place, feeling his own muscles relax under the warm touch.

[As if one day, I realised the very contradiction of myself: how was I to be like them if I could never truly experience all of what makes a human human?]

Kim Soleum felt his mouth twitch. There was so much he felt he had to say and yet not a word could come out. The creeping sense of realisation was dawning on him and he refused to listen to what evident meaning hid behind Braun’s words.

[I spoke to them, I found myself fascinated by all the twists and turns their voices would take when pressed between a friendly chatter and imminent death.]

A brief static shook Braun’s voice and he paused. His TV head leaned closer, resting against Soleum’s stomach, the sharp angles pressing into the soft skin.

[Humans are fascinating. You are so adaptive, you are so free…]

‘No, stop it,’ Soleum wished he could find strength to push away but his own body treacherously felt limp in the arms of the Host. ‘Stop!’ He wanted to cover his ears and hide. 

[You get to choose where you go, what you say. You get to choose who to call a friend and what you like and dislike. The moment the brain of yours finishes its early development you obtain the unexplainable power of thought beyond restraints of any script or rules.]

There it was. Shaking his head to the side, Kim Soleum refused to look down at the motionless figure at his feet: the fundamental human fear of facing something that breaks you very understanding of the universe. In front of him a darkness placed itself on his mercy declaring its suffocating envy of humankind. 

[You want to go home, but what makes a home, Friend?]

His glove hand reached for Soleum’s face and gently pushed it to face him. Closing his eyes shut, Soleum held his breath.

[Is it where you feel safe? Where nothing could harm you?]

A thumb brushed over his cheek.

[Is it where you have the people who care about you the most to keep your interest above their own?]

Slowly, Soleum opened his eyes and looked down into the deep blackness of the screen. Just then what he wouldn’t give to see the familiar smiling face, just to know how Braun truly feels instead of being suspended in the suffocating void of uncertainty.

[Is it the place where you feel like what you do really makes a difference?]

Feeling the last drops of strength leave his body, Soleum’s knees shook and slowly, he knelt on the floor, feeling his frame get enveloped in the familiar warmth. 

[Is there a chance I could ever come any close to imitating what ‘home’ truly means to you?]

There it was, the terrifying moment of being exposed to something that no human was meant to see. In that moment, everything in Soleum’s understanding of it all went crumbling down, taking the last remaining crumbs of sanity along with it. A darkness that wishes to be human, desperately holding on to the closest it ever get to feel the utterly human sense of hope, desire, addiction, and-

Slowly, Soleum pushed himself away from Braun’s grip, looking stern at the screen before him. 

“It hurts you to reside in the human realm, so what do you think will happen to me if I stay here?”

Braun’s shoulders shook at the strength with which the words were thrown at him.

“I can not eat the food here, my sleep is dreamless as if I am losing the grasp over my own mind. What will happen if I go on like this? You can bring me anything you want from the outside world, but it will only remind you of one simple truth, Braun,” raising from the floor, Soleum looked down at Braun, the frown on his face leaving his eyes without any light. “My existence here is only dragged thus far by my connection to the world I truly belong to.”

Braun tried to stand up, but with all the strength he had left, Soleum pushed down on his TV head, keeping him on the ground. How sick of a thought was it that he could be in control of the Host and yet, Braun obeyed, watching him expressionless from under his arm. 

“You know exactly what will happen to me, you can’t play dumb.” Forcefully, he pulled a sleeve of his left arm up, exposing the tattoos in the dim light of his room. “You could feign ignorance if you were just a darkness, but don’t try it with me after all the time we spend out at Daydream. How truly weak must you be that your own contamination struggles to take a hold of one pathetic human!”

With a hard punch, Soleum jumped away from Braun, leaving the slouched figure alone. In the dim light, the dark screen felt even more lifeless than before. In the moment of freedom, Soleum dug his fingers deep into his tattoo, praying he could find it. If it’s contamination Braun wanted, he would give it to him: in a form of something alien, painful and intrusive. Grabbing onto a familiar shape inside his wrist, he pulled out the long forgotten wrapper and in one swift motion, ripped the plastic off, smelling the sweet scent of the blue churro. 

‘What the hell am I even doing?’

Without giving himself time to reconsider, Kim Soleum showed the whole thing into his mouth and felt the sugar dissolve on his tongue. Ready to bite down and swallow, Soleum almost jumped when an arm wrapped around his waist and another covered his whole face, gripping tight into his jaw, keeping it open.

[Spit it out.]

Just those three words alone almost paralysed him on the spot: gone was the comforting tone of Braun’s voice, and gone was the gentle manner of handling him as the fingers on his face tightened the grip. 

[Spit it out.]

If he could shake his head, he would. But all there was left in this iron grip was to moan in protest and try to push the sweets down when a pair of giant fingers pushed into his mouth and pressed hard on the back of his throat. 

Released from the tight hold, Soleum coughed, bending in half onto the soft carpet. His head spinned, as the blue stained saliva and stomach acid uncontrollably spilled from his throat, twisting his insides into a pained knot. 

Barely given time to recover and catch a breath, Soleum was shaken back up, thrown on the couch and flipped to his back. Letting out a pained groan, he tried to crawl away, seeing Braun’s figure tower on top of him, painfully pressing down on his stomach, pinning him down with all the weight of the massive being before him.

[Your actions would insult me, had I not glimpsed into your fear, Friend.]

Soleum tried to push away. Feeling his eyes itch at the pain of tears forming in the corners due to the bruises forming all over his body, he shut his eyes and prepared for the worst. 

‘I died once, this was inevitable.’

Suddenly, the pressure vanished.

[If I ever gave you the idea that I would hurt you, Friend, I beg for your forgiveness.]

There wasn’t that same comforting tone to the Host’s voice, and yet, the words came out so genuine and fragile, Soleum couldn’t help opening his eyes and seeing Braun’s dim screen looking down. Once again, the Host knelt in front of him, on the floor, reluctant to touch him: his arms limp at the sides, fingers scratching at the wooden flooring in anxious human-like patterns. 

[I wish I had even an ounce of your courage, Friend.]

In the sense of the overwhelming silence between them, Kim Soleum tried to speak up, but his scratched throat let out a pained cough. Braun, jumping at the sound, leaned back and reached for the glass of water left untouched over the course of their “dinner”.

Kim Soleum hesitated to take it, but feeling like he has put up enough resistance and tested Braun’s patience enough today, he took the water and chugged it in one take, feeling the cold liquid cover over his upset empty stomach. Curiously, even then, he admitted to feeling no trace of hunger. Throwing the empty glass somewhere to the side of the couch, he finally spoke:

“What will become of me if I stay here?”
Braun’s screen shook in an electric static.

[What would become of you were you to come back out?]

What a hurtful answer, Soleum thought. A question to a question: a challenge he knew he wouldn’t win. Braun had promised him safety, care and comfort and not once had he proven to lie. When it came to Soleum’s side of the argument… Was there any real hope for him out there? The Wish ticket? Was he truly in his mind to think that he could get it, tracing his thoughts back to the creeping cold feeling of his blood draining in a public restroom at the express. You really never know how life would find a way to backstab you just when you think you got yourself prepared for anything.

“I don’t know,” slowly, he places his hands on Braun’s head and hesitantly tried to pull him closer. Unsurprisingly, Braun showed no resistance, following Soleum’s guidance as always, lying his head onto his lap. “I really don’t know.”

Braun’s arms wrapped themselves around Soleum’s waist: feeling now so familiar it didn’t startle the man.

“What you said about humans being adaptive is true. But I am scared, Braun.”

A soft wave of vibration went from Braun’s head all over Soleum’s body. A peculiar yet somehow relaxing sensation, as he traced his fingers over the metal antennae. 

[Sometimes I think I struggle to understand you, Friend. At first I thought maybe it is because I lack human understanding. But now…]

Braun straightened his back, pushing himself up and leaning closer, the wooden top edge of his head resting on Soleum’s forehead. 

[I catch myself thinking you might be closer to a darkness than I am.]

Soleum’s eyes shot wide and opening his mouth to protest, he felt something alien and yet familiar engulf his face, slipping right past his lips. In that small opening, Braun pushed in, his screen opening wide in a pitch black embrace: enveloping Soleum’s face, covering every inch of his skin, inhaling him deeper and deeper, into the endless void of his insides. Caught in it, Soleum shit his eyes and gasped for breath, only feeling the void slide further into his throat, touching, feeling, covering him from the inside, gently pushing onto the sensitive spot in the back of his throat, coating it with a light touch as if apologising for the earlier assault. 
He felt like he was drowning, back there and then, desperate for a breath of air. He felt like he was going blind, praying for a glimpse of light. He felt like his mind was finally giving in to the sweet surrender. It was suffocating, it was terrifying, it was fascinating. It was in that embrace of a darkness that the void pushing deep into his lungs finally managed to cover that little raging voice inside of him and silence its struggles. Gone was the pain, gone was the pressuring anxiety. Soleum felt his heartbeat slow down and after god knows how long, a thought in the back of his head could only ask if he would ever take another breath or if he was beyond the need for it. None of that existed anymore: for the first time in his life, he felt truly free of fear and pain coming with being human.
And then it all shook, sending his mind ringing. 

Pushing himself away, struggling to stand back up, Braun pulled out, breaking their ‘kiss’, leaving Soleum shaking on the couch, suddenly exposed to painful need for air. As soon as he steadied himself, Soleum looked up, seeing traces of the black “blood” on his arms trailing from the couch onto the floor. 

“Braun?”

Still shaken from whatever it was he was just put thought, his voice sounded weak. Braun faced away, his hands holding onto his screen, and upon trying to reply, his frame shook and a scratching metallic sound made Soleum twitch. 

“Braun what- “

Raising his hand to silence him, glove stained in black, Braun turned around.

His screen looked deformed, liquid, melting and dripping onto the floor, black handprints on the sides of his head. Trying to speak only sent more of the “blood” to pour out, and still, keeping the distance between them safe, Braun put up a smiling face into the distorted surface of his messed up screen. 

[My time here is almost up, Friend.]

Just saying that made him half bend and cough out a mouthful of inky black. Soleum, lost for words, tried to reach out, but just like a wounded wild animal, Braun stepped away, pushing his hands into his “face” trying to hold his insides from spilling, moulding them back as much as he can, pushing them into the tv frame. 

[It seems our dinner date is not meant to go without an accident today, I apologise.]

“Stop talking,” Kim Soleum could not handle hearing the inhuman pain in his voice and mastered the courage to stand up and approach faster than Braun could back from him. Grabbing onto his sleeve, he looked deep into the stirring black of his inside. “It hurts, doesn’t it? It hurts just to stay in your own darkness?”

Gently, holding to his own face, Braun nodded and a few drops of his blood spilled onto Soleum’s suit, sipping and staining it, sending a ticklish electric feeling into his skin.

“Go, go and only come back when you are ready.” 

The sternness in his voice made Braun choke a little. 

“I do not want to see you crumble here in front of me.” 

Gently, over a wave blurring his screen, a small smile formed. 

‘How cruel you are, Friend, to show me your concern I have no right to,’ Braun leaned in, his head gently touching Soleum’s before stepping away and heading for the door, a trail of blood tracing behind him. 

‘How cruel you are, to show me yourself in such a state,’ Soleum thought, his fingers brushing over the wet bloodstains on the palms of his hands.

“I won’t go anywhere, so promise to return.”

Pausing before the door, Braun turned around and, placing one of his stained hands on his broad chest, bowed down. At the end of the day, this was not the finale. He would be back after a quick intermission.
