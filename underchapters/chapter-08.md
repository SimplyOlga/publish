Braun was done with a report. He was not new to the idea of having higher ups looming over you, and yet, the frustration of formalities was still dulling the otherwise delightful day ahead. 
Yesterday, Roe Deer and him parted ways on such a pleasant note it made him only so much more excited to return to the lower floors of the building to continue his search. 

“I must say, your company made this chore run much more exciting, friend.” 

Braun purposefully slowed his walk as they were approaching the mall’s exit. He knew that once they step out the memories of their meeting will inevitably grow more and more foggy until disappearing almost entirely. Still, he held hope that with every chance he gets to unknowingly bump into Roe Deer in the office he would be closer to recognising him. An agonisingly optimistic hope, but hope none the less. After all, how long could one man possibly hide from him?

“Chores? I don’t think you got to buy anything in the end,” the Deer mask looked right back at him. Admittedly, there was now more confidence in his voice. Like a wild creature slowly getting tamed. 

“It isn’t always about gaining something material,” Braun responded. Quickly, his eyes shifted to the small pick bunny still tucked into the man’s pocket, showing up as a blurry pink spot in the black silhouette of the employee.

[Cute.]

He stepped closer, greeted with a familiar tension in his friend’s shoulders. Carefully, he gave the plush a few pats on the soft cotton head. 

“Take good care of him, as you promised.”

The Deer nodded, finally taking his step back, distancing himself. 

Braun’s plan might just work. 

As they stepped through the doors of the mall, greeted with the darkness of the nighttime, Roe picked up his phone and quickly bid a goodbye, disappearing in the direction of a train station. Braun stayed behind. 

Stretching his fingers under the soft leather, he closed his eyes. 

How weak he felt at this moment. 

His dependence on another darkness to keep him going in this realm was like a permanent painful reminder of just how much he has lost. 

[Chores, my friend, sometimes are quite frankly a matter of survival.]

His fingers tingled as he focused on some memories from the days long gone.

---
[Welcome, dear guests, to the Tuesday quiz show!]

The audience erupting in applause at the peak of his career. The guests, eyes filled with whimsy at the prospect of a great prize ahead, slowly turning into that purely human delicious fear. There was no time a human was closer to their raw essence than in the seconds preceding death. Fear never interested the Host. It was there to water down the concentrated emotions he was looking for. Set it aside through sweet words, lies and promises and what you are left with is what made his show so special. 

Regrets: “I wish I could tell my mom I love her”. 

Secrets: “my husband will never know our child isn’t his”. 

Anger: “I wasn’t meant to go like this!” 

Sorrow: “How insignificant my life has been to this point”. 

And often, to the delight of the Host - admiration. 

“If I go, may I go in front of an audience”. “Maybe, this is for the better”. “At last, I was noticed!”

The spotlight of his stage made anyone feel like a celebrity in their last moments, and that’s what made it special. It mattered not what his quests had been through before, on his show they were equal, they were his esteemed guests given the same opportunities to shine like stars!

Until it all came down crumbling.

---


He took a deep breath, feeling his material, physical human lungs stretch inside his ribcage. There were moments his body felt alien to him: and it was. 

[Humans are so complicated.]

His arms felt heavy, his steps conscious, every breath manually controlled. He had to be aware where his hands were, how his back was straightened, how his whole body weighed on the soles of his feet.



When he got “home” he felt more tired than usual. It always happened after a dive into a darkness. The limitations of his incomplete physique were more painfully vivid after recharging.

In the darkness of his company apartment he grabbed a glass and poured some whiskey. Sitting down on a large sofa, he took a sip feeling the burn in his sensitive human throat. 

Food and water were never necessary for his life, but a part of him leaned into some human habits driven by the nature of his persona.

Now, alone and relaxed, he could do what was still keeping him up. Resting his head on the back of the couch, he closed his eyes, freeing his mind from what was of little significance. 
In front of his view was a small room: an unmade bed, a simple desk and a chair with a black briefcase tossed onto it. The perspective changed: he could see a familiar hand with a tattoo grab a glass of water from the night stand before reaching somewhere next to him. A black tie was tossed somewhere nearby onto the bed and a soft tired sigh was heard. He was turned, watching a few steps taken before presented with a new angle. Roe Deer was looking in the mirror: his shirt slightly undone, his hair pushed back slightly away from his eyes. As he assessed his tired look in the mirror, his hand reached for the mask. Grabbing it at the edge, lifting it just a bit. 

Braun held his breath. 

“Ah right,” Roe sighed. 

He reached for the plush in his pocket, forgetting the mask. Braun saw himself get picked up and examined. 

“That spa did you wonders.”

The empty eyes of the mask were clearly investigating him, fingers giving a little tug to the small fluffy tail on the back.

“Cute.”

Braun saw himself getting pulled close, and felt the distant warmth on his cotton body.

“You still smell nice…”

Quickly, Roe put the plush back into his briefcase. 

Darkness enveloped Braun’s vision.

He opened his eyes. 

His human cheeks felt oddly hot, his fingers digging into the armrest.

[Oh, friend.]
