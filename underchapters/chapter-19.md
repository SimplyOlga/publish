The Coalition Barrage was over. What was left of the resort stood in smoke and debris, still and quiet. Quickly, Lee Jaheon pulled out, cutting the connection between their minds and leaving Kim Soleum alone to see his work burnt to the crisp. 

‘Well, shame I didn’t get to spend all the coins…’

Turning around, he headed to the Blue Gates. One last matter needed resolving, and this time, as promised, he would allow no interruptions. 


When he entered the Red Zone, even in the dark of night under the dim red lights, it brought back horrific memories from the time he was last here. Running through the streets, cautious out of habit, he slipped from stall to stall, from a kiosk to the merchandise store. Stopping by, pulled by a familiar sight, he grabbed the pink rabbit plush and quickened his pace. This was a peace offering of sorts, a plea for mercy if the worst came to happen. Just thinking of Braun devouring a darkness as strong as the Red Mascot was enough to leave his heart beating rapidly in his head. But all means, now, he was free, he could leave, he should leave. So why was he running to him instead of from him?

Two silhouettes at the gates of the Blue Zone. They stood silently, watching his figure approach. Suddenly, the fear subdued a little and sheer relief of seeing them both intact as before took over.

“Braun, Mascot!”

He caught his breath, bending in half at the pain in his lungs, sweat dripping from his head. Looking up, he saw the way the Blue Mascot shifted on its feet uneasily. 

‘What did he do…’

“Did it… did it work?”

The Blue Mascot nodded, giving a careful side eye to the man beside him.

“Braun?”

The Host smiled as usual, but something in the way his eyes never looked away from Soleum sent a shiver down his spine. 

The Host. 

Stronger than he had ever seen him before. 

Soleum swallowed and smiled, rising up to give him an awkward chuckle.

“I couldn’t have done this without you, Mr. Host.”

At the words, Braun frowned a little, but didn’t let his smile go. 

“I suppose, it’s Mr. Mascot now.” 

A laugh that left his mouth was deep and unshaken. Uncanny. Unusual. 

Soleum took a step back unwillingly, immediately spotted by Braun who walked up. 

“This place will need a grand reopening, a redecoration, a reimagining. Don’t you think so?”

Wide eyed, Soleum looked at the Blue Mascot, who only shook his head. 

‘No way…’

As Braun looked around, scratching at his chin deep on thought, Soleum quietly asked:

“But… Didn’t we agree it would be best to leave the park to the Blue Mascot?”

Pausing, Braun stood still and looked back between the two. 

“Ah, apologies, Friend,” he smiled softly, letting Kim Soleum catch a breath of relief before it was shattered again. “But as a starving man would not let go of a meal, how cruel would it be for you to ask me to let go of what I worked for?”

Shocked, Kim Soleum backed away until the two arms caught his shoulders and Braun’s face leaned in to look him in the eyes. 

“Why must I let go? Didn’t you want to be free? Didn’t you want to leave me again, just like back then? Playing around? But what about now? You, too, can not leave the resort behind.” 

He spoke fast but calm, threatening but matter-of-factly, his fingers digging into Soleum’s muscle. 

“I will help you, we will rebuild this place grander than ever before: with your vision and my ambitions, with my experience and your talent! My Deer Friend,” his eyes glimmered with passion, “We were meant for this!”

Soleum swallowed, feeling his back shaking. 

“But we had a deal…”

“The deal was for me to ensure the safety of the Blue Mascot as the true form of the Red Rabbit is consumed. And so it was done.”

Exploitation of words. Twisting of terms. Exploiting loops and weaknesses. 

“You told me…” Kim Soleum felt his voice break against his best attempts to keep both his fear and pain of betrayal hidden deep within. “You said that unlike the vows of the outside world, you would listen…”

“And I did. I did just as you said. Friend, must we really-”

“And what of the studio!”

Not realising how loudly he shouted, Kim Soleum jumped at his own words, seeing the Blue Dragon turn in concern.

“You said you would stay by your resolution, to go back, so why- Oh.”

Braun’s smile twitched and lowered. 

“You can’t…” 

A whisper that caused a crack in Braun’s mask. 

“You can’t go back at all…”

Kim Soleum stared at him in shock over his own revelation. As Braun let go of his shoulder and straightened, adjusting his suit, he stared somewhere far away. 

Soelum’s mind went back to the stage, the show, the admiration and silent obeying of the faceless crew. He thought of the command he had over the show, thinking back then it was all either due to Braun’s patronage or due to his power within the resort. Now, however, it crept on him in a sinister revelation. 

“I see you noticed, Mr, Host,” Braun’s voice, going softer around the new title, sent shivers down his spine. “It’s yours. I am yours.”

He pushed away, trying to steady his boiling mind. 

‘No, no…’

“You will do great, I am certain of it…”

Braun’s smile, comforting and yet hurt, was a sight that broke the last walls of defence around Kim Soleum’s heart. 

“You don’t mean it…”

He gripped onto the shirt of the ex-Host and pulled at it with force of hurting desperation. 

“Take it back, it’s yours, it’s who you are!”

Braun watched him with a complicated expression of someone torn between two influences reshaping his identity.

“You are the Host, THE Host, Braun, the show is yours,” Kim Soleum punched at his chest and mouthed with anger. “Give back the park to the Blue Mascot, and I will give you the studio, a change of power, a contract.” 

But Braun’s eyes narrowed in a cold piercing gaze. 

“You let me have a taste of my former glory, you tease and tempt me with a show of my dream, only to run away and leave me locked behind.” 

Feeling the gloved fingers on his, Soleum let out a groan as Braun untangled his fingers from the shirt and fixed the wrinkles on the fabric. 

“If that’s how you want it to be, then I entrust the studio to you, Friend. I would love for you to feel what it is like to be chained to existence in a broken shell of your own former glory.”

It hurt. Betrayal mixed with fear of his freedom slipping away. The anger. 

Kim Soleum felt his fingers clench into fists. 

“I see,” he mouthed, looking at the distressed Blue Mascot. “If this is how you wish to interpret our contract, then so be it. Keep the park.” 

Braun’s eyebrow rose in question at the surrender. 

“But if you think I will leave to stay locked in that studio of yours, nice try.”

Unclipping a cuff on his suit, Soleum pulled his sleeve up and looked at the tattoo on his wrist, digging in and finally showing Braun a contract signed back at the cabin. Unfamiliar with it, the ex-Host frowned. 

“Friend, thi-”

“No, the darkness needs a presence, doesn’t it? It won’t get mine directly.”
 
As he opened the parchment, footsteps echoed from the bushes and a figure unfamiliar to Braun emerged: ragged and disheveled, slouched and unpleasant to the eye. 

“You called for me.”

Triumphantly, Kim Soleum side-eyed Braun, and spoke:

“Your work will be changed. You are to keep an eye on the new venue I have arranged for you.”

Noticing how Braun’s lips curled in disapproval, he only continued. 

“Your duties will be… of simply existing in the facility.” 

Silent, the studio keeper nodded and as no more orders were issued, his clothes melted to the ground revealing a clean shaved face, a smile and a technical waist with camera and audio equipment. With a nod, he left into the shadows and left a sense of completion somewhere in the back of Soleum’s mind. 

“How crude.” 

Looking at Braun, Soleum scoffed. 

“You made me do this.”

The anger he felt subdued and a sense of victory washed over. 

“Your contract is temporary. Once your keeper is gone, you will be mine.”

Soelum laughed.

“Nothing about the studio is now yours. Your threats are empty.”

Braun placed his hand on his heart in an exaggerated manner. 

‘Plus, I don’t intend to stay here much longer,’ Soleum thought. 

“If this is how you see it, Friend. One day we will be on that stage together, when you are dragged back and realise you need me,” the usual composed tone came back and Braun pat him on the shoulder. 

Snapping his fingers, the area around them lit up in a soft light. The plastic walls crumbled down giving space to rich dark red velvets and shimmering copper ornaments. The amusement park was changing: gone was the whimsy of a child-like darkness, replaced with intricate decorum and facades. The cotton candy stand vanished and a dark wooden entrance to a bar sent out a hint of heavy tobacco scent. It was no longer the Red Zone. 

Still, a small memento tucked in Soleum’s pocket peeked out, and carefully taking it out, the man showed the pink rabbit to Braun, shaking the toy in his hands, making the ears bounce. 

“You really want to get rid of it all?” 

For a moment, a light of doubt flashed in Braun’s eyes, but he simply smiled, taking the toy into his hands. 

“Reconsider, going back to the studio and leaving this behind,” last attempt, last desperate call to bring everything back to how it used to be. 

“It will be alright, my dear Host,” Braun said, tucking the little pink fluff back into Soleum’s pocket. “As you keep an eye on this memento, I will hold onto my own.”

He put his hand into the jacket pocket and pulled out a small familiar shape. Surprised, Kim Soelum stared at the small plush of himself: stitched at the throat just like after the Tamra express. 

“Let’s keep each other close, until the time comes for us each to return a part of each other to the rightful owners.” 

Braun offered his hand. 

Thinking about how he will soon be leaving this world, Soleum stared at the little version of himself peeking from Braun’s front pocket and accepted the handshake.

“Deal.”
