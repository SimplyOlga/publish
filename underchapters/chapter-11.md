When his eyes opened, Kim Soleum felt an immense surge of physical power flow through him. Like a harsh headache just left him. 

His body felt lighter, his head clearer, his mood elevated by the weight of the Dream essence collector in his arms. 

The nightmare was over. 

His body was in one piece, his mind was at ease and his pay shimmered in his hands in a golden sparkling hue. 

Quickly, he tossed his items from under the pillow into his suitcase, put on his work clothes and after a quick breakfast in the quiet of his roommate’s absence, headed toward the company building. 

There was the spring in his step that only fitted a man with a grade A essence under his belt. 
After handing it over for the assessment, he stopped by the D squad room to check on the squad. As expected, he was the only one to successfully make it into the darkness. 

“Seems like the manual will have a revision done to it now,” he smiled at his colleagues. 

“What do you mean, Roe?” Eun Haje asked over her morning coffee. 

“I just submitted grade A essence for approval after my clearance.”


There was silence in the room as Kim Soleum basked in the admiration coming from his colleagues. They were clearly impressed with his talent. 

‘Nothing quite like showing my team how much trust they can put into my excellent performance!’

“Mr. Soleum, what method did you use?” Chief Lizard looked at him and for onceKim Soleum could sense some sincere warmth of affection in those eyes. After all, he had to warm up to him now after seeing such a display of capabilities!

“I riled up the students within the darkness to take down the teacher for me,” he proudly announced. 

Supervisor Park and Manager Eun exchanged looks. In their minds they had to be thinking:
‘How did we not think of that before!’

With a relieved smile, Kim Soleum reached for the coffee pot and poured himself half a cup. It was a delightful evening to be spent at the office as his credits were rolling in. Points, cash. And most importantly - the word of mouth. He was holding himself back from checking on the group chat, knowing full well that it will have a better effect if he comes in to confirm the rumours after letting his peers gossip and wonder first. 

‘Everything has to be done on cue.’

“You convinced the students to assist you?”

It seemed like Supervisor Park was eager to hear more. 

“Yes, I took on the role of the ceremony announcer and gave them a speech. After all, we all have been students once, I felt like I could connect with them!”

Over a sip of coffee, Kim Soleum watched his team silent in their admiration. 

“Please, come with me,” Chief Lee Jaheon gestured to the door. Somewhat unwillingly, Soleum put his mug down and followed as they left the office. 

Silently, they walked through the corridors, Soleum exchanging a few hello’s here and there, giving a handshake to a fellow new recruit. 

“You are in good spirits,” Lee Jaheon mentioned as they both got into an elevator. 

“Of course, my work is going smoothly and my role at the company is now more clear to me than ever. I believe I will be reaching new highs in my career very soon,” Kim Soleum laughed, giving a polite bow to his superior. “All thanks to your guidance, Manager!”

Chief Lizard didn’t reply, just waiting for the elevator bell to signal their arrival. 

In a maze of corridors, Kim Soleum admired the newly permitted facilities. It was exciting to open new prospects in his career. He was settling in, he was aiming higher, he was reaching new hi-

THUD.

With a dull pain in his back, he found himself pinned to a wall, lifted off the ground in one strong motion. Lee Jaheon’s reptilian eyes stared at him unblinking. 

“When that new recruit on the second floor spoke to you, what did they say?”

A direct question. 

Calling for a solid answer.

“Well, they congratulated me on my good performance last night,” Kim Soleum squirmed slightly under the pressure, giving an apologetic smile. “I am not sure what you mean, Manager…”

“What else?”

Soleum was confused. What more? Why did he need details? He was right there when it happened…

“Well, they probably asked something about how I did it, but I told them to ask me later when I was appro-”

With a sudden yank , he was once again being transported like a ragdoll. Lifted by his collar, he was dragged further through the hallway, like a misbehaving cat, before Chief Lee Jaheon abruptly stopped, and held him up in front of  a wooden sign.

Fox Counseling office

He opened the door wide and then- 

“Ouch!”

Kim Soleum was tossed inside.

“You are contaminated. Do not leave before you are fixed.”

And just like that Lee Jaheon closed the doors, leaving Kim Soleum all alone. 


It was a very hospital-like space: a small dressing room lit with a cold light. With nothing better to do, Kim Soleum took off his work clothes, neatly placing them on the chair and changed into a provided garment. 

It was barely a good fit for him but he decided to let it slide this once. 

With only one way left for him, he walked through a swaying curtain into the therapy room, approaching a sofa sitting in front of a wide window lighting the place with nice soothing candlelight.

Behind the window, a bar was just entering its working hours: glasses sparkling on the counter, prepared for a rush of regulars, the stainless surfaces of the polished wooden countertop filled with different bottles and taps. Under the dancing light of the flames, it felt inviting. Kim Soleum took a deep breath feeling immersed in what was behind the glass, forgetting the colder emptier space of the actual room.

From behind the counter, a bartender appeared: in a familiar black suit, with familiar black hair and with a confident inviting look on his face. 

Kim Soleum was greeted by himself. As they exchanged waves, the doppelganger spoke:

“I am glad to see you so comfortable as we start our session.”

He poured a drink into a glass and offered it to Kim Soleum, who politely dismissed it. It was barely 10 am after all.

“I am not sure what this is about, but I guess I will pay you the courtesy of my attention.”

The bartender nodded. 

“I am grateful for your time. In fact, this session is less about me teaching you, but you teaching me.”

Kim Soleum smiled at his “reflection”. 

“Of course, what is it I can help you with?”

The bartender pointed at himself.

“I feel like I am lacking, my esteemed guest. In my manners, in my appearance. Everything I do is to mimic someone I admire, and yet, there is something I am missing.”

Kim Soleum pondered. He stood up and walked towards the window to get a closer look. Inspecting the figure before him he was mentally checking one box after another: same hair, same suit, same eyes and mouth. 

Everything was the same and yet, still, he agreed - something was missing. And then it clicked.

“My of course, please, give me your hand,” he smiled at the bartender. As the “reflection” walked closer, showing his empty left arm, Kim Soleum reached out to examine it.

His arm was grabbed in a swift motion. 

“Is it really so significant?” 

The bartender asked, looking right into Soleum’s eyes through the glass, their hands intertwined right where the reality faced the reflection. 

“Why of course, it is a part of my image, as important as the rest!”

Kim Soleum smiled, trying to free his arm, but the grip was too tight on his wrist where the design with the film was squeezed under the strong finger of his “other self”.

“Isn’t your image, first of all, in your manners, your memories and , your identity?.” The bartender continued. “What makes a human, Mr. Soleum?”

“I am unsure I can answer, but what I am made of is every part of me.”

“Even if some of the parts came as recently as last night? And changed you as abruptly as to cause concern for your colleagues?”

Ah, he thought, so this is what this is all about. 

“I am sure they are just uncertain about their feelings…”

The grip tightened. 

“I will ask again, Mr. Soleum: what makes a human? Is it memories, experiences and personal traits of one’s mind? Or is it the suit, the face, the ornaments onof one’s body?”

There was uncertainty. 

“I would like to leave this conversation at that.”

The bartender carried on, his fingers tracing over the tattoo, leaving a burning itch as if under a scorching flame. 

Kim Soleum twitched, trying to once again free himself. 

“Stop this!”

“Are you implying that you, the real you, are so deeply connected to something as small as this ink on your skin?”

“I refuse to comment, stop this before I call the security!”

It hurt, it burnt, the ink patterns disappearing inch by inch before his eyes. 

“Is it so essential that, without it, your very existence crumbles?” 

It was almost gone. In an odd sense of desperation, Kim Soleum shouted:
“And what if it does?!”

In a last smudge, the tattoo was fully gone.

“Mr. Soleum,” the bartender straightened, stepping away from the heavy breathing “original”. “What is more important for the human identity: body or soul?”

It finally clicked. It was a weird sense of clarity: one that pushed heavy with the grounding sense of reality. 

“The soul. It’s the soul…”

The contamination was cleared.




With a violent jerk, Braun pushed a lamp off his side table. The loud noise of the shattering glass drowned under his pained groan. 

His gloved fingers dug into the cracked surface of the wood where his fist landed in anger a moment ago. 

He paced the apartment, gripping onto his hair, eyes shut, a painful sobbing whine escaping his bleeding bitten lips. 

Papers went flying, a bottle of whiskey crashed into a wall. 

He sank onto the floor, on his knees, scratching at the skin on this face in a desperate attempt to numb the cold, suffocating feeling in his chest, in his throat, in his mind. 

Just as he was about to leave, to go, to… something… Something was lost. 

Snapped. 

Cut from him.

Burnt out of his memory. 

Out of his essence. 

A part of him was scorched away leaving an invisible scar that hurt.

Hurt so much.

Braun bent in half, pressing his warm forehead against the cold of the wooden floor.
