With a loud slam, he shut the door behind himself, walking across the building, his pace bordering a run, pushing through the crowd ignoring the greetings and waves of his colleagues. Only once he locked himself in a restroom and splashed some cold water over his head did he take a deep sigh. 

No, there was no time to go slow and steady. This play was calling for an unexpected grand finale. Wiping the water from his face, Braun fixed his hair and rushed from the Daydream building deeper into the city centre. If the scene was to play out as he wanted, there were some preparations to be made. 




Box after box, scene after scene, day after day I spent watching mismatched recordings of the show in the same room. Only the shade would occasionally break my routine by bringing more. I grew accustomed to it, mostly focusing on grasping the pattern behind the questions Braun would ask, keeping notes with a pen the shade brought me upon request. The dark metallic fountain pen felt heavy in my hand and made me think of Braun leaning over his work desk making corrections to the scripts brought to him. No, I shook the thought away, there was no way the idea of this darkness extended to such details. Though, I looked around noticing just how elaborate the backstage would get, thinking about the extent of Braun’s existence within his own role. A light tap on the shoulder brought me back from my thoughts, as the shade asked for my attention. 

“What is it?” 

It was odd to think I spent days hiding from this creature in a locker barely able to breathe. Up close it looked like a normal human being with just a blur over the face, preventing you just enough from being able to figure out its facial features. Mute, it pulled something out from its pocket and handed it to me: a small metal casing. I almost jumped to my feet, taking it from the shade, giving my thanks and rushing to the projector, almost ripping apart the film that was already in it. Gently securing the tape in, taking a breath to calm my shaking hands, I put the casing with a familiar number aside and sat down, eyes glued to the image. 

The recording picked up at an unknown moment when Braun was almost leaning over the edge of the stage somewhere towards the audience. Behind him, Lee Jaheon stood calmly observing, while the other contestants were breaking down in tears, hesitant to try running away in the moment the Host was looking away. 

[So, you say you want to volunteer?]

Oh. I felt my heart skip a beat. 
Not a single time in the dozens of episodes I had watched had this happened. Someone from the audience was actively insisting on participation. Was that even allowed?

[I am not sure if this is allowed…]
Even Braun seemed hesitant, thinking for a moment. 

[But how could I possibly refuse someone so eager to get their moment of fame on my stage. That, of course, if the contestants do not mind stepping down from the race for the main prize?]

Behind him the two civilians nodded enthusiastically, almost screaming that they didn't mind. 

[Oh dear, in that case, please come on stage, our new guests!] 

Behind Braun, two shades came to escort the civilians from the stage to the audience. 

[Now now, don’t be sad, dear contestants, you still get to watch the rest of the show from the front rows reserved only for our most special members of the audience!]

The camera panned over to show the two of them squeezed between the dark figures of the faceless audience members who seemed fascinated at the prospect of sitting next to humans. In their stead, behind the podiums two men in bright blue identical jackets took their stance and watched Braun expectedly, casting worried glances at the two civilians in the audience. 

‘No way…’

[Well, now where were we? Right, I believe it is your turn now, our dear new guest!] 

Turning to the agent taking the middle podium, Braun pulled up a card. 

[Question number 8: what word would you use to describe the flavour of human flesh? 
1- sweet
2 - salty
3 - spicy 
4 - tender]

Here I caught myself thinking it had to be another tricky question where all the answers were correct. Involuntarily, I caught myself taking a note on paper, before realising a flaw in my thought. 

“Well, that depends on how you cook it, am I right?”


Both the Host and the guest laughed at the wild remark as I stared at the brown haired agent in disbelief. 

“I am tempted to say all of the above…”

‘No, no, this is wrong!’

“But!” Right before Braun was about to reveal the answer, the agent paused him with a quick gesture of his hand. “You said to describe the flavour, so I think ‘tender’ would not work there at all. So, choosing between the other three… Considering how high in salt an average modern diet of a human is, my answer is 2, salty.”

Once again, I caught myself flabbergasted by the confidence with which the agent spoke and a small bow he gave to the audience as soon as the Host counted his answer as correct. Moving to Lee Jaheon, Braun pulled up the last card in his hands. 

[Now, this show has been full of surprises tonight, but the time has come to see if one of the guests can claim the status of the MVP of the night by answering the last remaining question. Are you excited for your moment of spotlight, dear guest?]

“Yes.”

As soon as Braun lifted the card to his “face” the film ended, snapping from the machine and leaving me frustrated yet again. Calming down I realised my hands had been crumbing the papers I was taking notes on, so I took a deep breath and stood up, looking over the mess I had left in the room over the past many days. Checking the pages of notes in my hands, I made a decision. It was time for the grand finale!

But for the play to go as I wanted to, I needed to make some preparations. Opening the doors to the corridor for the first time in days I almost jumped seeing the shade standing right outside. It seemed that it was waiting for my commands in the meantime, used to how long it would usually take me to go through one box of films. 

“I have a request,” I started, watching the shade attentively look back at me with its empty face. “Multiple, actually. In fact, I want to help.”

It watched me quietly, its silence making me anxious to test the extent of my allowed control over it. 

“I want to help clean the studio,” I started, watching the figure shake a little as if surprised. It had been weird adjusting to understand its behaviour without words but somehow this little gesture made me excited. “I want to prepare it for the return of the Host.”

The way it straightened up and nodded was almost child-like. For a moment there it lightly lifted its arms as if reaching out to me, quickly tucking them into the pockets and nodding again, more vigorously than before. 

“Alright then, let’s get to work.”






The figure in his arms shivered as Braun applied pressure to his arm, squeezing the skull under his figures until it cracked, bent and popped open. Dropping the body down on the floor, he sighed, looking over the shoulder displeased with the attention of an unwanted guest watching over his every move. 

[What an annoyance. At least be subtle about your desires.]

With a cry, a bony figure of a woman drenched in blood, her long blue dress ripping and tangling under her open broken legs, ran straight into Braun, throwing hands into his hair, trying to rip them out only to be lightly pushed away. With a light click of his fingers, her figure caught in flame, melting into a puddle of blood and fat onto the ground, absorbed swiftly into the bright green grass. 

[I am not here to hunt down your prey, leave us alone.]

Looking directly at the unwanted audience, Braun picked the woman’s still beating heart now revealed unscathed on the ground. Ripping into it with his fingers, Braun yawned. 

This was an easy, albeit dirty and annoying way to feed. The entities in this darkness were weak but there were plenty to feast upon so after taking about 30, Braun walked away from the massacre into a quiet empty corner that looked like a little alley, dimly lit in the night. Gently, he pulled the deer plush from his pocket and tapped at it as if testing a microphone. 

“Friend? Are you there, Friend?”

Just a moment later, a voice came through. 

“Braun? Are you in a darkness?”

“Yes,” Braun listened intently. There was something in Roe Deer’s voice that was a little off. “Are you alright friend? You sound out of breath.”

“Yes, I was just… pacing over the studio a lot in the past days. It should be me asking you about your state considering how you left last time.”

Braun, concerned over the fact that his Friend was clearly getting bored over the past days, shamed himself for lacking powers to even connect to the studio enough to watch over him, but the mention of their last meeting did bring a smile to his eyes. 

“I am more than alright, Friend. In fact, this Braun will soon pay you a visit, as I promised to not keep you waiting for long.”

“Soon?” Soleum’s voice almost broke there. 

[Is he afraid after the last time?]

“Yes, soon, please wait for me, Friend. I must now go, but I promise you, our reunion is long overdue.” 

Opening the gates and stepping over from the yellow pavement to the bright blue bricks, Braun slipped through the exit of the blue area of the amusement park with a content smile on his face. 



Soleum, still trying to call out to Braun for explanation finally gave up and, rolling up the sleeves of his shirt, looked over the work he had already done with pride. Kicking a piece of a metal beam off the stage, he took a deep break with a content smile on his face.
