Now, how difficult is it to find an employee in the office of Daydream. When you know nothing about them. 

As Braun found out - very difficult. 


He has been at the corporation for a while, and if anything deserved credit here - it was the masks. They performed their job flawlessly. Annoyingly so. 

Each employee Braun had to encounter within a darkness resembled nothing of their origin. Their souls, their essence, tied to the firm structure of the mask, hidden from the prying eyes of any darkness that would wish to feast upon it. Darknesses were born of personal fears, memories, regrets. One way DayDream preserved the sanities of their employees was through straight up denying the access to them all together. 

From the perspective of darkness itself, it was almost painful to look at. Like trying to distinguish a shape that can not exist, describe a colour that can not be perceived. The face, the voice, the physical appearance all together: all vaguely shaped into the silhouette of the mask. A fish, a beetle, a cat. And now, he could not even recall what mask his park friend had…
 
The second highly annoying quality of the masks was integrated to avoid entities searching for specific employees outside of their darkness. The moment an employee takes of their mask outside of any darkness, the memory connecting their essence to their shape disappears from the memory of any darkness that recently encountered them.

Simply put - Braun had not a single memory of the friend that could lead him directly to the matter of his interest.


The interest itself was peculiar. 


Boredom at any company was something all employees would share in common. Routine checks, paperwork, assignments didn’t bother Braun much. After all, it was in his very essence to accommodate a prewritten script. However, as any good tv host would know - the best of show moments are born out of unexpected improvisations. And here it was - someone caught his attention. 

Someone who was extraordinary enough in their unorthodox methods. This was a great start for something. After all, a great host has a personal talent for finding talents around. And something about that employee at the park just drove Braun to now stand in the middle of the elite squad floors, trying to figure out a way to get the info without raising any suspicion. 
The list of employees involved in the mission was not something he could easily ask for without a reason even in his position. He knew there are memories of their conversations after the mission deep in his mind, but the interference of the mask was leaving it all in noise.

Braun remembered there was someone else (his squad?) who caused some kind of an emotional impulse from him , and yet, it’s all a blur. 

The one trick he had up his sleeve was also dismissed. 

Braun closed his eyes, leaning onto a cold wall of the office corridor. He focused his thoughts on the toy, hoping to get a read. Silence, darkness, emptiness. No presence of a living being around, no clues about its whereabouts. 

‘Are you hiding from me on purpose,’ Braun thought to himself. 




Meanwhile, Kim Soleum’s morning was full of anxiety. As he got ready to head out, the thought of the plush was in the back of his head, pestering him. Well, it is in his safekeeping, surely the Host didn’t expect him to actually carry the toy around. How weird would it look then? In the end, he opted out of taking it along. At this point it could be a small memento of getting out of the cursed park, nothing more. 


Later in the day, grabbing a coffee at the nearby employee kitchen, Kim Soleum felt utterly drained. After a full night of dreaming about getting chased by mascots, getting swallowed into the dark pits of barely operational ride tunnels, hearing screams of children turning from jolly to terrifying. And of walking through all of it side by side to… someone. Of course, he knew who it was. Yet, in his dream, he could never see Braun’s face. As if merged with his mask, he watched Soleum intently through the pitch black screen. His long coat felt unbearably heavy on him, enveloping Soleum’s body, almost claustraphobically tight, as if denying him any escape. 

At the thought, he shivered. 




“Mind if I?” 

The very voice startled him. Sumping to the side, he turned around to the familiar figure. The long black coat, the perfectly tailored suit and the wide smile he was just desperately trying to recall from the blurry memory of his dream. 

“Mr. Host…”

Braun greeted him with a squint of eyes as if to emphasise his already wide, clearly performative smile. 

Politely, Kim Soleum moved to the side, allowing the superior to get access to the slowly cooling down coffee pot. His coffee pot.

‘The company is giant, yet I have to bump into him the very next day.’ 


As if deep in his own thoughts, Braun poured himself a cup full of coffee, quietly humming some tune. 

In a way, Kim Soleum thought it would be fitting to thank him, again, for his assistance. Without his custom equipment the escape from the park would barely be possible. And yet, Soleum stood to the side, as if waiting for the man to go first. 

‘At the end of the day, corporate formalities aside, it was my escape plan. Must I really go out of my way to get to the ego of an elite? It’s always best to stay out of their way an-


“You are one of the newbies?” Braun finally broke the silence. 

“Yes, I started a bit over a month ago.”

Braun hummed in approval, taking a sip from his mug, never once breaking eye contact under which Soleum wanted to just leave, disappear, hide from sight. 

“I heard there were quite a couple exceptional employees with a lot of potential…”

So, as Kim Soleum realised, Braun didn’t know he was “the newbie” the talks of whom were part of the newbies routine these days. It was almost comforting. The less he stood out, the higher the chances to avoid getting dragged somewhere. 

And going on another mission by The Host’s side was not something Kim Soleum looked forward to…

“I believe everyone who made it through the selection deserves the credit for their… adaptability,” Soleum firmly replied. 

Braun shrugged.

“There is a difference between adaptability and innate talent for survival. Adaptability is nurtured. Instinct to live longer than the person next to you - that’s something manifesting vastly different among humans.”

Something in a way he spoke made Kim Soleum swallow. The way he spoke left no way out, as if backing him into a corner. 

“I suppose. But after all, everyone who stayed at the company is here for a reason, with a clear goal in mind.”

“Ah yes, the good old Wish Ticket,” Braun slightly shook his coffee cup in his gloved hand, watching the ripples on the top. “It’s always about the Wish Ticket.”

‘What is he implying…’

Kim Soleum had already met quite a lot of peculiar and borderline insane individuals at the company, and had read even more about some he never wished to encounter. But if what he thought Braun was implying was true - it had to be on a whole another level.

“You sound almost like someone disinterested in it?”

Braun gave him a knowing look. Yet Kim Soleum was not exactly sure just what he was supposed to “know”.

“No, no, just like you said, we are all here for the same exact reason,” he put his coffee cup down. “We all have something we want, something we have lost or wish to acquire.”

‘Something we have lost…’

Almost with some sympathy Kim Soleum realised - no matter how different the Host was from him: how crazy or unpredictable - at the end of the day he could have lost just as much as he had. 

“Well, duty calls!” Braun threw his paper cup into the trash. “Thank you for the coffee and delightful company, Mr. -?”

With a question, he extended his arm, awaiting. 

‘Oh.’

Did the Host not recognise him without the mask? ‘I suppose he sees a lot of people on a daily basis. Remembering a newbie he met once in a dire situation is not that important.’

“Kim Soleum,” he replied, reciprocating the handshake offer. There was no need for an elite to call him by the mask codename in a casual setting. After all, once again, Kim Soleum still hoped their paths would not cross in a Darkness again.

“This Braun is delighted to make your acquaintance,” the Host waved his goodbye before disappearing into the maze of office corridors. 



‘Weird, he is definitely weird’.
