# Smiley stickers

“Wait, Braun,” Kim Soleum pushed himself away, slipping through the sheets to try crawling out of the bed, only for an arm to swiftly wrap around his waist to pull him back. He stumbled, falling right onto Braun’s side. 

“These are just some standard Bureau items.” He tried to focus, giving his face a more serious look, one that could possibly convince someone. Definitely not Braun. “They are for calming down civilians, especially kids.”

A convincing lie. One that would not work on Braun.

“So, you thought this would cheer me up?”

An image of himself giving Braun shiny stickers to brighten his mood, gave Kim Soleum an odd feeling of dread. 

“I thought there could be children in the park.”

Braun listened to the lies with a patient smile, fingers turning the bright paper around, wondering how much more he could back his Friend into the corner he was willingly stepping in. After all, Braun had done a decent research on Daydream and Bureau items available on the market, present and discontinued alike. Of course he knew, and somehow the thought that the only two items his Friend had brought here were both to try getting Braun’s favour back was endearing. Of course, he decided to ignore the rather opposite sentiment from his Friend’s companion. 

But this was, without a doubt, a small payback for the early days when Kim Soleum would hide from Braun just a few office floors away. 

He peeled one off and brought it close to the man who suddenly thrushed more than one would next to a simple sticker given to “civilians, especially kids”.

“It would suit you quite well, deer.”

A light touch on the sweaty skin and Kim Soleum’s jaw clenched, sweat forming on his neck where Braun quickly placed a small kiss, to see his reaction. 

Without a delay, his Friend pushed himself closer, head tilted to the side to expose more skin to the caress.

{Oh.}

The man in his arms jolted at the voice in his head, only peaking the Macot’s curiosity more. 

{Do you perhaps enjoy the sound of this Braun’s voice?}

“Hah,” Kim Soleum let out a puff of air, eyes heavy-lidded and mind fogged with a blur of emotions that were not fully his. Mostly, it was the intoxicating absence of fear that screamed deep in his mind in alarm. “Who wouldn’t like voice of a TV host? I thought that was the casting requirement…”

On his skin, he felt the smile curving Braun’s lips as he pressed another kiss on the darkening mark on Soleum’s neck. 

“Is that all there is to it?”

No, of course there was more, and Braun knew that. The voice of a darkness meant to leave people defenceless over his charm, inhabiting a perfect body with dashing looks and too sharp of a tongue. Of fucking course there was more!

“Let’s say,” Kim Soleum blinked through the blur in his eyes, his mind feeling a little more clear, “I would be front row for your new season debut.”

Braun shifted, turning the man around for their eyes to meet:

“You really know how to make a man lose his composure.”

That was not the intention, but the sticker on his cheek took away the worries and the fear, leaving behind only the warm feeling of the other man’s hair brushing lightly over his chest, right where the ring sat deep inside. Was the effect of the sticker not as dramatic as he thought because he already had a rather positive attitude towards Braun? He decided against dwelling on it more, ruffling through the blond hair, feeling his own face betray his feelings with a smile he could not hold back. 

An odd sense of comfort. 

He knew it was not coming from the item, it was his own.

“Oh?!”

Kim Soleum jolted, pushing Braun away, turning his body to see another sticker right on his lower back, a burning blush spreading from his cheeks to the chest as he tried to peel it off, only for his feelings sliding right over the edge of the paper. 

Braun, unable to hold back, let out a heary laugh, dropping onto the pillow, hand covering his face as if such a display of sincere emotion was unbefitting the generally composed Host. Kim Soleum lifted his hand, prepared to give him a slap, but his own body beytrayed him, freezing him in spot as soon as he heard the deep rumble of Braun’s voice. 

No, this was getting worse. 

“Oh forgive me, Friend,” Braun was still out of breath, finally looking at his Friend with such fondness in his eyes it would make Soleum shiver any other time, but now it was melting down all of his defences, mind screaming to get closer, to feel the way Braun’s voice would feel against his own lips, to know what his laughter would taste like.

‘Surely, it is as sweet and coating as his voice.’

Kim Soleum wanted to shake his head to push such thoughts away, but instead, he only surrendered to the arms that pulled him back to bed, to the kiss that missed his lips just a little, leaving a small peck on the corner of his mouth. 

“How could this Braun resist?”

Kim Soleum felt sluggish, like all the energy had left his body, leaving him content and greedy for the warmth that held him. In a way, this wasn’t so bad, was it?

“Sleep now,” another smiley sticker landed on Soleum’s neck before he could even nod, a wave of drowsiness making him doze away before shortly falling asleep. “Dream about me tonight. But for once, I hope it is a sweeter dream than your usual nightmares.”



# The daydream office

If you were to ask anyone about Director Ho they would, well, unless they are a Daydream employee, know nothing about him. If you were to catch someone at the office however, asking to tell you just what kind of person the director is, they’d hesitate debating just how much to tell you. 

Inevitably, everything would always just come down to a simple:

“He seems like a man pleased with his position.”

Yes, the smile and the authority clearly would point to this conclusion, but as of the past couple months, the smile hid anything but the content. It was a simple habit from the days long gone, a trust inducing smile that would give him just what he wanted, then immediately leaving the others with unease at its origin. 

Ho Yuwon stacked the papers on his desk and pushed the rolling chair away, taking in a breath before walking to the door of his office, one that bore no counselling sign and served for the purpose of all the bureaucratic hell he did not wish to be. 

Checking his phone, Director Ho scrolled through the schedule, a smile finally popping onto his lips after an entire day of signing papers that meant little more than just to stall him, distracting him from the actual work he intended to do. 

A counselling session scheduled with a member of an elite squad who was a prime candidate for his project team. If he does well today, which he inevitably will, by the end of the session the employee, who had recently cleared a darkness as a solo survivor, would sign the contract that had already been tucked into Ho’s inner pocket. 

This day could still turn around for the better. 

He made his way a couple floors down, the elevator bell ringing as he reached the elite floor. Ho Yuwon adjusted his already perfect suit and put on his flashiest smile. 

He really needed another person for his team. And where else would be find someone both capable and courageous on the verge of self-sacrificial idiocy. 

No, such candidates would not just pop out of the thin air…

He made his way through the corridor, nodding at a few employees before entering the area where the top three squads had their flashy lounges. Stepping before the door to the B squad, Yuwon raised his arm about to knock when a sound made him turn around.

“Wait, sorry, uh!”

There seemed to be commotion behind the C squad door, muffled sounds of hustling and something dropping hard on the floor made the Director listen in.

Drama among squads was not uncommon, but within one unit? That was not advised. He would have to visit the team and inquire about the problem, for the sake of the company’s quota, of course. 

Still, for now, he had more pressing matters to tend to: he, as he once again reminded himself, was desperately short stuffed, in need of someone who would easily defy the will of Cheong Dallae, someone reckless enough to dive into a darkness with nothing more than a twist of words as a provocation or promise of a worthy reward.

Such fools were rare. 

He cleared his throat and once again readied himself to knock on the door when with a loud bang the B squad lounge opened and someone, with a loud panting, rushed into the corridor, hitting the director right into his shoulder. 

“Ah, sorry, I wasn’t-”

The two froze on the spot, Yuwon’s arm going down as he stared with a smile at the one man he never thought he would see here. 

Someone courageous enough to dive somewhere they could never come out of. 

Someone whose resolve could be easily influenced in the right direction with just a few right words.

Someone with an almost self-sacrificial tendency to see things through. 

“Good evening, agent.”

With a bright smile, Director Ho offered his hand to the agent who felt his blood draining from his face. 

“Would you like to come with me?”




# Hyunmoo 1 breakroom


Go Yougeun’s move into the new team was a smooth, quick and seamless process. A few words here, a filled application there and one immediate approval later she placed her small box of belongings onto the desk that once was Kim Soleum’s.

She looked at the standard issue keyboard and a few old files left behind for future reference. Opening one, she flipped through the pages, noticing a few handwritten remarks on the edges of the manual:

“Only after 17:40”

“No less than 13”

“While water works, alcohol produces better results”

Hastily scribbled letters blended into lines that reminded her of the letter the agent left for them at the studio, especially of the postscriptum meant only for her eyes. She slammed her own papers onto the desk, feeling better thinking her intrusion was only temporary.

Yes, soon Kim Soleum will return and they can all decide what they do with the wish ticket, maybe he will tell her more about his home far away. Maybe, before they part ways, he would put the team’s minds at ease. 

At the thought, she reluctantly turned to face the agent who’d been staring at her the entire time. As soon as their eyes met, he smiled. 

“You will do well here, agent Mint.”

She knew he was dying to use her other title, from her days at the Daydream, but since Ryu Jaekwan was going through his medkit on the couch, she didn’t comment anything, only returning the fake smile back at him. 

Of course Bronze could sense the tension that was already starting to weigh on him. 

“It will be fine, agent Mint,” he didn't turn to look at her, but gave a disapproving look to Choi who only shrugged, moving to pick a pack of cigarettes from his desk drawer before excusing himself for a smoke. 

Go Youngeun finally sat down, taking a deep breath. Yes, she had to make this count, she had to return the favour that Kim Soleum did for her, disregarding his own safety. 

For a few minutes she adjusted her desk layout and logged into the system to drown in emails and tasks she had to do before her first official duty with the field rescue team. 

Seeing her spirit deflating, Ryu Jaekwan fidgetted with a pack of bandaid in his fingers before setting everything aside and looking at the woman. In her slightly slouched posture as she leaned in to read the small text on her computer, Jaekwan could see Kim Soleum in his first days of working here: competent without a doubt, yet with a facade of a rookie he used to his advantage. At the realisation, he wondered what secret Go Youngeun could be holding, but remembering how things ended last time Choi had a chance to dig too deep despite Jaekwan’s protests, he kept his mouth shut. 

“Is something the matter, agent?”

Mint was the first to break the silence, and Jaekwan realised he might have been caught staring a little too long. 

“No, it’s just… I am glad to have you here. We both are.”

Go Youngeun was not sure what to say to that, considering they only knew each other in the passing, mostly through Roe Deer’s reports to the Daydream Director. 

“Thank you, agent.”

She leaned back awkwardly, unsure how to continue, tapping on the desk while Jaekwan tried to come up with something reassuring to say. 

“We will show you around, and teach you everything, don’t worry.”

She nodded. She didn’t need to be taught, but this was a lot easier. After all, she wasn’t here to be a good agent, she was here to allow herself time to find out just what happened to Kim Soleum.

“I will do my best.”

Jaekwan stood up, pacing between the bookshelves and the big whiteboard that still carried all the notes and mementos of the agents that had come and gone through this room. 

“He was the same, ready to go in, you know.”

Picking the right words, Jaekwan stared at the small pink plush that sat on a small pillow on the window shelf where they would put it between their missions for the long nights of paperwork, upon Soleum’s request of course. 

‘He… likes that spot. He can see the whole room from there.’

There was a lot to Kim Soleum he would never allow himself to pry into unless he would one day trust Jaekwan enough to share.

“This is the last reminder we have of him,” he picked the little rabbit and turned it around, checking the soft little tail and a torn tag on the back. “He would carry it with him, I guess it helped with the stress. I hope wherever he is now, he finds comfort in someone or something that he could hold.”

It was an embarrassingly sentimental thought voiced out loud, and he felt his face redden with blush as he turned to explain himself only to see Go Youngeun smile, without judgement or mockery, only with understanding. 

“I am sure he isn’t alone.”

“Right.”

He cleared his throat and looked at the toy again, picking up the small pillow and walking up to Mint’s desk, eyeing it for a good spot. There, on the top of a file holder, the agent placed the pillow and carefully sat the toy on top, giving it a little pat on the ears. 

“Here, I think he would like it if something that brought him comfort could also look after you.” 

Go Youngeun eyed the toy with a smile. 

“Well then,” she picked it and slightly moved the little plastic structure to the side, adjusting the angle and placing the toy back, now turned towards the room: 

“Here he can still the whole room, including the door,” she pointed at the door behind which Choi stood quietly listening with a tightening grip on his heart. “So that when one day Kim Soleum walks back in, he would be the first to know.”


# A calm day in a calm routine

Two spoons of sugar and just a little bit of milk to tint the coffee a lighter shade of brown. Two toasts and a side of a fried egg with sprinkles of black pepper and seasalt. A glass of orange juice straight from the fridge, the glass wet with the condensation in the heat of the morning. 

Braun took a sip of the coffee and eyed the morning paper: one still dropped at his mailbox daily upon a personal request. He read about the politics, a new art exhibition and report on the city’s new infrastructure developing project. Nothing particularly interesting, but he still noted the exhibition down into his calendar for the day after, noticing just how empty his week had been so far. 


The Daydream higher ups were holding down on giving him assignments, that much was obvious. Still, he didn’t dwell on it, folding the paper neatly, and finishing his breakfast: not too fast and not too slow either, enough to appreciate the small detail that was supposed to make his day better. 

Convinced that it did, he stood up and headed for the closet where a nice day off suit was already ironed and waiting for him: a nice slightly lighter shade of brown than what he usually wore. A lighter summer variation, one that would show that he was more than just a man whose mind was all about work 24/7. 

“Something a little more playful.”

He put the suit over his body, admitting that his choice was, as always, perfect. 

A fine shirt, a light blazer, a lightly unbuttoned collar that required no tie. Today he would leave his dormitory to appreciate the life that was not his to have: shamelessly hiding behind an act. 

The idea was giving him a shiver of excitement. 



The morning air was filled with the scent of freshly cut grass and bread on the shelves of a small bakery on his way to the museum, so he slipped in to get a croissant and finished it on the way, admiring the flaky pastry and stopping only when a tiny bark brought him from the thoughts. 

Turning around, Braun knelt down, the absence of the long coat allowing him to level himself with the tiny creature running towards him. 

“My oh my, you sure are energetic in the morning!”

The small pup circled around him and finally got to licking crumbs off his hand, a ticklish sensation tucked and recorded in Braun’s memory. 


To him, the world was a stage and he was a member of the audience, taking in the inspiration from all the details his own show had never ventured to cover. 

“Good morning to you, Mister Daly.”

Braun patted the dog between the ears and rose to take the older lady’s hand in his, planting a small kiss on her palm. 

“You are as gorgeous as ever, madame.”
It was a routine: a walk in the park, a chat with an older lady that would always bring her dog out at the same exact time, a stop by the book store to pick press, anything, he would cycle through the topics as they would update - a professional habit. 

A lunch before an activity he would decide beforehand. 

Perfectly mundane to the point where no one would spot what was inherently different about him.

A role played perfectly. 

He rolled the magazine in his hand and pulled at the heavy door of the museum, feeling the springs tense as a tune rang from his pocket. 

‘Hm.’

An inconvenience. A disruption. An unpredictable variable in his perfectly scripted day. 

He picked the call and evened his voice into a smooth greeting:

“Hello?”


“So sorry to be calling you, Mr. Host,” the voice on the other end immediately sounded full of requests to make. “But we have a situation here. I will redirect you to the Director.”

At the mention of Director Ho Braun’s curiosity led him to let go of the door after holding it for a mother with a stroller. A move of a real gentleman. 

“What is it?”

“Ah, a small bureaucratic complication.”

“That’s the ones I tend to leave for others to solve.”

“It is worth a few points.”

There it was again, a small charity here, a little gesture of good will there.

‘How uninspiring.’

Even in his slowest show he would never treat his contestants this way. 

“I understand. Give me details and I will head back.”



He put on his usual brown suit with light thin stripes, the bowtie, and the long black coat that made him feel like an actor about to enter his scene. 

On the way to the office, he read through a summary of the case: a researcher is standing accused by the board over an irresponsible waste of resources. Read: employees. 

‘You must really value this researcher, Director.’

He thought there was more to the connection between the two, but that was none of his concern. All that mattered was an outing, a possibility to “stretch his arms” and a few points.

Nothing else about this could possibly be exciting.

He would dive into the darkness. 

He would stroll through the park until the missing employee would be found, and later, he would go back home to enjoy an old black and white movie, slowly drifting to sleep before the next predictable day, planned long in advance.
