Admittedly, among the mannequins dressed in most expensive fabrics with displays of golds and silvers - Braun felt much more belonging. Kim Soleum noted how his coat, now rested on his arm, the perfect hair, the perfectly polished shoes all made the man feel like a tall foreign celebrity on a walk for a designer souvenir to bring back to a lover overseas. 

“So, this is what you came here for?”

Braun looked back at Soleum after eyeing a display of scarfs in one of the windows. 
‘Is all his clothes from here? Does the company really allow an employee to walk around in designer darkness?’ 

“Something like that,” he replied, moving forward. “It is nice to treat yourself from time to time, don’t you agree?”

The last purchases Kim Soleum had made for himself were stickers, blood sucking cutlery and a box of apples. 

“I suppose…” 

Together, they walked into a boutique with a nice set of suits on display. Everything here looked just right Braun’s alley: long wooden displays of fabrics, ties and shoes. Involuntarily, Kim Soleum had to admit that none of the suits on the mannequins looked even remotely as good as the one tailed to his companion.

“Something specific in mind?”

Braun was deep in thought. 

“Not really, to be honest. Actually, that’s exactly why I thought an extra opinion would help!”

Not something Soleum could see himself agreeing with: standing there with his cheap company assigned suit. 

“Sure, what are you looking for?”

Why not go along with this, he thought.


Braun stepped behind a headless mannequin, as if trying the suit onto himself from the point where Soleum was standing. 

“Not really my colour is it?”

Soleum shook his head. Brown fit the man better than the dark navy.

“I am afraid I don’t exactly know what I am looking for. You see, I have a very special occasion coming up, but I have no clue what to wear.”

‘A special occasion?’ Soleum was not sure he wanted to know, and still he asked. 

“Is it to do with your Wish Ticket?”

For a moment there, Kim Soleum could swear the Host’s eyes softened in what could only be described as a mix of sorrow and timid nostalgia. Whatever was brought up in his mind was so important to him as to invoke the most visible emotional response Kim Soleum was yet to see. It felt uncanny. The perfect image of the man was for the first time crumbling under the pressure of something unknown to Kim Soleum. The experience was as moving as it was unsettling. 

“In a way, yes. When the time comes, it needs to be perfect.” 

Braun moved on to feel some of the fabric on display. 

“I always thought you already have as many perfectly tailored suits as one could possibly need,” Kim Soleum noted, half attempting to shift the mood into more of a joke. Seeing the Host so driven by a swarm of unknown emotions was a heavy experience. 

“It is not for me,” Braun turned around with a smile. In his hands was a folded suit in dark burgundy red. 

He walked up to Soleum with a shimmer in his eyes that meant nothing good was to be expected. 

“When the time comes, I need everything to be perfect. From every angle, at every light, every guest invited to witness my success will be a perfect actor to the script I have in mind.”

He winked. 

“I understand.”

Kim Soleum did not in fact understand.

“So, I have a request, my friend.”

‘Uhhh…’

Soleum eyed the suit in his hands. 

“I thought we were now even on the favours?”

“We are, that is true. But I can assure you, everyone would benefit from having me in their debt.”

That was true, admitted easily even by Kim Soleum. Contaminated or not, Braun was strong, competent and way too comfortable in his way around a darkness. Being able to request his assistance in a time of need would be a good ace up his sleeve. Especially if he is contaminated, pulling out a “you owe me” could save Soleum’s life…



And so in a few minutes, he stood in front of a floor length mirror caught off guard at how different it made him look. And feel.

‘It has to do with the darkness,’ he thought. 

The man in the reflection, thought resembling him in every physical aspect of his appearance, just looked… different. The posture felt more confident, the eyes looked more alive and full of excitement. Almost as if it gave him a new persona to play. A mask of a man confident about his actions and words. Taking a full breath, Kim Soleum felt like he could take on another day no matter what was at stake. 

‘Wow. Is Braun’s whole persona perhaps fabricated by… fabrics?’

Never in his life would he think he would admire tailoring quite as much. 

A glove tapped at his shoulder. A floating glove that he had previously found in the changing room. Though uncanny and ghostly, it quickly proved to be a simple servant helping him to put the suit on, somehow adjusting it to fit his figure just right.

‘Like at a bridal salon,’ he thought with a chuckle. 

Of course, the time was nearing he had to step out. Involuntarily, he pulled the curtains just with a bit too much enthusiasm, clearly driven by whatever power was now sitting on his shoulders. 

Braun, sitting in a small velvet armchair with a cup of tea, lifted his eyes off a catalogue. Immediately, his eyes turned into narrow half-moons, a smile of delight on his lips. 

“It fits perfectly!”

Soleum did a little spin before he could even process it. 

‘Huh.’

“Well, they did adjust it, so it makes sense. It feels nice.”

“I didn’t mean it like that… It is perfect!”

Braun stood up and came up for a close inspection. At any other time, Soleum would definitely tense or even take a step back to distance himself, but now, with the suit telling him to keep up the good performance, he didn’t even flinch. 

Braun’s hand touched the fabric of the sleeve, testing the feel of it, his gloved fingers momentarily sliding under the jacket to feel the thickness of it, his fingers brushing at Soleum’s skin. His hands went up, testing the sewing at the seams, sliding over the shoulder, finding their way to the collar. Standing right in front of Soleum, his arms went to lift the collar of the shirt, a ghostly glove-assistant handing him a tie. With precise familiar movements, he carefully wrapped it around Soleum’s neck. Even with the suit on, a shiver ran down his spine. Soleum once again reminded himself: it would barely take an ounce of effort from Braun to snap his neck with this very tie. To choke him, watching his life dim from his eyes as he could only barely scratch at the sleeves of his suit. 

“Now it’s complete,” Braun stood aside, admiring his work. “I think this colour suits you well.”

Soleum turned around to see himself in the mirror. The difference dawned on him like a revelation: the person in the mirror was almost a mocking reflection of what he could have been but never will. The stance, the confidence, the glimmer in his eyes. Somehow, the contrast between the two versions - real and mirrored - were only making him feel only so much more pathetic and exhausted. Seeing his perfect self only reminded him how far from it he felt at the moment.

‘Does Braun feel the same when he inevitably gets tired of his perfect performance?’  

Before he could even catch himself thinking about the Host in such light, Braun’s hand returned on his shoulder. Now, seeing both of them in the mirror, Soleum could see just how uncanny it all felt. 

Braun’s figure was taller, broader, fully covering his figure from the eyes of anyone who could have walked by. Their suits, though similar, made them look almost in contrast to each other: the timid, nude coloured figure of the Host made him look like an old time celebrity, welcoming, experienced, all-knowing. The brighter, smaller shape of Soleum, with the sharp lines of his mask, felt like a younger, experimental co-host. Together, they looked like a perfectly matched duo. 

Soleum swallowed. 

‘Absurd.’ 

Suddenly he felt so out of it, uncomfortable and heavy under the weight of the new fabrics and the prying eyes of the Host admiring them in the reflection.

“I think we should take it, no?” Braun whispered. 

Every fiber of his body resisted happily agreeing. The persona he was now inhabiting was eager to leave this boutique and stroll into the world alongside his companion, charismatic, outgoing. Different. 

‘Doesn’t matter how nice it might be, this is wrong.’

One should never trust darkness, no matter how good it makes you feel. In fact, in most cases, the better you felt the deeper into contamination you’d slip. Even with the mall being generally safe, this is not the risk Soleum was ready to take.

“I agreed to try it on, Braun, not to take it. Unlike you, I don’t have any special occasions that would call for something like this.”

Turning around, he stared right into Braun’s eyes, putting a lot of effort to not soften under the influence of the suit. He had to stand on his point.

“I see. I hope one day such an occasion will come, friend.” 

With that, Braun politely stepped out and closed the curtains behind himself. Never once breaking eye contact. 

The moment he was out, Soleum took a deep breath. Without looking in the mirror, to avoid any temptations, he took the suit off and handed it to the visibly upset glove-assistant.



His gloved hand could still feel the moment it brushed over the warm skin. Humans really are interesting, he thought. Braun has witnessed many get contaminated and driven by darkness. He has also seen a lot of them succumb to much darker corners through purely human desires. Greed, hunger, survival instinct could at times do better work at exposing the hidden human sides than a darkness could ever imagine.

Now seeing his Roe Deer Friend get ever so slightly touched by something so familiar to him brought a smile to his lips. 

[He looked good,] was all he had on his mind. 

And so, his list of guests for his “special occasion” now included a new name. 

[Do you ever imagine how wonderfully you’d look on stage, dear?]
